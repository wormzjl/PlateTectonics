## 1. Master table: tectonic setting → deposit types

### Takeaway
Most deposit types map cleanly onto the three phases of the supercontinent cycle: **convergence** (arcs → porphyry, epithermal, VMS, and late orogenic gold), **collision/intracratonic** (MVT, orogenic Au, Sn-W granites, unconformity U, diamonds), and **extension/breakup** (VMS, Ni-Cu-PGE, IOCG, clastic-dominated Pb-Zn, sediment-hosted Cu). Cratonic interiors host deposits driven by the mantle (layered-intrusion PGE/Cr, kimberlite diamonds). These do not follow the cycle. I could not access the full Kerrich et al. (2005) table. The table below is a synthesis from accessible primary and review sources plus flagged textbook knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- C&H13 Fig. 4 assigns deposit types to settings. **Convergence:** epithermal Au, porphyry Cu-Au, VMS, CD (clastic-dominated) Pb-Zn, orogenic gold. **Collisional/intracratonic:** MVT, diamonds, PGE layered intrusions, IOCG. **Breakup:** VMS, MVT, CD Pb-Zn. — [Cawood & Hawkesworth 2013, Geol. Soc. Spec. Publ. 393](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- GBL10 groups types by cycle phase. Orogenic Au, VMS, epithermal Au-Ag, and porphyry Cu±Au and Mo form at active margins during supercontinent assembly. MVT Pb-Zn and unconformity-type U form inland of active margins and go with supercontinent formation. Tertiary Carlin-type formed in deformed shelf sequences on the North American craton margin during the growth of Amasia. Diamond, Bushveld-type Ni-Cu-PGE, IOCG and clastic-dominated Pb-Zn go with breakup or attempted breakup in intracontinental failed rifts, and CD Pb-Zn also occurs where breakup succeeded. — [Goldfarb, Bradley & Leach 2010, Econ. Geol. 105:459–465](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70230185)
- VMS deposits form in oceanic lithosphere at mid-ocean ridges or in supra-subduction-zone settings. They enter the continental record by accretion during ocean closure and terrane accretion. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- VMS form in extensional settings, with modern examples at spreading centres and in oceanic-arc terranes. They split into Kuroko, Besshi and Cyprus types and tend to occur in clusters restricted to specific stratigraphic intervals. — [BGS07 Copper profile](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)
- Ophiolites preserve Neoproterozoic and younger oceanic crust on land. They carry VMS in their upper crustal levels and podiform chromite in their upper-mantle sections. Ophiolite ages peak at ~800–750 Ma, 500–440 Ma, 160–150 Ma and 100–90 Ma, all tied to ocean closures. The short lag between generation and preservation is linked to extension of trapped oceanic lithosphere early in collision (Cawood & Suhr 1992). — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Porphyry Cu systems are "hallmarks of magmatic arcs constructed above active subduction zones". A minority occupy **postcollisional** and other settings that develop after subduction stops (Richards 2009). Large high-grade hypogene porphyry and high-sulfidation Au deposits correlate empirically with **contractional** settings with high surface uplift and denudation rates. — [Sillitoe 2010, Econ. Geol. 105:3–41](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- Porphyry settings also include flat-slab subduction, arc reversal after collision, and subduction of oceanic plateaus or ridges (Cooke et al. 2005; Solomon 1990). — [Wikipedia: Porphyry copper deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit)
- MVT deposits are characteristic of collisional settings. Their hosts are platform carbonates that typically formed on passive margins. Fluids are driven by crustal thickening and deformation in collisional and accretionary orogens, and they migrate away from zones of thickening, toward the foreland. The type-area MVTs lie inboard of the Appalachian and Ouachita orogens. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- CD Pb-Zn (SEDEX) deposits occur in extensional settings: rifts, passive margins, back-arc basins and intracratonic rifts. — [C&H13 citing Leach et al. 2010](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- IOCG deposits occupy extensional settings within pre-existing cratons. They are tied to pulses of anorogenic alkaline/A-type magmatism near craton margins or lithospheric boundaries. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Layered-intrusion PGE, chromite and V-Ti magnetite deposits tend to sit toward the centres of Archean cratons. A thick SCLM (subcontinental lithospheric mantle) may be needed to support and preserve large volumes of dense mafic magma (Groves et al. 2005a). Diamonds concentrate in Precambrian cratons, where the base of thick SCLM favours diamond growth, and kimberlites/lamproites carry them up. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Sn-W deposits occur in granites in collisional and some accretionary orogens, nearly all Phanerozoic: Appalachian–Caledonian (Nova Scotia, Cornwall), Tethyan (Thailand), Terra Australis, Andean. These granites come from melting of over-thickened crust plus mantle melt input during orogenic extension. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Unconformity/collision-related U deposits are well developed in Proterozoic belts in Canada, Gabon and northern Australia, associated with the Nuna cycle. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Iron formations formed on basin platforms, including passive margins (Superior-type), and alongside greenstone-belt volcanism, often linked to VMS (Algoma-type). — [C&H13 citing Bekker et al. 2010](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Sediment-hosted stratiform Cu deposits are restricted to continental rift zones, to low palaeolatitudes where evaporites form, and to sediments younger than ~2.3 Ga (atmospheric oxidation). They are hosted in red-bed/grey-bed sequences. — [World-class sediment-hosted stratiform copper deposits review](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/7774175)
- Sediment-hosted Cu is associated with red-bed sandstones and evaporites. The Central African Copperbelt is 600 km long and 50 km wide. — [BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)
- Plate-margin deposits (orogenic gold, VMS, MVT) correlate with the supercontinent cycle. Intracratonic and mantle-driven deposits (e.g., Ni-Cu-PGE) show no clear association. — [C&H13 abstract](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Groves et al. (2022) frame mineral systems as Geodynamics–Fertility–Architecture–Preservation. They argue that many disparate deposit classes occupy equivalent tectonic niches in their Geodynamic and Preservation components. — [Groves et al. 2022, Geosystems and Geoenvironment](https://doaj.org/article/4686208e77284388af35259ae9bd0fc7)
- Citation for the table the user asked about: Kerrich, R., Goldfarb, R.J. & Richards, J.P. (2005), "Metallogenic Provinces in an Evolving Geodynamic Framework", Econ. Geol. 100th Anniversary Volume, 1097–1136. Full text not accessible in this session. — [UNLP catalogue](https://www.bfa.fcnym.unlp.edu.ar/id/50701)

## 2. Secular (time) distribution

### Takeaway
Deposit ages are strongly episodic. Plate-margin deposits peak at supercontinent assembly: ~2.7–2.5, ~1.9–1.8, ~0.6–0.45 and ~0.35–0.25 Ga. That pattern is partly real and partly **preservation bias**, because collisional assembly encases deposits inside continents. Redox-sensitive deposits are gated by the GOE: first O₂ rise ~2.43 Ga, permanent ~2.22 Ga. Witwatersrand-type U-Au paleoplacers end, then sediment-hosted Cu, CD Pb-Zn and unconformity U begin. The Neoproterozoic oxygenation makes MVT abundant. The 1.7–0.8 Ga "Boring Billion" lacks orogenic Au, VMS, BIF and glaciations but is rich in IOCG, CD Pb-Zn, anorthosite-Ti and anorogenic Sn granites.

### Cited Findings
**Supercontinent timing**
- Supercontinent intervals used by C&H13: Superia 2.8–2.6 Ga; Sclavia 2.55–2.40; Nuna 2.1–1.7; Rodinia 1.25–0.95; Gondwana 0.65–0.45; Pangea 0.35–0.15 Ga. Mean cycle duration is ~500 Myr, range 700–200 Myr. Crustal addition and removal rates are up to ~3–5 km³/yr. — [C&H13 Figs 1–2](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- GBL10 windows: Kenorland formation 2800–2500 Ma, breakup 2450–2100 Ma. Nuna/Columbia formation 2100–1800, breakup 1600–1300. Rodinia formation 1300–1100, breakup 850–600. Gondwana-Pangea formation 600–300, breakup 200–60. Amasia forming over the past 250 Myr. — [GBL10](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70230185)
- Bradley 2011 measures "supercontinentality" by the number of continents and the area of the largest one. Passive-margin and detrital-zircon abundances both track the size of the largest continent back to the Neoarchean. Carbonatites, granulites, eclogites and greenstone-belt deformation events also carry the cycle's imprint. — [Bradley 2011, Earth-Sci. Rev. (USGS record)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70093650)
- Passive margins show frequency peaks in the late Archean, late Paleoproterozoic and late Neoproterozoic–early Paleozoic, matching supercontinent aggregation (Bradley 2008). — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)

**Preservation bias versus real generation**
- Magma volumes are high during subduction but have low preservation potential. Collision makes less magma but preserves it well. Preserved age peaks therefore line up with collisional assembly even though that is not the main crust-generation phase. Subduction-phase zircon volumes are estimated at nearly an order of magnitude greater than collision-phase volumes, yet observed ages cluster at assembly. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Preservation is better at retreating convergent margins, which form extensional basins, than at advancing ones. Subduction erosion further removes frontal-arc material. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Groves et al. 2005 link gold's temporal distribution to Earth's declining heat production and the resulting changes in tectonics. Plumes were more common in a hotter Earth. Episodic juvenile crust growth came from short-lived (<100 Myr) plume events and supercontinent formation. Archean SCLM is buoyant and later SCLM progressively denser, so formation and preservation progressively decoupled. Orogenic gold formed over ≥3.4 Gyr and has the highest preservation potential of any gold deposit type. — [Groves et al. 2005, Econ. Geol. 100:203–224 (Macquarie record)](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/100th-anniversary-special-paper-secular-changes-in-global-tectoni/); [related 2005 chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/tectonic-and-lithospheric-controls-on-the-heterogeneous-temporal/1343390)
- Some 25% of gold resources are in Archean deposits, mostly 2.7–2.5 Ga, yet Archean crust is <6% of present continental crustal volume. So the episodicity is not only preservation bias: Archean crust was better endowed (De Wit & Thiart 2005). — [C&H13 citing Goldfarb et al. 2001](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Frimmel proposes a "Mesoarchean gold event": an overwhelming share of crustal gold may have been concentrated at ca. 2.9–2.7 Ga. — [Frimmel-related citation page](https://rjes.wdcb.ru/v20/2020ES000703/refsdir-703/Frim14.html)
- Circum-Pacific deposits have not yet passed through a supercontinent cycle, which explains the more continuous Phanerozoic distribution of orogenic gold and VMS. Atlantic-bordering Paleozoic belts have already been through one. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)

**Type-specific time patterns**
- VMS: peaks in the Neoarchean and late Paleoproterozoic, then more continuous through the Phanerozoic with early and mid-Paleozoic peaks (Gondwana and Pangea assembly). — [C&H13 citing Huston et al. 2010](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Iron formations peak at 2.7 and 1.9 Ga and are absent through the Rodinian cycle. "Rapitan-type" iron formation reappears in the Neoproterozoic (Cryogenian). — [C&H13 Fig. 5 citing Bekker et al. 2010](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Komatiite-associated Ni distribution reflects Earth's evolving heat flow. Iron formations and CD Pb-Zn partly reflect atmosphere/hydrosphere redox evolution (Barley & Groves 1992). — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- CD (sediment-hosted) Pb-Zn first appears at ~2.02 Ga and peaks at 1.85–1.58 Ga. Drivers were crustal sulfide oxidation supplying sulfate, the first significant sulfate evaporites, and red beds. MVT became abundant only after the second (late Neoproterozoic) oxidation of the atmosphere. — [Leach et al. 2010, "Sediment-hosted lead-zinc deposits in Earth history" (USGS)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70230192)
- The main CD Pb-Zn pulse is 1.7–1.4 Ga (Broken Hill, Mount Isa, Sullivan), coinciding with Nuna breakup. MVT reached maximum abundance during Pangea assembly in the Devonian–Carboniferous. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Sediment-hosted stratiform Cu is restricted to sediments younger than ~2.3 Ga. — [SSC review](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/7774175). The USGS descriptive model lists main ages as Middle Proterozoic and Permian–early Mesozoic. — [USGS Bulletin 1693 model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull27xx.htm)
- Witwatersrand–Elliot Lake detrital uraninite favours a (modified) placer origin. If so, atmospheric O₂ before ~2.4 Ga was far below 0.2 atm. Hydrothermal U ores and U-enriched black shales appear ca. 2.0 Ga. — [Holland 2002 GSA abstract](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2002AM/webprogram/Paper38192.html). The GOE ended formation of U-bearing conglomerates. — [Kesler 2005 GSA abstract](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2005AM/webprogram/Paper91256.html)
- GOE timing: O₂ first rose above 10⁻⁵ PAL at ~2.43 Ga, fluctuated across that threshold for ~200 Myr, and became permanent at ~2.22 Ga with the Lomagundi excursion. — [Poulton et al. 2021, Nature 592](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v592y2021i7853d10.1038_s41586-021-03393-7.html). A 2018 study finds MIF-S persisting beyond 2.31 Ga. — [Nature Communications 2018](https://doaj.org/article/93a9c072bdb94e9abd5bb84301b68084)
- Boring Billion / Rodinian cycle (1.7–0.8 Ga): few passive margins, no Sr anomaly, no glaciations, no iron formations. Massif anorthosites and Ti deposits are abundant. **Orogenic gold of 1.7–0.9 Ga is far less than 1% of known production, although that period generated almost 20% of preserved crust.** VMS is scarce, possibly because accretionary orogens were advancing rather than retreating. IOCG, CD Pb-Zn and anorogenic Sn granites are well developed. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- IOCG timing is not directly tied to the cycle, but anorogenic magmatism is concentrated in the late Paleoproterozoic–Mesoproterozoic. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Porphyry Cu systems formed since the Archean, but Meso-Cenozoic examples are the most abundantly preserved because young arcs are least eroded. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). Most large porphyries are <20 Myr old (exception: Cadia-Ridgeway, 438 Ma). Andean Cu-Mo formation clusters in the Paleocene–Eocene, Eocene–Oligocene and middle Miocene–Pliocene (Cooke et al. 2005). — [Wikipedia: Porphyry copper deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit)
- Each deposit type tends to be time-bound: absent in some periods, sparse in some, abundant in others. — [GBL10](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70230185)

### Inferences
- **Time gates for the simulation.** These come from the cited findings. Values marked [TK] are provisional.
  - Komatiite Ni: mostly Archean, with a weaker Paleoproterozoic tail. Gate by mantle potential temperature, which the sim's heat-flow proxy can supply.
  - Witwatersrand-type U-Au paleoplacers: only before the permanent GOE (~2.22–2.4 Ga). After that, detrital uraninite/pyrite cannot survive transport.
  - BIF: abundant before ~1.85 Ga, with peaks at ~2.7 and ~1.9 Ga. Absent ~1.8–0.8 Ga. Brief Cryogenian (Rapitan) return tied to snowball glaciations. Requires anoxic deep ocean plus Fe²⁺ supply (plume/VMS).
  - Sediment-hosted stratiform Cu, CD Pb-Zn, unconformity U, sulfate evaporites: only after the GOE (≥~2.2–2.0 Ga).
  - MVT: mostly after the NOE (~0.8–0.54 Ga), peaking during Pangea-style assembly.
  - Phosphorites [TK]: large ones mainly from the Ediacaran onward.
  - Bauxite/laterite [TK]: needs exposure and a humid climate. Preserved examples are mostly young.
  - Coal: after land plants (Devonian onward, others' scope).
- **Assembly-phase boost.** When the sim closes an ocean, deposits formed in the arcs and back-arcs of that ocean should get a preservation bonus. They are shielded inside the new supercontinent, which is not true for deposits on open margins.
- **Boring-Billion mode.** If the sim produces a long tenure with advancing (not retreating) margins, it should naturally under-produce VMS and over-produce intracontinental IOCG, CD Pb-Zn and anorthosite-Ti. That matches the record and needs no special-casing if the generators key off margin kinematics.

## 3. Preservation and exhumation: numbers a simulation needs

### Takeaway
Deposit survival is governed by **formation depth versus cumulative exhumation since formation**. Epithermal (~0.5 km), porphyry (~1.9 km) and orogenic gold (~10 km) have age modes of ~2–3, 11 and 199 Ma respectively. The age mode is the time for typical exhumation to bring each type to the surface, and continued erosion then destroys them. Kesler & Wilkinson's "tectonic diffusion" model estimates that ~62% of all Phanerozoic porphyries have already been eroded. Exhumation rates of 0.1–1 km/Myr in active belts, slower in arid climates, set these clocks.

### Cited Findings
- Epithermal Ag-Au, porphyry Cu and orogenic Au form at ~0.5, 1.9 and 10 km depth. Their age modes are ~3, 11 and 199 Ma, and modal age increases with emplacement depth. Few deposits are younger than the modal age, because exhumation takes time to expose them. Erosion beyond that time destroys them, so deposits get fewer with age. — [Wilkinson & Kesler 2007, J. Geol. 115(6) abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/toc/jg/2007/115/6). C&H13 quote the same depths with age modes of 2, 11 and 199 Ma, and conclude that epithermal and porphyry deposits older than Mesozoic are rare. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- KW08 "tectonic diffusion" model: most porphyries were emplaced at ~1–6 km depth with an average vertical thickness of ~2 km. About **125,895 porphyry Cu deposits formed in the Phanerozoic**. About **62% (78,106) have been removed** by uplift and erosion, and **38% (47,789) remain** in the crust, including 574 known deposits at the surface. They contain ~1.7×10¹¹ t Cu, more than 8,000 years of mine production. — [Wikipedia citing KW08, Geology 36:255–258](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit)
- The model needs only the number and ages of known deposits. It treats deposits as moving vertically through the crust (uplift/burial) as a random process. — [Search summary of KW08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit) (the method description is a secondary summary)
- A later application of the same approach to Mo: ~5,600 porphyry Mo and Mo-rich porphyry Cu deposits formed since 300 Ma, and ~70% of them (~1.1×10⁹ t Mo) remain. — [Natural Resources Research 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11053-022-10024-z)
- A 2025 global model: porphyry Cu deposits typically originate at 2–5 km depth. Predicted global mean emplacement depths are 1–3 km. Exhumation in moderately active mountain belts is typically 0.1–1.0 km/Myr. In hyper-arid settings such as the Central Andes it does not exceed 0.25 km/Myr. — [Earth Surface Dynamics 13:683 (2025)](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/13/683/2025/)
- Yanites & Kesler 2015 assume ~2 km emplacement in convergent settings. In wet, high-precipitation regions exposed porphyries are young and sparse, which indicates rapid exhumation. In dry regions they are older and more abundant. Climate therefore controls the age and abundance of exposed porphyries. — [Yanites & Kesler 2015, Nature Geoscience 8:462–465 (Indiana Univ. summary)](https://geomorphology.earth.indiana.edu/news-events/_news/2015-yanites-kesler.html)
- Porphyry anatomy (S10):
  - Parent composite plutons sit at 5–15 km paleodepth and feed vertically elongate (>3 km) stocks.
  - Mineralized intrusions extend >2 km vertically (Chuquicamata, Escondida, Grasberg), perhaps 4 km.
  - Most deposits form at <~4 km.
  - Shallow-level (<1 km paleodepth) epithermal and base-metal veins overlie them.
  - Lithocaps sit above the porphyries and are more extensive. Their roots can reach >1 km down in telescoped systems.
  - Diatremes are ~1 km across near the surface and ≥2 km deep.
  - Altered rock volumes are 10 to >100 km³.
  - "Erosion level is a cogent control" on which deposit types are preserved or expected at depth. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- Orogenic gold depth classes: epizonal <6 km, mesozonal 6–12 km, hypozonal >12 km. The overall range runs from near-surface to ~15–20 km. The crustal-continuum model spans a ~20–25 km vertical profile at <180 °C to >700 °C, from sub-greenschist to lower-granulite facies. — [RWTH thesis](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/957583/files/957583.pdf); [Mineralium Deposita 2022 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00126-022-01146-8). The 2022 review says the 6–12 km concentration is widely accepted. It notes that the continuum model is contested for hypozonal deposits and that slab-derived fluids may come from 25–50 km. — [same](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00126-022-01146-8)
- High-level deposits have poor long-term survival, especially in actively exhuming settings, whether or not they have passed through a supercontinent cycle (e.g., epithermal Au-Ag). — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Mesoproterozoic orogenic-gold scarcity has been attributed to deeper (high-grade) exposure levels of those belts (Goldfarb et al. 2001). C&H13 dispute this. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)
- Frimmel: the strong Cenozoic bias of porphyry and epithermal gold has long been explained by the low preservation potential of shallow crustal deposits. — [Frimmel abstract](https://information.americangeosciences.org/open-collections/igc/3075)
- Cratons with thick SCLM give a high preservation potential (Groves & Bierlein 2007). Fluids that form MVT migrate away from zones of thickening and exhumation, which helps preservation outside the main orogen. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)

### Inferences
**Parameter table for the simulation.** Sourced values are bold. Others are [TK] placeholders for the deposit-type researchers to confirm.

- **Survival rule.** For each deposit, record `z_form` (depth at formation) and `h` (vertical extent). Track cumulative exhumation `E(t)` (erosion minus burial) from the per-cell stratigraphic column.
  - **Buried:** `E < z_form`.
  - **Partially exposed and being mined by erosion:** `z_form ≤ E < z_form + h`. Release placers and supergene enrichment.
  - **Destroyed:** `E ≥ z_form + h`.
  - Burial by later sediments (negative E) protects a deposit. This reproduces the KW08 picture without special-case code.
- **Calibration targets.** Tune the sim's erosion coefficients so that synthetic exposed-deposit age histograms show modes near ~2–3 Ma (epithermal), ~11 Ma (porphyry) and ~200 Ma (orogenic Au) for active margins. Also check that roughly 60% of Phanerozoic porphyries are eroded by the present. Wet-climate arcs should show younger, sparser exposed porphyries than arid arcs (Yanites & Kesler).
- With 0.1–1 km/Myr exhumation, a 1.9 km porphyry is exposed after roughly 2–20 Myr and destroyed by ~4–40 Myr (1.9 km depth plus ~2 km extent). That is consistent with the 11 Ma mode. An orogenic Au deposit at 10 km needs ~10–100 Myr to be exposed at those rates, but post-orogenic exhumation slows dramatically, which is consistent with the ~200 Ma mode. (My arithmetic, not a published result.)
- Eroded deposits should not simply vanish. Their metal should go downstream into placers (Au, Sn, diamonds, PGE) in the sim's sediment-routing step. Downstream placer concentrations then become an indicator of eroded upstream lodes, which also makes for good gameplay.

## 4. The "mineral systems" approach as a generation algorithm

### Takeaway
A mineral system means all the geological factors that generate and preserve a deposit. In practice that is **source (fertility) → transient geodynamic trigger → pathway/architecture → trap (physical/chemical) → preservation**. Prospectivity is low if any one is missing, so it is a logical AND. This maps directly onto a procedural pipeline. The plate/lithosphere simulation supplies triggers and architecture, cell lithology and history supply fertility and traps, and the stratigraphic/exhumation record supplies preservation.

### Cited Findings
- Origin: Wyborn, Heinrich & Jaques (1994) adopted the petroleum-systems concept for minerals. A mineral system is "all geological factors that control the generation and preservation of mineral deposits": mobilisation from a source, transport and concentration, then preservation. Economic ore may be only hundreds of metres across, but the fluid-rock system extends tens to hundreds of km. — [Wyborn et al. 1994 record (Geoscience Australia)](https://ecat.ga.gov.au/geonetwork/srv/api/records/a05f7892-f8a0-7506-e044-00144fdd4fa6); definition as quoted in secondary sources
- WA Mineral Systems Atlas: four critical ingredients (SOURCE, PATHWAY, TRAP, PRESERVATION). Prospectivity is low if any one is absent. Trap = the physical and/or chemical mechanism(s) that deposit ore components. Preservation = processes allowing the deposit to survive to the present. The Atlas follows McCuaig et al. (2010). — [GSWA Mineral Systems Atlas](https://dmp.wa.gov.au/msa/mineral-systems-816.aspx)
- McCuaig, Beresford & Hronsky (2010) translate (1) critical processes → (2) constituent processes → (3) targeting elements reflected in geology → (4) mappable targeting criteria (direct or proxy). — [Macquarie record of McCuaig et al. 2010, Ore Geol. Rev.](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/translating-the-mineral-systems-approach-into-an-effective-explor/)
- McCuaig & Hronsky (2014, SEG Spec. Publ. 18) critical elements: **whole-lithosphere architecture, transient geodynamic triggers, fertility, preservation of the primary depositional zone**. The scale of a decision must match the scale of the geological process. — [McCuaig & Hronsky presentation](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mineral-systems-key-to-exploration-targeting/63074363)
- Hagemann et al. (2016, Ore Geol. Rev. 76:504–522) describe a mineral system as a chain of linked processes:
  - tectonic triggers that set time windows
  - sources of metals and fluids
  - pathways for fluid or magma
  - focusing of flow into traps
  - deposition at the traps
  - post-depositional features that allow detection
  - processes that enhance exhumation, preservation and upgrading
  
  Mineral systems are less well understood than petroleum systems. — [AMiner record of "Mineral system analysis: Quo vadis"](https://www.aminer.org/pub/56d922a2dabfae2eeea71468/mineral-system-analysis-quo-vadis)
- An IAEA presentation lists a related source–pathway–trap scheme with seven factors and attributes the concept to Knox-Robinson & Wyborn (1997). — [IAEA presentation](https://www-pub.iaea.org/iaeameetings/cn216pn/Wednesday/Session7/subhash.pdf)
- Groves et al. (2022): Geodynamics, Fertility, Architecture, Preservation integrate information across time and terrane scales. Disparate deposit classes share tectonic niches. — [Groves et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/4686208e77284388af35259ae9bd0fc7)
- The USGS "three-part" assessment is effectively a generative recipe:
  1. Delineate permissive tracts.
  2. Choose grade-tonnage models.
  3. Estimate the number of deposits consistent with those models.
  
  Monte Carlo combines them into metal distributions. — [USGS porphyry assessment (search summary)](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/quantitative-mineral-resource-assessment-copper-molybdenum-gold-and-silver); [Singer et al. 2005 deposit density](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197200)

### Inferences
**Generic algorithm (one pass per deposit "system type", run over the sim's event log):**
1. **Trigger (time window).** Iterate over geodynamic events from the plate sim: subduction segments per timestep with convergence rate, obliquity and slab age; collisions; rift initiation; plume heads/LIPs; basin inversion; ridge segments. Each system type declares which events trigger it and its time gates (Section 2: redox, mantle temperature, land plants).
2. **Fertility (source).** Score the source by checking:
   - **Arc systems:** slab age, subduction duration, crustal thickness, melt oxidation proxy.
   - **Orogenic Au:** presence of a hydrated metavolcanic/metasedimentary pile (greenstone, turbidite) undergoing prograde metamorphism.
   - **Basin brines:** the cell's stratigraphic column, e.g., red beds plus evaporites plus reduced shale for SSC.
   - **Mafic/ultramafic magmatism:** plume temperature, S-saturation potential, and crustal S sources for Ni-Cu-PGE.
3. **Architecture (pathway).** Use plate-boundary-parallel and transverse lithospheric faults, craton edges (lithospheric thickness gradient), rift-bounding faults, and unconformities. In practice, raise probability density within some km of these "structures" in the coarse grid.
4. **Trap (deposition site).** Use local lithology from the stratigraphic column: carbonate (skarn/MVT/CRD), reduced shale/grey beds (SSC, SEDEX), Fe-rich rocks (orogenic Au), seafloor at the time (VMS/BIF), and the unconformity surface (U). Also use the depth window (epithermal vs porphyry vs orogenic levels).
5. **Deposit instantiation.**
   - Draw the number of deposits from a density model (deposits per 100,000 km² of permissive tract, Section 5).
   - Cluster them along the belt (5–30 km clusters).
   - Draw size from a log-normal grade-tonnage model.
   - Record `z_form`, `h`, age, and the depth-zoned template.
6. **Preservation / modification.** Over remaining sim time, update depth from the stratigraphic column (burial or exhumation). Destroy, expose or bury deposits per the rule in Section 3. Add supergene enrichment where a deposit sits near the surface in a suitable climate, and placers downstream where it erodes. Metamorphic overprint changes mineralogy (e.g., VMS → metamorphosed massive sulfide).
7. **Combine as a fuzzy AND.** For example, take the product or minimum of the component scores. This matches the "any component absent → low prospectivity" rule.
- Steps 1–4 can run on the coarse grid at sim time. Step 5's block-level placement can run lazily at chunk generation, from a stored list of deposit objects (centre, footprint polygon, z_form, template id, grade/size seed).

### Gaps
- None of the primary mineral-systems papers' full texts were read. The wording comes from abstracts and secondary summaries.
- No published "mineral systems → procedural world generation" implementation was found. The algorithm above is my synthesis.

## 5. Relative abundance, economic importance, spacing, and size distributions

### Takeaway
A few deposit types dominate each commodity.
- **Cu:** porphyry ~60% of world copper (up to ~75% for whole porphyry systems), sediment-hosted ~20%.
- **Au:** orogenic ~30%, Witwatersrand ~30% (~1/3 of all gold ever mined), porphyry ~16%, epithermal ~12%.
- **Fe:** BIF-hosted ores supply the bulk of iron.
- **Pb-Zn:** sediment-hosted (SEDEX + MVT) ≈ half or more of resources.

Within each type, size is log-normal and extremely skewed: the top 10% of deposits hold 70–86% of the metal. Porphyry density is ~2–128 deposits per 100,000 km² of permissive exposed rock (median ~15). Deposits group into 5–30 km clusters along belts hundreds of km long.

### Cited Findings
**Copper**
- Porphyry deposits supply ~60% of world copper. Porphyry plus sediment-hosted together supply ~80%, and mines in these two types produce ~12 Mt Cu/yr. Sediment-hosted ≈ 20% (of identified resources, per USGS). — [USGS Fact Sheet 2014-3004](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2014/3004/pdf/fs2014-3004.pdf)
- Porphyries are the world's largest source (~60%) and resource (~65%) of copper. — [USGS SIR 2010-5070-B porphyry copper model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105070B)
- Porphyry deposits: 50–60% of world copper production. Sediment-hosted ≈ 20% of world production and the primary source of cobalt (Central African Copperbelt). VMS tonnages are 0.1–500 Mt. Sediment-hosted Cu averages 22 Mt @ 2.1% Cu and 23 g/t Ag (Mosier et al. 1986). Crustal Cu is ~50 ppm and the minimum exploitable grade ~0.4%, a concentration factor of ~80. — [BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)
- **Conflict:** BGS07 says porphyries supply 99% of world Mo, while S10 says porphyry Cu systems supply "half the Mo". These probably differ in whether climax-type porphyry Mo deposits are counted as "porphyry". S10 also says porphyry Cu *systems*, which include skarns and epithermal deposits, supply nearly **three-quarters of world Cu**, perhaps one-fifth of Au, and most Re. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf); [BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)
- Largest deposits: Cu 203 Mt (Los Bronces–Río Blanco); Mo 2.5 Mt (El Teniente); Au 129 Moz (Grasberg, second largest). Porphyry sizes range from <10 Mt to 10 Gt, and skarns and epithermal orebodies from <1 Mt to >1 Gt. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)

**Gold**
- Of all gold mined plus known reserves and resources: ~30% orogenic, ~30% Witwatersrand-type conglomerate, 16% porphyry (Cu-)Au, 12% epithermal, and ≤12% all others (Carlin-type the most notable). — [Frimmel "Crustal Gold Cycle" abstract (attribution to this link is likely but not certain)](https://information.americangeosciences.org/open-collections/igc/3075)
- Witwatersrand cumulative output is ~53,000 t, about one-third of all gold ever mined. Remaining resources are close to 30% of global known resources. — [Frimmel 2019 review](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/The%20Witwatersrand%20Basin%20and%20Its%20Gold%20Deposits.pdf). Popular figures of ~40% or more are looser. — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/40-percent-of-the-worlds-gold-is-3-billion-years-old)
- Orogenic gold of 1.7–0.9 Ga is <1% of production. Archean deposits hold ~25% of gold resources. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)

**Lead-zinc**
- Sediment-hosted Pb-Zn deposits hold the world's largest Pb and Zn resources and dominate production. Nine of the ten largest are SEDEX. Among deposits ≥2.5 Mt there are 35 SEDEX (excluding Broken Hill-type) and 15 MVT (excluding Irish-type). Zn grades are similar, while Pb and Ag grades are ~25% higher in SEDEX. — [Leach et al. 2005, "Sediment-hosted lead-zinc deposits: A global perspective" (USGS)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70146125)
- Sediment-hosted deposits hold 490.6 Mt of Pb + Zn + Cu, equivalent to VMS, skarn, porphyry, epithermal and mixed types combined. That makes them about half of identified Pb-Zn resources. — [Mudd et al. 2017, "The world's lead-zinc mineral resources"](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/geo_fac_articles/208)
- SEDEX median: 15 Mt @ 5.6% Zn, 2.8% Pb, 30 g/t Ag (Briskey 1986). — [BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)

**Iron**
- BIF-hosted high-grade (>60 wt% Fe) hematite ores make up the bulk of world iron-ore production and reserves. — [Univ. of Johannesburg publication record](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/palaeoproterozoic-banded-iron-formationhosted-high-grade-hematite/). Secondary sources quote ">60% of global iron reserves". A ~90% production figure seen online is unverified. — [EarthHow (secondary)](https://earthhow.com/banded-iron-formation-bif)

**Crustal abundance versus ore grade**
- Concentration factors (minimum economic grade ÷ crustal abundance): Al ≈ 4, Fe ≈ 4, Cu ≈ 80, Zn ≈ 570, Pb ≈ 2,900, Cr ≈ 2,940, Sn ≈ ~3,000. — [LibreTexts (Perkins et al.) table](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geology/Mineralogy_(Perkins_et_al.)/09%3A_Ore_Deposits_and_Economic_Minerals/9.01%3A_Mineral_Commodities/9.1.04%3A_Natural_Abundances_of_Elements). An alternative table gives Cu ≈ 250, Zn ≈ 1,000, Pb ≈ 5,000 and Au ≈ 2,000 (6 ppm ore / ~0.003–0.004 ppm crust). — [LibreTexts (Earle)](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geology/Environmental_Geology_(Earle)/08:_Geological_Resources/8.01:_Metal_Deposits)

**Size distribution (grade-tonnage)**
- Tonnage and grade frequency distributions are approximately log-normal (Singer). — [USGS PP 907-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp907A)
- "World-class" deposits, the top 10% by contained metal, hold >86% of all gold, 79% of Ag, 84% of Cu, 71% of Zn and 73% of Pb. Thresholds: ≥100 t Au, 2,400 t Ag, 2 Mt Cu, 1.7 Mt Zn, 1 Mt Pb. Tonnage predicts contained metal better than grade. More than 68% of the 193,000 t of gold discovered is in four deposit types, and >62% in four countries. — [Singer 1995, Econ. Geol. (USGS record)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197194)
- Global porphyry Cu median: 250 Mt @ 0.39% Cu (2017 update), previously 240 Mt @ 0.44% (2008). — [Hammarstrom & Robinson 2017 SEG abstract](https://www.segweb.org/SEG/Events/Conference-Archive/2017/Conference-Proceedings/files/topics/..%5Cpdf%5CPoster-Presentations%5CAbstracts%5CP089-Hammarstrom.pdf)

**Spacing and density**
- Porphyry Cu deposit density is 2–128 deposits per 100,000 km² of exposed permissive rock, skewed high. 90% of control areas have ≥4, 50% have ≥15, and 10% have ≥35 per 100,000 km². Zones closer than 2 km were merged into one deposit. — [Singer et al. 2005, "Porphyry copper deposit density" (USGS)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197200)
- Example tract: an Intermontane island-arc tract has ~11 deposits per 100,000 km² (known plus estimated undiscovered). — [same search; USGS Canada/Alaska assessment](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5090/c)
- Porphyry systems form linear belts many hundreds of km long, less commonly isolated systems. **District-scale clusters or alignments reach 5–30 km** across or in length. Alignments run parallel or transverse to the arc. A cluster's formation ages can spread up to 5 Myr (Chuquicamata, Yanacocha) or ~18 Myr (Cadia). Individual systems last ~0.1 to several Myr, while clusters and belts stay active ≥10 Myr. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- In the Central Andes, many smaller deposits cluster around a coeval giant porphyry (Farrar et al. 2023). — [Wikipedia: Porphyry copper deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit)
- VMS tend to occur in clusters within specific stratigraphic intervals. — [BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Game abundance weighting.** The real world's metal is dominated by a few types and a few giant deposits. If the game wants recognisable geology, porphyry Cu should be the default copper source in arcs, sediment-hosted Cu a strong second in rifts, and VMS a frequent but small source. Gold should come from orogenic belts in old cratons and accreted terranes, and from Witwatersrand-type paleoplacers only if the world has a pre-GOE foreland basin.
- **Size draws.** Use log-normal tonnage per type with the medians above. Combined with the Singer 10%/80% concentration, a world with ~20 porphyries in a belt should have 1–2 "giants" holding most of the copper. This gives a natural "find the big one" exploration loop.
- **Density.** In-game Minecraft scale can be compressed. The *relative* spacing should be kept: clusters (5–30 km) inside belts (hundreds of km) inside provinces. With 1 block = 1 m, a real 5–30 km cluster spacing would be too sparse for play, so a scale factor will be needed. That is a design choice, not a research finding.
- **Crustal abundance does not predict deposit frequency.** Fe and Al need only ~4× enrichment, so they are "everywhere" (BIF, laterite, bauxite). Cu needs ~80–250×, and Pb, Cr, Sn and Au need ~2,000–5,000×, so they need specific mineral systems. The game can use concentration factor to decide which commodities appear as bulk rock (layers) versus rare focused deposits.

### Gaps
- No reliable share of world Cu was found for IOCG, VMS or magmatic Ni-Cu, and none of Zn for VMS versus SEDEX versus MVT specifically. The USGS OFR 2009-1252 sediment-hosted Zn-Pb grade-tonnage models were not opened.
- Log-normal σ (standard deviation of log tonnage) for each type was not retrieved. Cox & Singer 1986 (USGS Bulletin 1693) has them.
- Typical spacing for orogenic gold deposits along shear zones, or VMS within districts, was not found.

## 6. Existing procedural and game approaches to geology-driven ore placement

### Takeaway
Games use **host-rock class + depth window + shape template + rarity/density + surface indicator**: TerraFirmaCraft, Dwarf Fortress, Geolosys and Vintage Story all follow this pattern. None uses tectonic history or time. Academic work uses **plate-kinematic predictors** (convergence rate, obliquity, slab age) and **three-part permissive-tract/density/grade-tonnage assessments**. Both can be adapted directly to a sim-driven mod. The main lessons are nested deposits (child ores), surface indicators and prospecting gradients, and that rock-type gating alone gives believable local results but not believable regional patterns.

### Cited Findings
**TerraFirmaCraft (1.20.x)**
- Ores are "rare veins that require some prospecting". Ore blocks are graded Poor, Normal or Rich, and richer veins are more lucrative.
- Each ore is restricted to rock categories (igneous extrusive, igneous intrusive, metamorphic, sedimentary) plus elevation limits. Examples: native copper in extrusives above y=40; native gold below y=70, larger and richer deeper; garnierite below y=0 in intrusives; cassiterite above y=80 in uplift regions or dikes; ruby more common in "Collisional Mountains"; native silver in granite/diorite in uplift regions.
- Sedimentary minerals and coals form very large flat deposits near the surface. Kimberlite (gabbro only) and emerald form thin vertical bodies up to ~100 blocks tall. Rivers can be panned for Cu, Au, Ag, cassiterite, amethyst and opal. — [TFC Field Guide: Ores and Minerals](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/ores_and_minerals.html)
- TFC vein feature classes are Cluster, Disc, Pipe and KaolinDisc veins, plus an Indicator class. — [TFC source tree](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/tree/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/feature/vein)
- VeinConfig fields:
  - `blocks`: map from host rock block to weighted ore states
  - `indicator`
  - `rarity`
  - `density` (0–1)
  - `min_y` / `max_y`
  - `project` / `project_offset`
  - `random_name` (seed)
  - `biomes`
  - `near_lava`

- EarthByte spatio-temporal machine learning on plate reconstructions: high orthogonal convergence speed is the most important feature for porphyry Cu emplacement in the Americas. — [EarthByte](https://www.earthbyte.org/predicting-the-emplacement-of-cordilleran-porphyry-copper-systems-using-a-spatio-temporal-machine-learning-model/)

### Inferences
- **What to borrow:**
  - TFC's host-rock → ore-state map and grade tiers. They make ores TFC-compatible.
  - DF's nested inclusions (child ores in a parent body).
  - Vintage Story's two-scale prospecting (regional ‰ density versus local node search) plus child deposits.
  - Geolosys's shape vocabulary (pluton, dike, layer, sparse, top-layer) and surface samples.
- **What to add:**
  - A regional "province" layer driven by the tectonic simulation. Use the EarthByte-style kinematic predictors for arc porphyries.
  - Time and preservation logic (Sections 2–3), which no game currently has.
- **TFC compatibility:** generate TFC-style `VeinConfig`-like objects at world creation. Fill the `blocks` maps from the sim's rock at that location, and set `min_y`/`max_y` from `z_form − E` (current depth) scaled to world height. Indicators then become a natural output of "partially exposed" deposits.

### Gaps
- The TFC 1.20 vein *shape* algorithms (cluster/disc/pipe internals), Geolosys current-version code, and the Vintage Story deposit JSON schema (e.g., disc generator parameters, ore maps) were not read in detail.
- "Unearthed" was not researched.
- No academic paper was found that procedurally simulates ore deposits from a full synthetic plate history for games. The closest analogues are the EarthByte ML studies on real reconstructions and KW08's diffusion model.

## 7. Recommendations: a small set of "deposit generators" with shared machinery

### Takeaway
About eight parametrised generators can cover nearly all major deposit types if they share one mineral-systems pipeline (trigger → fertility → architecture → trap → instantiate → preserve) and one depth-zoned template system. Each generator should have time gates (redox, mantle temperature, biosphere), a clustering model, a log-normal size draw, and an explicit `z_form`/extent tracked against the per-cell exhumation history.

### Cited Findings
- Different settings and supercontinent phases have characteristic deposit suites, and preservation differs by setting and depth. — [C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf); [GBL10](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70230185)
- A single porphyry system can host, by depth and distance:
  - porphyry Cu-Mo-Au at the centre
  - skarn, carbonate-replacement and distal sediment-hosted Au in carbonate rocks
  - subepithermal veins
  - high- and intermediate-sulfidation epithermal deposits in and around the lithocap above
  
  Few systems contain the full spectrum. — [S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- Disparate deposit classes occupy equivalent geodynamic and preservation niches, which supports shared machinery. — [Groves et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/4686208e77284388af35259ae9bd0fc7)
- The four components (source, pathway, trap, preservation) are required jointly. — [GSWA Atlas](https://dmp.wa.gov.au/msa/mineral-systems-816.aspx)

**Shared machinery (implement once):**
1. **Event log** from the plate sim, per timestep and per segment: subduction (rate, obliquity, slab age, overriding-plate stress), collision, rift, ridge, plume/LIP, basin subsidence, uplift.
2. **Per-cell column** with lithology, age, burial depth through time, peak metamorphic grade, and cumulative exhumation `E(t)`.
3. **Mineral-system scorer:** fuzzy AND of fertility × trigger × architecture × trap, with per-generator weights and global time gates (redox curve with GOE ~2.43–2.22 Ga and NOE; mantle temperature; land plants).
4. **Instantiation:**
   - Deposit count from a density per area of permissive tract (e.g., porphyry median ~15 per 100,000 km², range 2–128).
   - Hierarchical clustering: province → belt (hundreds of km) → cluster (5–30 km) → deposit.
   - Log-normal size and grade draws by type (porphyry median 250 Mt @ 0.39% Cu; SEDEX 15 Mt @ 5.6% Zn; SSC 22 Mt @ 2.1% Cu). Top ~10% of draws should carry ~70–85% of the metal.
5. **Depth-zoned templates:** each generator defines a vertical profile of ore and alteration zones relative to `z_form`. Template plus present-day depth gives what is exposed at the surface (indicator), what is at depth, and what has been eroded (sent to placers).
6. **Preservation pass:** the survival rule from Section 3, plus an assembly bonus for deposits enclosed by a later collision, and destruction by subduction erosion at advancing margins.
7. **Lazy block placement** at chunk generation, from stored deposit records, mapped to TFC-style host-rock-aware ore states and grade tiers (poor/normal/rich from distance to the template core).

- **Validation:** after world creation, compute synthetic statistics and compare them with the real-world targets:
  - share of Cu by type (~60% porphyry, ~20% sediment-hosted)
  - share of Au by type (~30/30/16/12)
  - age modes of exposed deposits (~2–3, 11, 199 Ma for epithermal, porphyry and orogenic)
  - fraction of eroded porphyries (~62%)
  - scarcity of Boring-Billion orogenic gold and VMS
  
  Exact match is not needed, but large deviations reveal broken generators.

### Gaps
- The generator split is a design proposal. No source proposes this exact taxonomy.
- Several parameters are [TK]: G3 basin depth, G5 pipe depth, G6 depths, laterite/bauxite climate thresholds. They should come from the deposit-type researchers' notes.
