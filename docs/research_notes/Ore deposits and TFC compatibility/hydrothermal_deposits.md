# Magmatic-hydrothermal, volcanogenic and metamorphic-hydrothermal ore deposits: a catalog for simulation rules

Scope notes for the report writer:
- One "##" section per deposit family. Each section has Takeaway, Cited Findings, Inferences, and Gaps. Every proposed simulation rule and every Minecraft/TFC block mapping is in the Inferences subsections, because these are design reasoning and not sourced facts.
- "Depth" means paleodepth below the paleosurface at the time of formation, unless the text says otherwise.
- The main source spine is the USGS Bulletin 1693 descriptive models (Cox & Singer 1986, HTML at pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693), Sillitoe (2010, Econ. Geol. 105:3-41), the USGS Mineral Deposit Models for Resource Assessment (SIR 2010-5070 chapters), Galley, Hannington & Jonasson (2007, GSC VMS synthesis), Muntean et al. (2011, Nature Geoscience) for Carlin, and the Goldfarb/Groves papers for orogenic gold. Sources I could not open in full are marked "(abstract/secondary)".
- The Minecraft vertical window is about 384 blocks (y = -64 to 320). At 1 block = 1 m, a player sees only the top ~0.3-0.4 km of crust. The porphyry, VMS and orogenic-gold sections show that deposit zoning spans 1-20 km. So the main design decision is which slice of a deposit's vertical model falls inside the window after simulated exhumation. Section 1 covers this.

## 1. Cross-cutting: formation depth, exhumation and preservation (the erosion-depth logic)

### Takeaway
Whether a hydrothermal deposit is preserved and exposed depends mainly on its formation depth compared with the erosion since it formed. Shallow types (epithermal, hot-spring Hg, sinter, porphyry lithocaps) are mostly Cenozoic and are eroded quickly in wet, fast-uplifting arcs. Deep types (orogenic gold at 2-20 km, Sn-W greisens, pluton-related skarn roots) survive for billions of years. VMS deposits form on the seafloor, so they survive only if they are buried and then accreted or obducted onto continents rather than subducted. The simulation should track, for every deposit instance, its formation depth and the cell's net exhumation since formation.

### Cited Findings
- Porphyry Cu systems formed worldwide since the Archean, but Mesozoic-Cenozoic examples are the most abundantly preserved, "probably because younger arc terranes are normally the least eroded" (Sillitoe citing Seedorff et al. 2005; Kesler & Wilkinson 2006; Wilkinson & Kesler 2009). Erosion level is a "cogent control" on which deposit types in a porphyry system are preserved. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- Kesler & Wilkinson (2006) is "The Role of Exhumation in the Temporal Distribution of Ore Deposits" (Economic Geology 101(5):919-922). Later authors cite it for the point that an economic deposit needs both a genetic process and later exhumation that brings it within mining reach. — [Frontiers 2025 paper citing it](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2025.1573046/pdf) (secondary)
- Kesler & Wilkinson (2008, Geology 36:255-258) used a "tectonic diffusion" model of how porphyry Cu deposits move vertically through the crust. They estimate that ~125,895 porphyry Cu deposits formed during the Phanerozoic, that ~47,789 remain at various crustal depths, and that these hold ~1.7 × 10^11 t Cu. — [WorldWideScience abstract index](https://worldwidescience.org/topicpages/c/copper+deposit+coastal.html); [Wikipedia: Porphyry copper deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit) (abstract/secondary)
- Yanites & Kesler (2015, Nature Geoscience) treated porphyry Cu deposits as depth markers emplaced at ~2 km. They found that deposits are young and sparse in high-precipitation regions, and older and more abundant in dry regions such as the Atacama and the American Southwest. From 314 deposits they derived exhumation rates of 0.03-1.4 km/Myr, assuming a uniform 2 km emplacement depth. — [Indiana Univ. summary](https://geomorphology.earth.indiana.edu/news-events/_news/2015-yanites-kesler.html); [U. Michigan news](https://news.umich.edu/a-climate-signal-in-the-global-distribution-of-copper-deposits); critique in [Boggiani et al. 2025, ESurf](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/13/683/2025/)
- Boggiani et al. (2025, Earth Surface Dynamics) ran a global landscape-evolution model (goSPL). They argue that porphyry Cu deposits typically originate at 2-5 km depth, not a single 2 km. They say preservation is controlled mainly by climate-driven erosion plus tectonic uplift, and that regions like Papua New Guinea can exceed 10 km/Myr erosion. — [Boggiani et al. 2025](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/13/683/2025/) (secondary summary of article)
- Hypogene porphyry mineralization is typically associated with crustal thickening and rapid exhumation, whereas supergene enrichment requires slow exhumation. — [Dahlström et al. (NERC repository)](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/532814/1/4912_dahlstrom_et_al.pdf)
- Orogenic gold deposits, formed over at least 3.4 b.y., have "the highest preservation potential of any gold deposit type" (Groves et al. 2005, Econ. Geol. 100:203-224). — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/100th-anniversary-special-paper-secular-changes-in-global-tectoni/) (abstract)
- VMS deposits on the modern seafloor form at mid-ocean ridges and in arcs. The deposits preserved in the geologic record formed mainly in oceanic and continental nascent-arc, rifted-arc and back-arc settings. — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf)
- Epithermal veins in the USGS models are "mainly Tertiary" (Comstock most 40-3.7 Ma; Creede most 29-4 Ma; Sado most 38-5 Ma). Hot-spring Au-Ag is "mainly Tertiary and Quaternary". Hot-spring Hg is listed as Tertiary. — [USGS Comstock 25c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5nqr.htm); [USGS Creede 25b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7a0j.htm); [USGS Sado 25d](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7khf.htm); [USGS hot-spring Au-Ag 25a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0g4n.htm); [USGS hot-spring Hg 27a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4von.htm)

### Inferences
- **Core data structure (proposed):** each simulated deposit instance stores `type`, `t_form`, `z_top_form` and `z_bot_form` (depth range of its ore zone below the paleosurface), `footprint` (radius or length × width), `grade_class`, and a list of vertically stacked sub-zones (for example lithocap / porphyry shell / pluton root). At world creation, compute `E = net exhumation of the host cell since t_form` (sum of erosion minus later sedimentary or volcanic burial, from the per-cell stratigraphic column). Then:
  - If `E > z_bot_form`: the ore zone is eroded. Emit detrital products downstream only for resistant minerals: native gold, cassiterite, wolframite, and gems (see placer researcher). Emit nothing for sulfides or Hg.
  - If `z_top_form - E` is within the visible window (0 to ~350 m below the present surface): place the ore zone, offset to the correct depth.
  - If the ore zone is deeper than the window: place only distal or overlying signatures that do fall in the window (alteration halos, pyrite halo, lithocap, sinter). Optionally add a sparse "deep ore" teaser near y = -64.
  - If the deposit was buried (E < 0): its top is now at `z_top_form + burial`, usually below the window unless exhumed again later.
- **Survival half-life heuristic:** a deposit formed at depth d in a cell with long-term exhumation rate e survives for roughly t ≈ (d + vertical extent) / e. With the Yanites & Kesler rates (0.03-1.4 km/Myr) and a porphyry ore shell at ~1-4 km, survival times range from ~3 Myr in wet, fast-uplift arcs to more than 100 Myr in arid or stable settings. This reproduces the observed bias toward young porphyries. Use cell precipitation (from the climate model) and uplift rate to set e.
- **Vertical compression:** 4 km of porphyry zoning (Section 2) cannot fit in 384 blocks. Recommended approach: use the simulation's E to pick the slice, and optionally compress each deposit's vertical model (for example 5-10×) so a player can find one or two adjacent zones (such as a supergene blanket over hypogene ore, or a lithocap passing down into phyllic alteration) inside the window.
- **Coarse grid vs. sub-cell deposits:** most hydrothermal orebodies are 0.1-5 km across, far smaller than any plausible tectonic-sim cell. The simulation should output per-cell endowment probabilities and cluster centroids. Chunk generation then places individual bodies inside the cell deterministically from the world seed, using the cluster rules in each section.

### Gaps
- I could not read Kesler & Wilkinson (2006, 2008) or Wilkinson & Kesler (2009) in full. Their exact diffusion parameters (mean emplacement depth, erosion/burial variance) are not verified here. They would let the "tectonic diffusion" model be implemented directly.
- I found no single compiled table of formation depth versus deposit type across all hydrothermal classes. The per-type depths below are assembled from separate sources.

## 2. Porphyry Cu-Mo-Au systems (including lithocaps and supergene enrichment)

### Takeaway
Porphyry Cu systems are the world's dominant copper source (~60-75% of Cu). They form above oxidized, hydrous arc magma chambers at 5-15 km. Their ore shells sit within the upper ~4 km of crust, and their lithocaps and high-sulfidation epithermal tops sit in the upper ~1 km. They occur in arc-parallel belts tied to 10-20 Myr magmatic epochs, are favored by contraction, crustal thickening and rapid uplift, and form clusters 5-30 km across. Exposure requires roughly 1-4 km of exhumation. Supergene enrichment requires slow exhumation, a falling water table and a seasonally dry climate.

### Cited Findings
- **Commodities and importance:** porphyry Cu systems supply nearly three-quarters of the world's Cu, half the Mo, perhaps one-fifth of the Au, most of the Re, and minor Ag, Pd, Te, Se, Bi, Zn, Pb. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). The USGS says ~60% of copper production and ~65% of resources. — [USGS SIR 2010-5070-B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105070B)
- **Sizes and grades:** porphyry Cu ± Mo ± Au deposits range from <10 Mt to 10 Gt, and skarns from <1 Mt to >1 Gt. Typical hypogene grades are 0.5-1.5% Cu, <0.01-0.04% Mo and 0.0x-1.5 g/t Au. Large high-sulfidation epithermal deposits average 1-3 g/t Au. Record holders are Los Bronces-Rio Blanco (203 Mt Cu), El Teniente (2.5 Mt Mo) and Grasberg with its contiguous skarn (129 Moz Au). — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Ore minerals:** chalcopyrite + pyrite ± molybdenite, and chalcopyrite + magnetite ± bornite ± native Cu. Late veins carry enargite, tetrahedrite, galena, sphalerite and barite. — [USGS model 17](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9ik5.htm). Cu-Au subtype: chalcopyrite ± bornite with traces of native gold, electrum and tellurides; magnetite is abundant in the potassic core; Au(ppm)/Mo(%) > 30. — [USGS model 20c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5m0l.htm). Cu-Mo subtype: Au/Mo < 3. — [USGS model 21a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5mlr.htm)
- **System scale:** systems are 10 to >100 km³ of altered rock, centered on stocks or dike swarms above composite parental plutons at 5-15 km paleodepth. Stocks are vertically elongate (>3 km) and commonly ~1 km in diameter. Mineralized intrusions extend >2 km vertically (Chuquicamata, Escondida, Grasberg) and perhaps 4 km. Systems "typically span the upper 4 km or so of the crust". Most deposits form at <~4 km, but potassic ore at Butte formed as deep as ~9 km. System radii reach ~8 km and areas approach 100 km². — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Emplacement depth (USGS):** porphyry Cu-Au stocks show "evidence for volcanic center; 1-2 km depth of emplacement". — [USGS model 20c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5m0l.htm)
- **Alteration zoning (vertical):** from deep to shallow, barren early sodic-calcic, then potassic (ore), then chlorite-sericite, then sericitic (phyllic), then advanced argillic (lithocap). Lithocaps may reach >1 km thick if not eroded, with original areas of several to >10 km² and locally up to 100 km². Their pyrite ± enargite ± covellite assemblages contrast with chalcopyrite ± bornite in the potassic zone. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). Lateral zoning outward: sodic-calcic, potassic, phyllic, argillic, propylitic. — [USGS model 17](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9ik5.htm). Lithocaps can be >10 km long. — [Cooke "Mines and Wines" presentation citing Sillitoe 1995](https://smedg.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Mines-and-Wines-v3-DR_Cooke.pdf)
- **Fluids and temperature:** alteration evolves through cooling from >700 °C to <250 °C as the underlying pluton solidifies. Hypersaline liquid plus vapor causes potassic alteration. Below ~350 °C a single-phase, low- to moderate-salinity liquid causes sericitic alteration and peripheral mineralization. Meteoric water is not required. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Magma type:** porphyry intrusions are exclusively I-type and magnetite-series, typically metaluminous medium-K calc-alkaline, sometimes high-K or alkaline. Mo-rich porphyries are tied to the more felsic intrusions. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Tectonic setting:** mainly magmatic arcs (including back-arcs) above active subduction. A minority are postcollisional. Stress regimes range from moderately extensional through oblique-slip to contractional. Strongly extensional bimodal basalt-rhyolite settings lack significant porphyry Cu. There is a prominent empirical link between contractional settings (crustal thickening, surface uplift, rapid exhumation) and large high-grade deposits: the Laramide of SW North America, the Eocene-Oligocene and Miocene-Pliocene central Andes, mid-Miocene Iran, and Pliocene New Guinea and Philippines. Changes in stress regime are considered especially favorable. Large-volume ignimbrites (calderas) "seriously downgrade" porphyry potential. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). USGS model: "Rift zones contemporaneous with Andean or island-arc volcanism along convergent plate boundaries. Uplift and erosion to expose subvolcanic rocks." — [USGS model 17](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9ik5.htm)
- **Timing:** individual systems last ~100,000 years to several Myr. Clusters, alignments and belts stay active for 10 Myr or longer. Porphyry belts form during metallogenic epochs of typically 10-20 Myr. Precursor plutons predate porphyry stocks by 1-2 Myr or less. Formation ages within clusters spread up to 5 Myr (Chuquicamata, Yanacocha) or ~18 Myr (Cadia). Porphyry stocks can be assembled in as little as 80,000 years (Batu Hijau). — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Spatial pattern:** belts are linear and orogen-parallel, from tens to thousands of km long. Each belt corresponds to a magmatic arc of similar size. Belts of different epochs are usually spatially separate because arcs migrate as slabs steepen or shallow. Deposit densities commonly reach 15 per 100,000 km² of exposed permissive terrane (Singer et al. 2005). Clusters (equidimensional) and alignments (arc-parallel along intra-arc faults, or arc-transverse along lineaments) are 5-30 km across or long. Deposits within them are 100s-1,000s m apart. Recent major discoveries lie within <1-3 km of known deposits. Major deposits can also be isolated (Pebble, Butte, Bingham). — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Associated deposits in carbonate wall rocks:** proximal Cu-Au skarn, distal Zn-Pb and/or Au skarn, and then, beyond the skarn front, carbonate-replacement Cu and/or Zn-Pb-Ag ± Au and sediment-hosted (distal-disseminated) Au. High-sulfidation epithermal deposits form in lithocaps, with disseminated Au ± Ag "within the uppermost 500 m or so". Intermediate-sulfidation veins form on lithocap peripheries. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Telescoping:** rapid erosion during mineralization (high uplift, pluvial or glacial conditions) or volcano sector collapse causes shallow alteration to overprint deeper zones. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Weathering and surface indicators:** green and blue Cu carbonates and silicates in outcrop. Where leaching is intense, barren leached outcrops remain, with fractures coated by hematitic limonite with a bright red streak. Secondary chalcocite forms at the present or paleo water table. — [USGS model 17](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9ik5.htm). Cu-Mo subtype: "Intense leaching of surface; wide areas of iron oxide stain"; chalcocite blanket below the leached zone. — [USGS model 21a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5mlr.htm). Cu-Au subtype: iron staining may be weak if pyrite is low; Au enriched in residual soil. — [USGS model 20c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5m0l.htm)
- **Supergene controls (Sillitoe 2005):** the best profiles need vertically extensive orebodies with steep fracture arrays, high pyrite/Cu-sulfide ratios, and non-reactive (sericitic or advanced argillic) host alteration. Central Andean enrichment spanned several contractional uplift-exhumation pulses that drove water-table descent. Enrichment in northern Chile "virtually ceased at ca. 14 Ma" with the onset of hyperaridity. — [Sillitoe 2005 GSA abstract](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2005AM/webprogram/Paper91736.html). The youngest supergene alunite ages cluster around 14 Ma. — [Reich et al. (U. Chile)](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/126164/Reich_Martin_Supergene.pdf)
- At La Escondida, enrichment-blanket Cu grade increases with depth as the blanket followed a descending water table (Alpers & Brimhall 1989). — [Escondida paleohydrology paper](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Paleohydrologic%20evolution%20and%20geochemical%20dynamics%20of%20cumulative%20supergene%20metal%20enrichment%20at%20La%20Escondida%20Atacama%20Desert%20northern%20Chile%20Available.pdf) (secondary summary). **Contested:** Shaw et al. (2021) argue that erosional exhumation and recharge or morphotectonic setting matter more than aridity-induced water-table decay. At Cerro Colorado they record weathering from ~31 to ~2 Ma. — [Shaw et al. 2021](https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/publications/a-rusty-record-of-weathering-and-groundwater-movement-in-the-hype/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed simulation rule (porphyry Cu family):**
  - *Trigger:* active subduction with arc magmatism in an overriding-plate cell, with at least ~5-10 Myr of arc activity at that location (needed to build mid-crustal composite plutons).
  - *Gates:* the cell is in the arc belt (above the slab at roughly the depth where arc magmas form; take position from the simulated slab geometry). Stress regime is not strongly extensional (exclude cells with bimodal rift volcanism). No large caldera/ignimbrite flare-up flag in the same window, or apply a strong penalty.
  - *Weights:* (1) crustal thickness and contraction rate (Sillitoe's contractional link); (2) a change in convergence rate or slab dip within ±2-5 Myr (stress-regime change); (3) presence of arc-transverse structures (inherited sutures or transform traces in the cell); (4) arc magma flux. Gold-rich (Cu-Au) variants should be favored in island arcs and thin crust, Mo-rich variants in thick continental crust with felsic magmas. Both follow from the USGS 20c/21a settings and Sillitoe's Mo-felsic association.
  - *Epochs:* the per-arc "metallogenic epoch" is 10-20 Myr. Produce deposits in pulses. When slab dip changes, move the active belt inboard (flattening) or trenchward (steepening). This yields multiple parallel belts of different ages.
  - *Density:* calibrate so that exposed deposit density in a mature, moderately eroded arc is ~15 per 100,000 km² of permissive terrane. The formation rate must be higher, because only ~38% of Phanerozoic porphyries remain at any depth (47,789 of 125,895 in the Kesler-Wilkinson model).
  - *Clustering:* sample cluster centers along the arc (inter-cluster spacing tens of km; inferred, not sourced). Place 1-6 stocks per cluster within a 5-30 km cluster, 0.3-3 km apart, with ages spread 0-5 Myr.
  - *Vertical model:* paleosurface 0 km, then steam-heated or sinter cap (0-0.1 km), then lithocap / HS epithermal Au (0-1 km; disseminated Au in the top 0.5 km), then the transition to phyllic (sericitic) at ~1-1.5 km, then the porphyry ore shell (potassic core with chalcopyrite ± bornite ± magnetite) from ~1.5-2 to ~4 km in a column ~1 km across, then barren deep potassic/sodic-calcic, then the parental pluton (granodiorite/tonalite) at 5-15 km.
  - *Peripheral types* depend on wall rock: if the column contains carbonate within 0.5-4 km of the paleosurface, spawn skarn and carbonate-replacement deposits out to several km radius (Section 4).
- **Supergene rule (proposed):** if the deposit's ore shell top lies within ~0.1-0.5 km of the surface during an interval when (a) atmospheric O2 is modern-like (post-GOE oxygenation in the life/atmosphere timeline), (b) the climate is semi-arid to seasonally wet, not hyperarid, and (c) exhumation is slow (<~0.1 km/Myr) with a falling water table, then build a leached cap of limonite/hematite, an oxide zone of malachite (Cu carbonates and silicates), and a chalcocite enrichment blanket with some native copper just below the paleo water table. Remove it if later erosion exceeds its depth. Freeze it if the climate turns hyperarid (the Atacama ~14 Ma analogue). The O2 dependence is my inference from supergene oxidation chemistry. It was not explicitly stated in the sources read.
- **Block mapping:** the TFC list has no chalcopyrite or bornite. **Hypogene ore → TFC tetrahedrite** (the closest TFC copper sulfide). Tetrahedrite really occurs in late veins and peripheral zones (USGS 17/21a), so it is a defensible proxy, but flag it as a simplification. **Oxide zone → TFC malachite. Supergene blanket → mostly tetrahedrite as a chalcocite proxy, plus scattered native copper.** Au-rich porphyries add sparse native gold. Cu-Au potassic cores add **magnetite**. Pyrite halos → **pyrite** (TFC gem block). Leached cap / gossan → **limonite** and **hematite**. Lithocap → **kaolinite** and **sulfur** (steam-heated zone). Vanilla fallback: copper ore, plus sparse gold ore for Cu-Au porphyries. Mo and Re: no mapping.
- **Surface indicators:** bleached, red-brown limonitic hills (leached caps), white kaolinite/alunite lithocap ridges, and green malachite staining. In-game these could be colored terracotta or "altered rock" variants if the mod adds them.

### Gaps
- I did not verify a numeric arc-trench distance or slab-depth window for where porphyry belts sit. It is commonly cited but no source was read here.
- The relationship between slab flattening and porphyry formation (for example a "peak 0-5 Myr after flattening") is not quantified in Sillitoe (2010). Only the qualitative link to contraction, uplift and stress-regime change is supported. The flat-slab trigger hypothesis remains debated in the literature but was not researched in depth here.
- The USGS grade-tonnage medians (Singer et al.) exist only as figures in the HTML Bulletin 1693 pages and were not extractable. SIR 2010-5070-B (porphyry Cu model) was identified but not read in full.

## 3. Porphyry Mo (Climax-type and low-F arc type), porphyry Sn and rhyolite-hosted Sn

### Takeaway
Climax-type Mo deposits are rare (about 13 known), but large (100-1,000 Mt at 0.1-0.3% Mo). They form from F-rich (>1% F), high-silica (>75% SiO2), A-type granite-rhyolite in post-subduction extensional settings far inboard over thick crust, late in magmatic cycles. Low-F porphyry Mo is an arc-type calc-alkaline variant. Porphyry Sn (Bolivian type) forms 1-3 km beneath stratovolcanoes in thickened foldbelts. Rhyolite-hosted Sn is the shallow volcanic expression of Climax-like magmas and is mostly Oligocene-Miocene.

### Cited Findings
- **Climax-type definition (Ludington & Plumlee 2009):** only 13 deposits fit, all in western North America and mostly Tertiary. They form in a postsubduction, extensional setting and are invariably associated with A-type granites formed late in a magmatic cycle. Ore shells of quartz-molybdenite stockwork sit above and around the tops of highly evolved granite/rhyolite porphyry. Hosts have F commonly >1%, Rb >500 ppm, and enriched Nb-Ta. Grades are typically 0.1-0.3% Mo, and sizes typically 100-1,000 Mt. — [USGS OFR 2009-1215](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20091215) (secondary summary)
- **USGS model 16:** granite-rhyolite with >75% SiO2 and high Rb, Y, Nb. Mainly mid-Tertiary. "Mainly extensional zones in cratons. May be related to subduction, but found far from continental margins in areas of thick crust, and late in the cycles." The ore zone is a stockwork "draped over small, <1 km² stocks". Minerals: molybdenite + quartz ± fluorite ± K-feldspar ± pyrite ± wolframite ± cassiterite ± topaz. Halo of rhodochrosite, rhodonite and spessartine garnet. Associated with Ag-base-metal veins and fluorspar deposits. Weathers to yellow ferrimolybdite stains. — [USGS model 16](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull56zz.htm)
- Topaz rhyolites (Burt et al. 1982) are bimodal high-silica rhyolites inferred to be F-rich, with chemistry resembling Climax-related melts. About 30 occurrences are known. — [USGS OFR 2009-1215 / data release](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20091215) (secondary)
- **Low-F porphyry Mo (model 21b):** tonalite to monzogranite in orogenic belts with calc-alkaline intrusions. Molybdenite + pyrite + scheelite + chalcopyrite + argentian tetrahedrite. F <1,000 ppm. Zoned outward from Mo-Cu-W to Cu-Au to Zn-Pb-Au-Ag. — [USGS model 21b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0pwm.htm)
- **Porphyry Sn (model 20a):** subvolcanic quartz-porphyry stocks "emplaced 1 to 3 km beneath or within vents of terrestrial strato-volcanoes". The setting is a Paleozoic foldbelt cut by subduction-generated high-level stocks. The classic Bolivian deposits are Miocene. Stocks are funnel-shaped and 1-2 km². Zoning runs from a quartz-tourmaline core to sericite-tourmaline, sericite (with disseminated cassiterite) and propylitic. Supergene enrichment is unlikely, but cassiterite concentrates in nearby placers. Examples: Chorolque and Llallagua (Bolivia). — [USGS model 20a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9jce.htm)
- **Rhyolite-hosted Sn (Mexican type, model 25h):** cassiterite and wood tin in discontinuous veinlets 0.1-10 cm wide, seldom longer than 75 m, in alkali-feldspar rhyolite (>75% SiO2, including topaz rhyolites) flow-dome complexes, with derivative placers. Chiefly Oligocene-Miocene, in silicic volcanic fields over thick continental crust. Possibly a surface expression of Climax Mo systems. — [USGS model 25h](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2nn2.htm)

### Inferences
- **Climax rule (proposed):** trigger in a continental-interior cell 300-1,000+ km from the paleotrench (inferred from "far from continental margins"). Conditions: thick crust (above the ~45-50 km class), within ~0-15 Myr after subduction ends or the slab is removed, and a switch to extension with bimodal/A-type high-silica magmatism. Probability is very low: roughly 1-2 deposits per continent per major post-subduction extensional episode, given 13 known worldwide. Vertical model: ore shell 0.5-1.5 km above the cupola top, cupola at ~1-3 km depth (inferred; depth not given in sources read), rhyolite domes and Sn-topaz rhyolites at the surface.
- **Porphyry Sn rule:** continental arc over thickened older foldbelt crust (Bolivian Altiplano analogue). Favor cells whose stratigraphic column contains thick pelitic/metasedimentary basement (S-type melting source, inferred from Section 14 logic). Ore zone 1-3 km below the paleosurface.
- **Block mapping:** Climax Mo → no TFC/vanilla Mo block. Could place **TFC topaz** (gem) in greisen/topaz-rhyolite zones and fluorite (no block). Porphyry Sn and rhyolite Sn → **TFC cassiterite**, plus placers downstream. Low-F Mo → minor tetrahedrite (argentian), but mostly no mapping.

### Gaps
- Formation depth of Climax ore shells (commonly quoted as ~1-3 km) was not found in sources read.
- The 2025 USGS Climax-type magma geochemistry training set was referenced but not read.

## 4. Skarns (Fe, Cu, Zn-Pb, W, Sn, Mo, Au) and carbonate-replacement (manto) deposits

### Takeaway
Skarns need an intrusion in contact with carbonate (limestone/dolomite) or calcareous clastic rocks. Metal type follows magma type and setting. Fe skarns form with gabbro-granite in island arcs, Andean arcs and rifted margins. Cu(-Au) skarns form with oxidized tonalite-monzogranite at continental-margin arcs, often as part of porphyry systems. Zn-Pb skarns are distal, hundreds of meters or more from the intrusion. W skarns form at batholith contacts in syn-late-orogenic belts. Sn skarns form with post-orogenic specialized leucogranites. Skarns form mostly at <4 km. Carbonate-replacement (manto) Ag-Pb-Zn-Cu bodies extend beyond the skarn front.

### Cited Findings
- Meinert (1992, Geoscience Canada 19:145-162) reviews seven major classes of skarn deposit: Fe, W, Au, Cu, Zn, Mo and Sn. — [Geoscience Canada record](https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/GC/article/view/3773). Meinert, Dipple & Nicolescu (2005) "World skarn deposits" (Econ. Geol. 100th Anniv. Vol. 299-336) calls skarns "a most important source of Fe, W, Pb-Zn, Au, Cu". — [citation in repository](https://repository.geologyscience.ru/bitstreams/ccde4e35-915d-4854-b996-e2088d65e127/download) (secondary)
- **Depth:** skarn deposits "commonly form at depths of < 4 km". — [Muntean et al. 2011](https://nbmg.unr.edu/staff/pdfs/Muntean_Carlin_Gold_Nature_Geoscience_w_SupInfo.pdf)
- **Porphyry link:** carbonate wall rocks around porphyry Cu stocks commonly host proximal Cu-Au skarns, less common distal Zn-Pb and/or Au skarns, and, beyond the skarn front, carbonate-replacement Cu and/or Zn-Pb-Ag ± Au and sediment-hosted Au. Porphyry system radii reach ~8 km. Cu-Au skarns range from <1 Mt to >1 Gt. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Fe skarn (18d):** magnetite ± chalcopyrite ± Co-pyrite ± pyrite ± pyrrhotite in diopside-hedenbergite + grossular-andradite + epidote. Plutons range from gabbro and diorite to granite. Settings are oceanic island arc, Andean arc and rifted continental margin. Mainly Mesozoic-Tertiary but any age. "Magnetite generally crops out or forms abundant float"; strong magnetic anomaly. — [USGS model 18d](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull86em.htm)
- **Cu skarn (18b):** chalcopyrite + pyrite ± hematite ± magnetite ± bornite ± pyrrhotite, with possible Au and Ag. Diopside-andradite center, wollastonite-tremolite outer zone, marble periphery. Continental-margin late-orogenic magmatism. Weathers to Cu carbonates and silicates and Fe-rich gossan. Calc-silicate pebbles in streams guide exploration. — [USGS model 18b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7nxq.htm)
- **Zn-Pb skarn (18c):** sphalerite + galena ± pyrrhotite ± magnetite ± chalcopyrite. Mn-rich calc-silicates (Mn-hedenbergite, bustamite, rhodonite). Especially at shale-limestone contacts. "Deposit may be hundreds of meters from intrusive". Gossan with strong Mn-oxide stains. — [USGS model 18c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4u7i.htm)
- **W skarn (14a):** scheelite ± molybdenite ± pyrrhotite ± sphalerite ± chalcopyrite at contacts and roof pendants of tonalite-granodiorite-quartz monzonite batholiths intruding limestone. Orogenic belts, syn-late orogenic. Mainly Mesozoic. — [USGS model 14a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2y9a.htm)
- **Sn skarn (14b):** cassiterite ± scheelite ± sphalerite etc. near leucocratic biotite/muscovite "specialized" granite (SiO2 >73%, K2O >4%, enriched in Sn, F, Rb, Li, Be, W) intruding carbonates. Granite emplacement is generally late or post-orogenic. Skarn develops "up to 300 m from intrusion". Much Sn may be locked in silicates. Erosion leads to tin placers. — [USGS model 14b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2i5q.htm)
- **Polymetallic replacement / manto (19a):** Ag-Pb-Zn-Cu in massive lenses, pipes and veins in limestone or dolomite near porphyritic calc-alkaline plutons. Zoned outward from enargite-tetrahedrite-Cu, to galena-sphalerite-Ag sulfosalts, to sphalerite-rhodochrosite. Wall rocks are dolomitized and silicified (jasperoid). Mobile belts with moderate deformation and small plutons. Oxidizes to ochreous masses with cerussite, anglesite, hemimorphite and cerargyrite (horn silver). — [USGS model 19a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7q0k.htm)

### Inferences
- **Skarn rule (proposed):** whenever any intrusive event (porphyry, granite, gabbro) is placed at depth z_i, scan the cell's stratigraphic column for carbonate layers (limestone, dolomite, marl) within the intrusion's roof zone (z_i - 3 km to z_i, and above 4 km paleodepth). Choose the metal suite from the intrusion type:
  - arc gabbro-diorite → Fe skarn (magnetite);
  - oxidized arc granodiorite/porphyry → Cu-Au skarn (proximal), with Zn-Pb skarn 0.2-1 km out and manto Ag-Pb-Zn plus distal Au up to several km;
  - reduced syn-orogenic granodiorite → W skarn (scheelite);
  - post-orogenic specialized S-type/A-type granite → Sn skarn within 300 m, with Sn veins and greisen in the cupola;
  - Mo skarn → minor variant of W or Cu skarn (no source read).

  Geometry: irregular to tabular bodies along the contact, typically tens to hundreds of meters thick and up to ~1-2 km along strike (inferred from model descriptions; not quantified in sources). Probability scales with carbonate thickness and contact area.
- **Dependence on simulated history:** carbonate platforms are created by the deposition model (warm shallow seas). So skarn abundance should naturally rise in arcs built on former passive margins, such as the Cordillera on its Paleozoic miogeocline. The "miogeosynclinal sequences" setting in USGS 18b/18c and 19a supports this.
- **Block mapping:** Fe skarn → **magnetite** (TFC; vanilla iron). Cu skarn → **tetrahedrite** (proxy) plus **malachite** gossan, and native gold if Au-rich. Zn-Pb skarn and mantos → **sphalerite** (TFC; galena has no TFC block), **native silver** in oxidized mantos (horn silver proxy), and **limonite** gossan. W skarn → no TFC W block (scheelite unmapped). Sn skarn → **cassiterite**. Au skarn → **native gold**. Garnet/pyroxene skarn rock could be a decorative "skarn" stone if the mod adds custom rock.

### Gaps
- No source read gives skarn tonnage or grade distributions per class. The USGS grade-tonnage pages exist (for example [Cu skarn GT](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2ir2.htm) and [Fe skarn GT](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull317y.htm)), but their numbers are only in figures.
- Au skarn and Mo skarn characteristics (reduced vs. oxidized Au skarns) come from Meinert's reviews, which I could not read in full.
- Lapis lazuli (lazurite) forms by metasomatism of marble near intrusions, a skarn-like process. It is relevant to the TFC lapis block, but no source was read. Cross-check with the metamorphic/gem researcher.

## 5. Epithermal Au-Ag (high-, intermediate-, low-sulfidation; alkalic Au-Te) and sinter

### Takeaway
Epithermal deposits form from the paleosurface to about 1-1.5 km depth (rarely 2 km) at <150-300 °C, mostly by boiling or mixing in volcanic-hosted geothermal systems. High-sulfidation (HS) types sit in porphyry lithocaps, while intermediate-sulfidation (IS) types are porphyry-peripheral veins. Low-sulfidation (LS) types are geothermal-like, often in extension, with silica sinter at the surface. Because they are so shallow, preserved examples are overwhelmingly Cenozoic (most 40-4 Ma in USGS compilations). Dimensions are small: <0.5-2 km laterally and <0.5 km vertical ore intervals.

### Cited Findings
- **Depth and temperature:** fluid inclusions show epithermal ores forming from <150 °C to ~300 °C, from the surface to as deep as 1-2 km. LS fluids resemble those in active geothermal systems. — [White & Hedenquist 1995, SEG Newsletter](https://www.literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Epithermal%20gold%20deposits%20Styles%20characteristics%20and%20exploration.pdf). A teaching summary gives <300 °C and <1 km depth, typical deposit dimensions of <500 to ~2,000 m horizontally and <500 m vertically, and frequently blind deposits. — [CUSO epithermal course notes](https://mineral.cuso.ch/fileadmin/mineral/1-Epithermal-Deposits.pdf). Other reviews citing Simmons et al. (2005) and Hedenquist et al. (2000) give ore deposition at <1,500 m and up to ~300 °C (secondary; I could not open Simmons et al. 2005).
- Paleosurface temperatures are ≤~100 °C, rising along the boiling-point-for-depth curve to ~300 °C at ~1,000 m. Au-Ag deposits can form as deep as ~1 km, with vertical ore intervals up to 500-600 m. — Hedenquist's work as summarized with [White & Hedenquist 1995](https://www.literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Epithermal%20gold%20deposits%20Styles%20characteristics%20and%20exploration.pdf) (secondary summary; numbers should be verified)
- **Sulfidation states and continuum:** sulfidation state (Barton 1970) is set by temperature and S2 fugacity. Transients in sulfidation state occur in all three epithermal types. Einaudi et al. (2003) argue for a continuum linking porphyry, base-metal vein and epithermal environments. — [Einaudi, Hedenquist & Inan 2003](https://pyrite.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGF11USA-2013/Readings/EinaudiHedenquist2003sulfidation.pdf)
- **HS in lithocaps:** HS deposits form in lithocaps above porphyry Cu deposits. Massive sulfide (pyrite-enargite) lodes form in deeper feeders, and disseminated Au ± Ag in the uppermost ~500 m. IS veins form on lithocap peripheries. Large HS Au deposits also form in contractional settings at the tops of tectonically thickened crust, but not together with giant porphyry Cu (Sillitoe & Hedenquist 2003). Pueblo Viejo is a major isolated HS deposit. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Steam-heated blankets:** H2S-bearing vapor condenses into groundwater in the vadose zone, producing blanket-like advanced argillic alteration over the system. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)
- **Epithermal quartz-alunite Au (HS; model 25e):** native gold + enargite + pyrite in vuggy veins and breccias within advanced argillic (quartz-alunite-pyrophyllite ± diaspore, zunyite) alteration. Found within volcanic edifices and caldera ring fractures, or over evaporitic basement. Late native sulfur may occur. Weathers to abundant yellow limonite, jarosite and white kaolinite. — [USGS model 25e](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0vxx.htm)
- **Quartz-adularia veins (LS/IS):**
  - *Comstock (25c):* Au, electrum, Ag sulfosalts and argentite in vuggy quartz-adularia veins over clastic basement. Most 40-3.7 Ma. Fracture systems, normal faults, ring fractures. — [USGS model 25c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5nqr.htm)
  - *Creede (25b; IS-like, base-metal rich):* galena, sphalerite, chalcopyrite, sulfosalts ± Au in quartz-carbonate veins. Tied to saline fluids from evaporites or seawater-bearing basement. Most 29-4 Ma. — [USGS model 25b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7a0j.htm)
  - *Sado (25d):* over older volcanic or batholithic basement. Most 38-5 Ma. — [USGS model 25d](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7khf.htm)
- **Hot-spring Au-Ag (25a):** fine-grained silica and silicified breccia with Au, pyrite and Sb-As sulfides in rhyolitic centers and the shallow parts of geothermal systems. Mainly Tertiary-Quaternary. Settings are subduction-related arcs, rifted continental margins and leaky transforms. Zoned from chalcedonic sinter and massive silicification at the top down to quartz-adularia veins. Au + As + Sb + Hg + Tl are higher in the system, and Ag increases with depth. — [USGS model 25a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0g4n.htm)
- **Alkalic Au-Ag-Te veins (22b):** calaverite and sylvanite with purple fluorite and roscoelite in caldera breccias related to syenite, phonolite and shoshonite. Settings are a disrupted Precambrian craton with thin cover, or the late shoshonitic stage of island arcs. Mainly Cretaceous-Tertiary. Example: Cripple Creek. Tellurides weather easily, and the resulting "flour" gold may not form placers. — [USGS model 22b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9xtf.htm)
- **Boiling:** boiling is widely linked to gold precipitation but is not universal. It can be "cryptic", for example through the ~450 m vertical extent of Profitis Ilias, Milos. — (search-derived summaries of epithermal literature; see [CUSO notes](https://mineral.cuso.ch/fileadmin/mineral/1-Epithermal-Deposits.pdf))

### Inferences
- **Proposed simulation rule:**
  - *HS:* generate only as the top member of a porphyry system (Section 2) or as a standalone in contractional, thickened arcs. Ore zone 0-0.5 km for disseminated Au and 0.3-1 km for enargite lodes. Lithocap 0-1 km, several to 100 km².
  - *IS:* 0-1 km on the lithocap periphery (1-5 km from porphyry centers), or in arcs with evaporitic basement (Creede-type).
  - *LS:* triggered by (a) extensional or back-arc arc segments, or rifted continental margins with bimodal or rhyolitic volcanism, or (b) leaky transforms. Ore 0.1-1 km below the paleosurface. Sinter apron at the surface.
  - *Alkalic Au-Te:* rare; craton-margin alkaline magmatism (shoshonite/phonolite) after subduction or rifting.
  - *Preservation:* require E ≤ ~1 km since formation. Allow rare old preservation when the cell was buried under younger volcanics or sediments soon after formation (E < 0 phase), then gently exhumed.
- **Geometry:** veins 1-5 m wide (inferred), 0.5-2 km long, in swarms. Bonanza ore shoots occupy a 100-500 m vertical "boiling window". Place it within the visible window when E is in [0.2, 0.8] km. Sinter is a 1-10 m thick siliceous cap (inferred).
- **Block mapping:** LS and IS → **native gold** and **native silver** (TFC) in quartz veins; vanilla gold. IS base-metal veins → **sphalerite**, **tetrahedrite**. HS → native gold plus **tetrahedrite** as an enargite proxy, **pyrite**, **kaolinite**, and **sulfur** near the top. Sinter/chalcedony → **opal** (TFC gem) as a rare drop. Hg-rich tops grade into Section 6 cinnabar (**redstone**). Alkalic Au-Te → native gold. Purple fluorite has no mapping.

### Gaps
- I did not open Simmons, White & John (2005) or Hedenquist et al. (2000) directly. Depth and temperature ranges differ slightly across sources (1, 1.5 or 2 km maximum).
- No quantitative global abundance figures for epithermal Au (share of world Au) were found.

## 6. Hot-spring and other Hg (cinnabar) deposits, and simple Sb (stibnite)

### Takeaway
Cinnabar (TFC redstone) has three main hydrothermal settings: (1) hot-spring Hg in siliceous sinter near the paleo water table (<300 m deep), in continental-margin rifts with small-volume mafic-intermediate volcanism; (2) silica-carbonate Hg at serpentinite-shale contacts in accreted terranes above subduction thrusts (to ~1,000 m deep); and (3) Almadén-type stratabound Hg, the world's largest Hg concentration (Silurian; intraplate submarine alkaline volcanism). Hg also accompanies Carlin-type and hot-spring Au. Simple Sb (stibnite) veins form in faults and shear zones in any orogenic area and are associated with Hg, Au and W.

### Cited Findings
- **Hot-spring Hg (27a, "Sulphur Bank type"):** cinnabar + native Hg + minor marcasite, disseminated and coating fractures in siliceous sinter over graywacke, shale, andesite and basalt. Tertiary. Formed near the paleo groundwater table in fossil hot-spring systems along high-angle faults. Setting: "Continental margin rifting associated with small-volume mafic to intermediate volcanism." Alteration above the paleo water table is kaolinite, alunite, Fe oxides and native sulfur; opal at the water table; pyrite, zeolites, K-feldspar, chlorite and quartz below. Geochemistry Hg + As + Sb + Au. Associated with hot-spring Au. — [USGS model 27a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4von.htm)
- **Silica-carbonate Hg (27c, "New Almaden type"):** cinnabar at the contact of serpentinite and siltstone-graywacke above a subduction-related thrust in accreted terrane. Serpentinite replaced by quartz + dolomite (± hydrocarbons). Tertiary. Associated with stibnite veins. — [USGS model 27c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull69rb.htm)
- **Depths:** in the California Coast Ranges, hot-spring Hg deposits form at <300 m and silica-carbonate deposits extend to ~1,000 m. Active springs still precipitate Hg and Au (Smith, Kesler, Blum & Rytuba 2008, EPSL). — [USGS publication record](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/isotope-geochemistry-mercury-source-rocks-mineral-deposits-and-spring-deposits) (abstract)
- At Sulphur Bank, part of the Hg vapor is fixed as cinnabar by native sulfur generated by bacterial reduction of sulfate. — [Northern Miner](https://northernminer.com/news/americas-reclamation-of-california-mercury-deposits-a/1000137723) (secondary, journalistic)
- **Almadén (27b):** stratabound disseminated cinnabar and native Hg in permeable sedimentary rocks near volcanic centers along a major deep-seated fault zone. Almadén is Silurian. "Absence of regional metamorphism or plutonism following mineralization." — [USGS model 27b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull56g7.htm). The Almadén district is the largest Hg concentration on Earth. Ore occurs in the Lower Silurian "Criadero Quartzite" (three mineralized levels) plus stockworks. The model (Higueras et al. 2013) ties ore to Silurian intraplate alkaline submarine volcanism that degassed Hg accumulated in magma chambers. Cr-mica ages are 365-427 Ma, and two mineralization styles are separated by up to ~80 Myr. — [Higueras et al. 2013 (UCM repository)](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/92cec923-d7ff-4b44-ba9f-1330cb3ab76e). IGME calls it "the largest known mercury deposit" with production dating to the 3rd century BCE. Metallurgy ceased in 2003. — [IGME heritage sheet](https://www.igme.es/patrimonio/LigMes/2022/Almaden.pdf). The often-quoted "~1/3 of all Hg ever produced" appears in search summaries but was not verified in the documents themselves.
- **Simple Sb (27d):** stibnite + quartz ± pyrite ± calcite veins, pods and disseminations in brecciated or sheared fault zones. Any orogenic area. Paleozoic-Tertiary. At least a third contain Au or Ag. Associated with low-sulfide Au-quartz veins, epithermal Au, hot-spring Au, carbonate-hosted Au, Sn-W veins and hot-spring/disseminated Hg. — [USGS model 27d](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull40bm.htm)
- Carlin-type ore contains cinnabar as an accessory and has an As + Hg + Sb + Tl signature. — [USGS model 26a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7m7b.htm)

### Inferences
- **Why this matters for TFC:** in TFC, cinnabar yields redstone, so these rules control redstone availability. Hg deposits are shallow (<0.3-1 km) and mostly Tertiary. Without a fallback, redstone would be rare on old, deeply eroded continents. The design could add an "Almadén-type" stratabound option (rare, any age, survives because it sits within thick sedimentary sequences) and Carlin/orogenic accessory cinnabar, so redstone exists across more of the world.
- **Proposed simulation rules:**
  - *Hot-spring Hg:* cells with geothermal activity in continental rifts, back-arcs, leaky transforms, or arcs with small-volume mafic-intermediate volcanism in the last ~0-30 Myr. Ore at 0-0.3 km. Requires E < 0.3 km. Place cinnabar disseminated in sinter-like silica caps along faults, as patches 50-500 m across (inferred).
  - *Silica-carbonate Hg:* accretionary-prism cells where the column contains serpentinite (ophiolitic slices) juxtaposed with shale or graywacke, during or after accretion with a high-heat-flow trigger (slab window / transform onset, inferred from the California Coast Range setting). Ore at 0-1 km along the serpentinite contact.
  - *Almadén-type:* intraplate submarine alkaline basaltic volcanism (plume or rift) erupting through a quartz-sandstone or shale shelf sequence. Stratabound in the sandstone. Very rare; ~1 per continent.
  - *Accessory Hg:* add low-probability cinnabar to the tops of epithermal (Section 5) and Carlin (Section 11) systems.
- **Simple Sb:** add stibnite (no block) as flavor to orogenic Au (Section 9) and Hg systems. **No TFC/vanilla mapping for Sb.**
- **Block mapping:** cinnabar → **TFC cinnabar** (gives redstone); vanilla fallback **redstone ore**. Native Hg has no block. Sulfur in the acid-altered zone above the water table → **TFC sulfur**. Opal at the paleo water table → **TFC opal** (rare). Kaolinite above the water table → **TFC kaolinite**.

### Gaps
- No global Hg tonnage share by deposit type was found. The USGS hot-spring Hg grade-tonnage page lists deposits but gives numbers only in figures ([GT page](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2p5z.htm)).
- Major Hg belts in China (Guizhou, carbonate-hosted), Idrija (Slovenia) and Huancavelica (Peru) were not researched. Idrija and the Chinese belts may need an additional "carbonate-hosted Hg" rule.

## 7. Iron oxide-copper-gold (IOCG) and iron oxide-apatite (Kiruna-type, IOA)

### Takeaway
IOCG deposits (Olympic Dam type) are structurally controlled hematite- or magnetite-rich breccias with Cu-Au ± U ± REE. They are temporally but not spatially tied to anomalous, often alkaline, mantle-influenced mafic-felsic magmatism. Giant examples are mostly Proterozoic (most 1.6 Ga-850 Ma, near Archean-Paleoproterozoic cratons). Groves et al. link giant IOCGs to Precambrian mantle-plume activity, but this is contested. Kiruna-type IOA are massive magnetite-apatite bodies in intermediate to alkalic volcanic sequences, from Paleoproterozoic (Kiruna, ~1.88 Ga) to Cretaceous-Holocene (Chilean Iron Belt). Their origin, magmatic (immiscible melt or magnetite flotation) or hydrothermal, is actively debated.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition (Groves et al. 2010, Econ. Geol. 105:641-654):** magmatic-hydrothermal deposits with economic Cu ± Au ± U grades. They are structurally controlled, commonly with breccias, and have abundant low-Ti iron oxides or iron silicates intimately associated with Cu sulfides. They lack quartz veins and silicification and show a temporal, but not spatial, link to causative intrusions. The intrusions are "highly anomalous, commonly alkaline to subalkaline, mixed mafic (even ultramafic) to felsic", with evidence for mantle derivation. The authors warn the IOCG group became "too embracing". — [Groves et al. 2010 (abstract via Scinapse)](https://scinapse.io/papers/2083808432) (abstract/secondary)
- Groves argues that giant IOCG formation and preservation was "largely a Precambrian phenomenon related to heightened activity of mantle plumes". — [AusIMM webinar description](https://www.ausimm.com/videos/community-event/geoscience-societyagc-webinar-iron-oxide-copper-gold-iocg-deposits) (secondary; flagged as presenter interpretation)
- **Size, setting and age:** orebodies are ~10 to >4,000 Mt at 0.2-5% Cu and 0.1-1.41 g/t Au. Olympic Dam is 8,330 Mt at 0.8% Cu. They form at depths from >10 km to the paleosurface (porphyry and skarn are shallower, <5 km). Ages range from 1.8 Ga to 15 Ma, most 1.6 Ga-850 Ma. Larger deposits lie near Paleoproterozoic and Archean cratons. Alteration runs from sodic-calcic (albite-epidote; albite-magnetite at depth) to potassic (K-feldspar) and hematite-sericite at shallower levels. Fluids are mixtures of deep high-temperature magmatic or metamorphic brines and oxidized surficial or ground waters. Olympic Dam is linked to the 1,570 Ma Hiltaba granite suite. — [Wikipedia: IOCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide_copper_gold_ore_deposits) (tertiary; numbers consistent with primary literature but not verified there)
- **USGS Olympic Dam model (29b):** hematite + bornite + chalcopyrite (+ chalcocite) in breccia filling a deep narrow graben in Proterozoic alkali granite. "The only example is 1,500 m.y. old". Signature Cu + U + Co + Au + Ag + LREE + F + Ba; very low Pb and Zn. Local gravity high from iron oxides; magnetic high. — [USGS model 29b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5flh.htm)
- **Kiruna / volcanic-hosted magnetite (25i):** massive concordant and discordant magnetite-apatite in andesitic to trachytic flows and subvolcanic intrusions, with actinolite-diopside alteration. Mesozoic-Holocene around the Pacific. 1,300-1,500 Ma in Sweden and Missouri as listed by the model. Setting is a continental-margin subduction-related volcanic terrane, especially with high-K volcanics and the waning stages of volcanism. Strong magnetic anomalies. — [USGS model 25i](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7mn9.htm)
- **Kiirunavaara:** Paleoproterozoic (~1.89-1.88 Ga). More than 2 Bt of ore mined. Orebody over 4,000 m along strike, averaging 80 m wide. — [Scandinavian Resources project page](https://scandinavianresources.com/srl) (company source). It extends from the surface to 1,300-2,400 m depth. — (mining summary via [SGA/LTU field trip page](https://www.sga.agh.edu.pl/LTU_kiruna_2023.html), secondary). The Kiirunavaara and Malmberget deposits together host ~3 Gt current resources (conference paper; secondary).
- **Genetic debate (Chilean Iron Belt):** Knipping et al. (2015, Geology) studied ~350 Mt Los Colorados with Fe-O isotopes and magnetite chemistry. They argue for a magmatic origin by buoyant magnetite-bubble suspensions that deposit massive ore along transcurrent faults, linking IOA to IOCG. — [Knipping et al. 2015 (U. Chile repository)](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/134701). Cerro Negro Norte (Cretaceous, Coastal Cordillera) is associated with a diorite dated 129.6 ± 1.0 Ma and supports a magmatic-hydrothermal flotation model. — [U. Chile repository](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/150642). The competing models are immiscible Fe-oxide melt versus hydrothermal replacement. — same sources.

### Inferences
- **Proposed IOCG rule:**
  - *Trigger:* large-scale mantle-derived magmatism (LIP or plume head, or alkaline mafic-felsic suites) affecting the margin of an old craton (Archean-Paleoproterozoic lithosphere in the cell's column), commonly in a back-arc or intracontinental extensional or transtensional regime.
  - *Secular weighting:* strongly favor 1.9-0.85 Ga; allow rare Phanerozoic (Andean Cretaceous IOCG such as Candelaria, not sourced here).
  - *Gates:* oxidized brines need evaporite or redbed sources or an oxygenated hydrosphere (inferred). Favor cells with evaporites or redbeds in the column and post-GOE time.
  - *Geometry:* breccia pipes or graben-fill bodies 1-5 km long, along crustal-scale faults.
  - *Depth:* 1-10 km. Hematite-rich (shallow) vs. magnetite-rich (deep) facies selected by formation depth.
  - *Abundance:* very few giants. ~1 per craton margin affected by a plume, plus satellites.
- **IOA rule:** waning stage of continental-arc or back-arc magmatism with high-K andesite/trachyte (Chile Cretaceous) or Paleoproterozoic continental arc/back-arc (Kiruna). Magnetite-apatite lenses tens to hundreds of meters wide and 1-4 km long along faults. Depth 0-2 km; exposure robust because massive magnetite resists weathering.
- **Block mapping:** IOCG → **hematite** (TFC) or **magnetite** depending on depth, plus **tetrahedrite**/**malachite** (Cu proxy), **native gold**, and **native copper** in supergene zones. U, REE and Co have no mapping. IOA → **magnetite** (TFC; vanilla iron). Apatite has no mapping. Large magnetic anomaly flag for any in-game prospecting tool.

### Gaps
- I did not read Hitzman et al. (1992) or Williams et al. (2005) for depth and temperature; the IOCG ranges come from Wikipedia.
- The age of Carajás IOCGs (~2.57 Ga, Archean) and Andean IOCG examples are not sourced here.
- The magmatic vs. hydrothermal IOA debate is unresolved in sources read. Treat both as "magmatic-hydrothermal" for simulation purposes.

## 8. Volcanogenic massive sulfide (VMS: Cyprus, Besshi, Kuroko/Noranda, bimodal types) and modern seafloor massive sulfides (black smokers)

### Takeaway
VMS deposits are seafloor-exhalative Cu-Zn-Pb-Ag-Au sulfide lenses (mounds) above stockwork feeder "pipes". They form in extensional submarine volcanic settings (ridges, rifted arcs, back-arcs) from seawater convection driven by subvolcanic intrusions, from 3.4-3.55 Ga to the present. About 800 deposits over 0.2 Mt are known. They provide ~22% of world Zn production historically, plus significant Cu, Pb, Ag and Au. They occur in clusters (camps) of 8-35 deposits above large subvolcanic sills, within linear rifts or calderas. Preservation requires the host to escape subduction: ophiolite obduction (Cyprus type) or arc/back-arc accretion. Modern black smokers vent at ~350-400 °C and build typically 1-5 Mt mounds. About 1,000 deposits totaling ~600 Mt may exist in the modern neovolcanic zones.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition and products:** typically lenses of polymetallic massive sulfide formed at or near the seafloor. A mound-shaped to tabular stratabound body of >40% sulfide sits on discordant stockwork pipes. They are major sources of Zn, Cu, Pb, Ag and Au, and significant for Co, Sn, Se, Mn, Cd, In, Bi, Te, Ga and Ge. — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf). They form where magma-heated fluids mix with bottom or pore water. Ages range from 3.55 Ga to zero-age deposits on the modern seafloor. Sizes range from <1 t pods to Rio Tinto at 1.5 Gt. — [USGS SIR 2010-5070-C](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105070C)
- **Global numbers:** close to 800 deposits over 200,000 t, from the 3.4 Ga Pilbara to active modern systems. More than 5 billion t of sulfide ore supplied up to 2002. At least 22% of world Zn, 6% Cu, 9.7% Pb, 8.7% Ag and 2.2% Au (Singer 1995). Size classes: >50 Mt is giant, >150 Mt is supergiant. The Iberian Pyrite Belt has 88 deposits, 7 over 100 Mt, totaling 1.575 Gt. Neves Corvo is >270 Mt. Kidd Creek is ~148-149 Mt. Bathurst camp >300 Mt. Windy Craggy 297 Mt. — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf)
- **Classification:** by metal ratio (Cu-Zn, Zn-Cu, Zn-Pb-Cu; Franklin et al. 1981, Large 1992). By host-rock lithology, five groups (Barrie & Hannington 1999): mafic, bimodal-mafic, bimodal-felsic, siliciclastic-mafic (pelite-mafic), and bimodal-siliciclastic (felsic-siliciclastic). Mafic-associated groups are more common in oceanic arcs and spreading centers; felsic-dominated groups in arc-continent margin and continental arc regimes. — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf)
- **Cyprus type (24a):** pyrite + chalcopyrite + sphalerite in pillow basalts of ophiolites, above sheeted dikes. Formed at submarine hot springs along axial grabens of oceanic or back-arc spreading ridges, or seamounts. "Archean(?) to Tertiary--majority are Ordovician or Cretaceous." Massive sulfide (>60%) over a stringer zone. Some are overlain by Fe-rich ochre. Weathering gives "massive limonite gossans". — [USGS model 24a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull15r9.htm)
- **Besshi type (24b):** thin, sheet-like, laterally extensive bodies of pyrite-pyrrhotite-chalcopyrite in thinly laminated clastic sediments and mafic tuffs, in strongly deformed metamorphic terranes. Mainly Paleozoic-Mesozoic. Possibly a sediment-covered spreading ridge or rifted arc basin. Clusters in an en echelon pattern. Co/Ni > 1. — [USGS model 24b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2qg5.htm)
- **Kuroko / Noranda type (28a):** Cu-Zn-Pb massive sulfide in marine rhyolite-dacite. Archean-Cenozoic. Island arcs with local extension, and Archean greenstone belts. Zoned: upper "black ore" (sphalerite-galena-barite) over lower "yellow ore" (pyrite-chalcopyrite) over a stringer zone. Gypsum and anhydrite present in some. — [USGS model 28a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0bfp.htm)
- **Setting and district scale:** all VMS form in extensional settings. Preserved deposits formed mainly in nascent-arc, rifted-arc and back-arc settings. Most significant deposits cluster in mining camps restricted to linear rifts or calderas, above elongate gabbroic or composite subvolcanic intrusions. Prospective complexes have high-level sill-dike swarms, felsic extrusive centers, and large subvolcanic composite intrusions (>15 km long, 2,000 m thick). Stacked semiconformable alteration zones reach 2,000-3,000 m aggregate thickness. Host-rock lithology is assessed up to 3,000 m below and 5,000 m along strike. Camp examples: Archean Noranda (14 bimodal-mafic deposits), Paleoproterozoic Flin Flon (17), Snow Lake (8, from two intrusions), Ordovician Bathurst (35). A 5 km diameter circle approximates each deposit's proximal alteration footprint. — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf)
- **Secular peaks (Canadian exploration criteria):** VMS formation coincides with periods of major ocean closing and terrane accretion: Late Archean (2.8-2.69 Ga), Paleoproterozoic (1.92-1.87 Ga), Cambro-Ordovician (500-450 Ma), Devono-Mississippian (370-340 Ma) and Early Jurassic (200-180 Ma). — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf). The 2.1-2.0 Ga Cape Smith/Labrador Trough rifts had extensive hydrothermal systems but lack large VMS, which is posed as an open question. — same source.
- **Zoning within the lens:** chalcopyrite-rich core with a sphalerite-galena-rich outer zone, from progressive "zone refining". — [Galley et al. 2007](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Volcanogenic%20Massive%20Sulphide%20Deposits.pdf)
- **Modern seafloor:**
  - Vent fluids reach up to ~400 °C. Massive and stockwork sulfides precipitate as they mix with seawater. Seafloor deposits can reach up to ~100 Mt. — [Herzig, Petersen & Hannington, ISA technical study](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/5041/1/TechStudy2.pdf)
  - Classic black smokers vent at ~350 °C. Many deposits contain 1-5 Mt of sulfide (for example TAG). The largest are sediment-hosted: Middle Valley up to ~15 Mt and Atlantis II Deep (Red Sea) up to ~90 Mt. — [GEOMAR review (oceanrep)](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/30396) (secondary summary)
  - Slow-spreading ridges can host larger, Cu-Au-rich SMS deposits than fast ridges. — [German, Petersen & Hannington 2016](https://darchive.mblwhoilibrary.org/entities/publication/4fdd8b81-1684-572d-b199-f22aac087aa9)
  - From 92 known deposits, ~1,000 deposits with ~600 Mt total sulfide are predicted in the modern neovolcanic zones. The Endeavour segment has ~1.2 Mt. — [Jamieson PhD thesis (U. Ottawa)](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/items/8b3cef80-4c5c-4e79-bac4-c0694fabf913/full) (single model-based estimate)

### Inferences
- **Proposed simulation rule:**
  - *Formation:* every Myr, each submarine extensional volcanic cell has a probability p of forming a VMS cluster. Eligible cells are mid-ocean ridge segments, back-arc spreading centers, rifted/nascent arcs, and sediment-covered ridges. p is weighted by magma supply (spreading rate, arc rifting rate) and favors slow-spreading ridges for large Cu-Au mounds.
  - *Subtype by column composition:*
    - Ophiolitic MORB with no sediment → Cyprus (Cu-Zn, pyrite-rich).
    - Basalt plus terrigenous sediment → Besshi (Cu-Zn-Co, thin sheets).
    - Bimodal arc rhyolite-dacite-basalt → Kuroko/Noranda (Zn-Pb-Cu-Ag-Au, barite).
    - Continental back-arc siliciclastic-felsic → Iberian/Bathurst type, giant Zn-Pb.
  - *Geometry:* lens 50-500 m diameter and 5-100 m thick (inferred from Mt-scale tonnage), over a 100-500 m stockwork pipe. Clusters of 5-35 lenses at 1-5 km spacing over a 10-30 km subvolcanic intrusion footprint.
  - *Preservation:* after formation, track the host crust. If subducted, the deposit is deleted. If accreted in an arc collision, obducted (ophiolite), or the back-arc basin closes onto a continent, it becomes a "continental VMS", deformed and metamorphosed with the terrane. Burial under ≥0.1-1 km of later volcanics or sediments protects it from seafloor oxidation. Exposure later depends on E.
  - *Secular pattern:* the Archean-Paleoproterozoic peaks should emerge from the sim's rapid crustal growth and accretion events. The Galley peaks coincide with accretion, which preserves VMS.
  - *Oxygenation dependence (inferred):* Precambrian (pre-GOE) seawater lacked sulfate, so barite-rich Kuroko-type tops should be rarer before ~2.4 Ga. No source read states this explicitly.
- **Block mapping:** massive pyrite → **pyrite** (TFC gem; very abundant), **sphalerite** (TFC), **tetrahedrite** as a chalcopyrite proxy, **native gold**/**native silver** (minor, Au-rich VMS). Gossan cap → **limonite**/**hematite**, plus **malachite** if Cu-rich. Cyprus ochre → **limonite**. Barite and gypsum/anhydrite → **gypsum** (TFC) for Kuroko-type tops. Vanilla: copper ore plus gold ore (sparse).
- **Surface indicators:** red-brown to yellow gossans ("iron hats"), chert/exhalite horizons, and pale altered footwall (chlorite-sericite) beneath.

### Gaps
- I could not open Franklin et al. (2005, Econ. Geol. 100th Anniv. Vol.). The global secular-distribution argument (for example bimodal-felsic dominating the Archean) is not verified here.
- Galley's age peaks are framed for Canadian exploration and may not represent the global distribution. Iberian (~350 Ma) and Urals (Devonian) giants fit the 370-340 Ma peak.
- Deposit lens dimensions (diameter and thickness) were not given numerically in sources read. The values above are inferred.

## 9. Orogenic gold (mesozonal and related quartz-vein gold in greenstone belts and slate belts; including Homestake-type BIF-hosted)

### Takeaway
Orogenic gold forms in accretionary and collisional orogens from low-salinity, aqueous-carbonic (5-20 mol% CO2) metamorphic fluids along second- and third-order structures near crustal-scale faults. It deposits over an unusually large depth range: epizonal <6 km, mesozonal 6-12 km and hypozonal >12 km (to ~15-20 km), at ~180 to >700 °C (most ~250-400 °C, not verified here). It is episodic in the Precambrian (peaks ~2.8-2.55 Ga and ~2.1-1.8 Ga) and continuous since ~600 Ma. It has the highest preservation potential of any gold deposit type. Phanerozoic orogenic gold (with placers) exceeds 1 billion oz, about twice the known Precambrian endowment.

### Cited Findings
- **Depth classes:** Groves et al. (1998, Ore Geol. Rev.) subdivide orogenic deposits into epizonal (<6 km), mesozonal (6-12 km) and hypozonal (>12 km). Gold deposition spans 15-20 km to near-surface. — [USGS pub record, Groves et al. 1998](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70021148) (abstract). The "crustal continuum" model accommodates this range; one cited example ranges from deep hypozonal (15-20 km) to shallow epizonal (2-5 km). — [Hart, Geoscience BC report](https://cmscontent.nrs.gov.bc.ca/geoscience/publicationcatalogue/GeoscienceBC/GBCR2007-01-29_Hart.pdf)
- **Temperature, metamorphism and the continuum debate:** the continuum model has deposits forming along crustal faults through a 20-25 km vertical profile at temperatures from <180 °C to >700 °C, synchronous with peak metamorphism from sub-greenschist to lower granulite facies. Work on hypozonal deposits suggests the continuum is not valid across the whole P-T range. — (review summarized in search; [RWTH Aachen thesis](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/957583/files/957583.pdf), secondary; **controversial**). Superior Province (Canada) deposits cluster at mesozonal depths (>6 to <12 km). — [EJM geobarometry paper](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/ejm/detail/8/83503/Geobarometry_in_Archean_lode_gold_deposits)
- **Fluids:** aqueous-carbonic ore fluids with CO2 typically ~5-20 mol%, notable CH4 and/or N2, and H2S ~0.01-0.36 mol%. Composition is consistent from the Paleoarchean to the Tertiary, which suggests a common (metamorphic devolatilization) source. — [Goldfarb & Groves 2015, Lithos 233:2-26 (abstract via Scinapse)](https://scinapse.io/papers/1025143415)
- **Secular distribution:** episodic from the Middle Archean through the Precambrian, continuous through the Phanerozoic. Typically in deformed, metamorphosed mid-crustal blocks near major crustal structures, consistently associated in space and time with granitoids. Phanerozoic economic orogenic gold (including placers) is "just over one billion ounces"; known Precambrian is about half that, excluding Witwatersrand. — [Goldfarb, Groves & Gardoll 2001 (USGS record)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70023618). A Goldfarb talk abstract gives vein-formation peaks at ~3.1, 2.7-2.5, 2.1-1.7 and 0.6-0.0 Ga. It ties the Precambrian pattern to episodic crustal growth and the post-1.7 Ga pattern to modern-style plate tectonics. — [SMEDG 1999 talk abstract](https://smedg.org.au/old_site/oct99.html). Another abstract gives 2.8-2.55 Ga, 2.1-1.8 Ga and 600-50 Ma, tied to the addition of oceanic lithosphere to craton margins during supercontinent growth. — [GSA 2011 abstract](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2011AM/webprogram/Paper194899.html). Frimmel (2018) proposes that most crustal gold was first concentrated at ~2.9 Ga, with the main orogenic peak at 2.75-2.55 Ga. — [Frimmel 2018 accepted manuscript](https://diffusion.mern.gouv.qc.ca/Public/Biblio/Dossiers/PierrePilote/frimmel2018.pdf)
- **Preservation and tectonics:** orogenic gold has "the highest preservation potential of any gold deposit type". Archean subcontinental lithospheric mantle is buoyant, producing equidimensional cratons rimmed by younger linear belts. Plume events were more abundant in the hotter early Earth. Juvenile crust growth was tied to short-lived (<100 m.y.) plume events and supercontinent assembly. — [Groves et al. 2005 (Macquarie record)](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/100th-anniversary-special-paper-secular-changes-in-global-tectoni/) (abstract)
- **USGS low-sulfide Au-quartz veins (36a; "Mother Lode", "mesothermal"):** gold in massive persistent quartz veins in regionally metamorphosed volcanic rocks and volcanic sediments. Hosts are greenstone belts, oceanic metasediments (graywacke, chert, shale), alpine gabbro and serpentinite, and late granitic batholiths. Precambrian to Tertiary. Continental-margin mobile belts and accreted margins. Fault and joint systems produced by regional compression. "Veins age generally post-metamorphic." Carbonate (ankerite, siderite) alteration halos; chromian mica (fuchsite) + dolomite + talc near ultramafics; sericite + arsenopyrite in granitoids. Persistent along regional high-angle faults; best overall in greenstone areas; high-grade shoots at metasediment-serpentinite contacts. Arsenic is the best pathfinder. Weathering gives abundant quartz chips in soil and gold panned from soil. Associated with placer Au-PGE. Examples: Grass Valley, Mother Lode, Ballarat, Nova Scotia goldfields. — [USGS model 36a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull8x2r.htm)
- **Homestake-type (36b; BIF-hosted):** stratabound gold in iron-rich chemical sediments (banded iron formation) in Archean greenstone belts. Commonly near a regional "break" between metavolcanic and metasedimentary domains. Greenschist facies. Origin "controversial": syngenetic exhalative vs. later hydrothermal. Gossans from oxide and carbonate iron formation. — [USGS model 36b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull8hx1.htm)
- **Timing debate:** the continuum review places mineralization synchronous with peak metamorphism, whereas USGS 36a calls veins "generally post-metamorphic". — compare [RWTH thesis](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/957583/files/957583.pdf) and [USGS 36a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull8x2r.htm). **Flag:** consensus in the reviews read is "late-orogenic", but the metamorphic timing of host vs. source is debated.

### Inferences
- **Proposed simulation rule:**
  - *Trigger:* a collision or accretion event (terrane docking, arc-continent collision, or a continent-continent collision's late stage) in which the cell's crust undergoes prograde metamorphism to greenschist-amphibolite. The fluid source is devolatilization of hydrated volcanic/sedimentary sequences (greenstones, turbidites) at depth.
  - *Timing:* mineralization in a window from ~peak metamorphism to ~10-20 Myr after (late-orogenic, during the switch from compression to transpression; inferred). Episodic in the Archean-Paleoproterozoic sim when accretionary orogens are frequent.
  - *Gates:* the cell lies within ~5-20 km of a crustal-scale shear zone or suture. The sim tracks plate boundaries, so place "crustal-scale faults" along former sutures and transcurrent boundaries. The column contains mafic volcanic or turbidite (slate belt) sequences.
  - *Host preferences (weights):* greenstone belts (mafic-ultramafic volcanics, especially with BIF or Fe-rich tholeiites, which act as chemical traps), turbidite slate belts (Victoria, Meguma), and competent granitoid contacts.
  - *Geometry:* deposits along second- and third-order splays spaced ~5-20 km along a major shear zone (inferred; spacing not sourced). Each deposit is a vein set or shear zone 0.1-2 km long and 1-20 m wide, with ore shoots plunging 1-2+ km down-dip.
  - *Depth:* choose the depth class from the metamorphic grade of the host at formation time: greenschist → mesozonal 6-12 km, sub-greenschist → epizonal <6 km (Sb-Hg-rich), amphibolite → hypozonal >12 km.
  - *Exposure:* because formation is deep and vertically extensive (≥2 km), deposits are exposed in deeply eroded old cratons and survive billions of years. Model them as a 2-5 km tall ore column so they appear in-window across a wide range of E.
  - *Abundance:* make orogenic gold the main gold source on old cratons. The Phanerozoic endowment is about twice the Precambrian (Goldfarb 2001), which includes placers.
- **Placers:** orogenic gold is the main source of placer gold (USGS 36a lists placer Au-PGE as associated). When E exceeds the ore column top, spawn alluvial placers in downstream drainage cells (cross-ref placer researcher).
- **Block mapping:** **native gold** (TFC) in quartz veins; vanilla gold ore. Accessory **pyrite** (TFC gem), and arsenopyrite and stibnite with no block. Epizonal variants carry **cinnabar** (TFC/redstone) as a rare accessory (Hg-Sb association; inferred from the shallow end of the continuum and USGS 27d associations). Homestake-type → native gold plus **magnetite**/**hematite** from the BIF host.

### Gaps
- The cause of the ~1.7-0.6 Ga paucity is not explained in sources read. The cited abstracts imply it by listing peaks that skip this interval. It is commonly attributed in the literature to preservation and supercontinent tectonics (Groves et al. 2005, full text not read).
- I found no source with numerical spacing of orogenic gold deposits along shear zones or typical deposit dimensions.
- Grade and tonnage numbers for orogenic gold (USGS 36a GT page) are in figures only.

## 10. Intrusion-related gold systems (IRGS; reduced, Tintina-type)

### Takeaway
IRGS are gold-only systems with direct spatial and temporal links to reduced (ilmenite-series) intermediate to felsic intrusions. They are typically emplaced in deformed shelf sequences inboard of accreted terranes. They carry a Bi-Te-W-As-Sb-Mo signature and low-salinity CO2-rich fluids, so they overlap strongly with orogenic gold. Sheeted quartz vein arrays give bulk-tonnage, low-grade open-pit deposits, and coarse gold feeds placers. They are distinguished from oxidized porphyry Cu-Au.

### Cited Findings
- IRGS are gold-only deposits with direct spatial and temporal links to intermediate-felsic intrusions. The Tintina Gold Belt (Alaska-Yukon) is the reference province. Thompson & Newberry (2000) defined "reduced IRGS" to distinguish them from oxidized porphyry-associated magmas. In Hart's (2005) scheme, oxidized I-type intrusions go with porphyry Cu, while reduced granitoids go with Sn-W and Bi-Te-As-Sb-Mo-Au. — [Tasmanian exploration review](https://mrt.stgro-2780.saas.squiz.cloud/mrtdoc/tasxplor/download/13_6650/EL362008_201305_03_Review.pdf); [JCU record: "Intrusion-related gold systems: the present level of understanding"](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/995/) (secondary)
- Reduced IRGS can resemble orogenic gold: both have anomalous Bi, W and Te, low-salinity CO2-rich fluids, and igneous associations. The best discriminators include regional setting, such as deformed shelf sequences on the inboard side of accreted terranes. Classification of convergent-margin gold is "controversial". — [AusIMM 2005 paper](https://www.ausimm.com/publications/conference-proceedings/2005-ausimm-new-zealand-branch-annual-conference/distinguishing-intrusion-related-from-orogenic-gold-systems/) (abstract)
- Sheeted vein systems allow bulk-tonnage, low-grade open-pit deposits. They typically host coarse gold that forms placers when eroded. Sn and W can occur in the same setting. — [Nasdaq/INN explainer](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/what-are-intrusion-related-gold-systems-updated-2025) (secondary, industry)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rule:** in a back-arc or inboard continental margin with thick sedimentary (shelf/turbidite) cover in the column, during late- to post-collisional or post-accretion magmatism of reduced granitoids, spawn IRGS at pluton cupolas. Gold sits in sheeted veins within and above the cupola, at 2-6 km depth (inferred; depth not sourced). Zoning runs outward from W-Bi-Au in the pluton to As-Au and Sb-Au distally (inferred from the Bi-Te-As-Sb signature). Pair them spatially with Sn-W (Section 14) and orogenic gold in the same belt.
- **Block mapping:** **native gold**, **bismuthinite** (TFC; Bi is part of the signature), **cassiterite** (minor, where Sn-W overlaps), and placers downstream.

### Gaps
- Hart (2005) and Thompson et al. (1999) could not be read. Quantitative depth, temperature, size and grade data for IRGS are missing. Fort Knox, Pogo and Donlin are commonly cited examples but are not sourced here.

## 11. Carlin-type sediment-hosted disseminated gold

### Takeaway
Carlin-type gold is "invisible" Au in arsenian pyrite, replacing silty carbonaceous carbonates. It forms at <3 km and 180-240 °C from low-salinity CO2-bearing fluids. In Nevada (>6,000 t Au, the second-largest Au concentration on Earth) it formed in a narrow 42-34 Ma window, at the switch from flat-slab compression to extension and renewed magmatism. It is localized along reactivated Proterozoic rift structures beneath a regional thrust. The magmatic vs. amagmatic origin is debated. The type needs an unusual combination of passive-margin carbonate stratigraphy, an old rifted craton margin, and a slab-removal trigger.

### Cited Findings
- **Nevada characteristics (Muntean et al. 2011, Table 1):**
  - Formed 42-34 Ma, corresponding to a change from compression to extension and renewed magmatism.
  - Clusters along old, reactivated basement rift structures, preferentially in carbonate-bearing rocks in the lower plate of a regional thrust.
  - Alteration is carbonate dissolution and silicification, sulfidation of Fe, Au-bearing arsenian pyrite, and late orpiment, realgar and stibnite. Signature Au-Tl-As-Hg-Sb-(Te) with low Ag and base metals.
  - Non-boiling fluids at 180-240 °C, mostly ~6 wt% NaCl eq., CO2 <4 mol%.
  - Formation depth <3 km. No zoning over 5-10 km laterally or <2 km vertically. No coeval porphyry Cu, skarn or distal Ag-Pb-Zn.
  - The deposits contain >6,000 t Au, the "second largest concentration of Au in the world", and account for ~6% of annual world production. The four largest clusters (Carlin trend, Cortez, Getchell, Jerritt Canyon) hold >90% of Carlin-type Au worldwide.
  - Hosts are lower Paleozoic miogeoclinal carbonates. Gold sits in pyrite in solid solution or as submicrometre particles.
  - Model: magma generated by upwelling asthenosphere after shallow subduction, impinging on modified lithospheric mantle, released Au-rich fluid at 10-12 km. It ascended, mixed with meteoric water, and sulfidized carbonate within a few km of the surface. Formation tracked the southwestward sweep of Eocene magmatism.

  — [Muntean, Cline, Simon & Longo 2011, Nature Geoscience](https://nbmg.unr.edu/staff/pdfs/Muntean_Carlin_Gold_Nature_Geoscience_w_SupInfo.pdf)
- **Debate:** "a comprehensive and widely accepted genetic model remains elusive". Competing models invoke meteoric-water convection, epizonal intrusions, or deep metamorphic/magmatic fluids. Cline et al. (2005) propose that removal of the Farallon slab drove deep crustal melting, metamorphism and devolatilization. — [Cline et al. 2005](https://pyrite.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFnevada2007/Readings/General_CTD/Cline2005.pdf) (abstract)
- **Depth examples:** Alligator Ridge formed at ≤300-800 m, with peak fluids ~230 °C, and fluids derived from ≥8 km. — [USGS pub 70025177](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70025177). Post/Betze and Purple Vein formed at minimum ~2.8-3.0 km (lithostatic). — [UNLV thesis](https://digitalscholarship.unlv.edu/thesesdissertations/1421)
- **USGS model 26a:** host is thin-bedded silty or argillaceous carbonaceous limestone or dolomite; the best hosts are carbonate turbidites deposited in somewhat anoxic environments. Jasperoid. Signature Au + As + Hg + W ± Mo, and As + Hg + Sb + Tl. Weathers to light-red, gray or tan oxides and brown jasperoid. Associated with stibnite-barite veins, W-Mo skarn and placer Au. — [USGS model 26a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7m7b.htm)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rule:** very rare and province-scale.
  - *Gates:* (1) the column contains thick silty/carbonaceous carbonate (passive-margin slope facies) of Paleozoic or other age over a rifted old craton margin; (2) that margin was later overthrust (a regional thrust in the column, lower-plate position); (3) a flat-slab episode under the cell ended within the last ~5 Myr, followed by an extension plus magmatism sweep, or slab rollback/removal.
  - *Formation:* deposits cluster along "trends" (inherited rift faults), 4-6 clusters per province, each 10-50 km long (inferred). Bodies sit at 0.3-3 km depth, 100-1,000 m across, stratabound along favorable beds and fault intersections.
  - *Frequency:* at most 1 province per planet per few hundred Myr, given only Nevada is world-class. China has smaller analogues (not researched).
- **Block mapping:** **native gold** (simplification: real gold is invisible in pyrite), **pyrite**, accessory **cinnabar** (redstone). Realgar, orpiment and stibnite have no mapping. Jasperoid could be a decorative red chert block if the mod adds one.

### Gaps
- Chinese Carlin-like deposits (Yunnan-Guizhou-Guangxi) are not covered. One snippet gives depths of 1-3 km.
- The sources read do not settle whether Carlin-type deposits can form in non-Nevada settings at comparable scale.

## 12. Polymetallic Ag-Pb-Zn(-Au) veins and Sn-polymetallic (Bolivian/Potosí) veins

### Takeaway
Polymetallic quartz-carbonate veins with base-metal sulfides, Ag sulfosalts and Au form near clusters of small hypabyssal intrusions in arc belts, often peripheral to porphyry systems. They are zoned from Cu-Au at the center through Zn-Pb-Ag to Mn at the periphery. In supergene zones they become high-grade native and horn silver. The Bolivian Sn-polymetallic (Sn-Ag) variant is multistage telescoped veins around felsic subvolcanic centers (Potosí).

### Cited Findings
- **Polymetallic veins (22c):** quartz-carbonate veins with Au and Ag plus base-metal sulfides (pyrite, sphalerite, chalcopyrite, galena, arsenopyrite, tetrahedrite-tennantite, Ag sulfosalts, argentite) related to hypabyssal intrusions in sedimentary and metamorphic terranes. Most are Mesozoic-Cenozoic. Settings are continental-margin and island-arc volcanic-plutonic belts, especially zones of local domal uplift, near-surface fractures within thermal aureoles of small intrusion clusters, and sometimes peripheral to porphyry systems. Wide propylitic and narrow sericitic/argillic alteration; jasperoid in carbonates. Anomalies zoned Cu-Au outward to Zn-Pb-Ag to Mn. Weathering: minor gossans, Mn-oxide stains, Zn and Pb carbonates. "Supergene enrichment produces high-grade native and horn silver ores in veins where calcite is not abundant." Placer gold. — [USGS model 22c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull7kdv.htm)
- **Sn-polymetallic veins (20b):** multistage Cu-Zn-Sn-Ag veins with felsic ignimbrites and subvolcanic intrusions. Late Cretaceous-Miocene (Japan), Miocene (Bolivia). Continental margin, syn-late orogenic. Metal zoning runs Sn+W to Cu+Sn, Cu+Zn, Pb+Zn, Pb+Ag and Au+Ag from center to periphery or deep to shallow, commonly telescoped. Mineralogy includes cassiterite, stannite, native bismuth, bismuthinite and argentite. Examples: Potosí (Bolivia), Ashio and Ikuno (Japan). Cassiterite in soils and gossans. — [USGS model 20b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9qer.htm)
- Shallow-level (<1 km paleodepth) Zn-Pb-Cu-Ag ± Au intermediate-sulfidation veins occur alongside porphyry deposits. — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Rule:** spawn as satellites of every arc intrusive cluster (porphyry or barren). Use 3-20 veins per district, 0.2-2 km long, at 0-3 km depth, outward to ~5-8 km from the center (consistent with porphyry system radii). In districts over thickened, Sn-fertile basement (Bolivian), substitute the Sn-Ag variant.
- **Block mapping:** **sphalerite**, **tetrahedrite** (genuinely abundant here), **native silver** (especially in supergene zones; strong justification), **native gold** (minor), **bismuthinite** (Sn-polymetallic variant), **cassiterite** (Sn variant). Galena has no TFC block; vanilla has none either.

### Gaps
- Grade and tonnage figures for polymetallic veins (USGS GT page) were not extractable.

## 13. Five-element (Ni-Co-As-Ag-Bi ± U) veins

### Takeaway
Five-element veins host native Ag and Bi (± As) encapsulated by Ni-Co-Fe arsenides in carbonate (± quartz) gangue. Examples are Cobalt (Ontario), Erzgebirge, Schwarzwald, Kongsberg, Sardinia and Black Hawk (New Mexico). The current leading model (Markl et al. 2016, "natural fracking") has a sudden influx of methane or hydrocarbons into a hot, saline, metal-rich basinal fluid. The strong reduction precipitates native metals and then arsenides. They typically form in extensional, post-orogenic settings, in crystalline basement or at basement-cover contacts near organic-rich sediments.

### Cited Findings
- Kissin (1992) is the classic review "Five-element (Ni-Co-As-Ag-Bi) Veins". Uranium, Cu, Pb, Zn, Sb and Hg may also be present, and one or more of the five elements may be missing. — (search-derived; [Sciforum slides](https://sciforum.net/paper/download/9363/slides), secondary)
- Paragenesis is native metals (Ag, Bi, ± As) encapsulated by Fe-Co-Ni arsenides and carbonates. A typical sequence is native elements (Bi ± Au), then Ni-Co arsenides and sulfarsenides in quartz, then Pb-Zn-Cu ± Ag sulfides in siderite. — [Tübingen thesis record](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/82865); [Sciforum slides](https://sciforum.net/paper/download/9363/slides) (secondary)
- **Genesis:** Markl, Burisch & Neumann (2016, Mineralium Deposita 51:703-712), "Natural fracking and the genesis of five-element veins". — [Tübingen record](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/73056). Their model involves rapid influx of methane or other S-poor hydrocarbons, released by natural fracturing, into a highly saline metal-rich fluid. Native metals precipitate where the oxidized ore fluid meets the reduced fluid. — [NMGS Black Hawk abstract](https://nmgs.nmt.edu/meeting/abstracts/view.cfm?aid=2923). Burisch (2016): five-element veins form where strongly reducing methane-bearing fluids enter an active hydrothermal sulfide system. — [Burisch dissertation](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/10900/71965/1/Dissertation_Burisch_2016.pdf). At Punta Corna (Alps), pre-ore methane was consumed during arsenide formation. — [Leoben record](https://pure.unileoben.ac.at/en/publications/methane-and-the-origin-of-five-element-veins-mineralogy-age-fluid/)
- In SW Sardinia (Arburèse), veins follow the Arbus pluton contact, with Pb-Zn in the north and five-element veins in the south. They formed post-289 ± 1 Ma (post-Variscan). Ni-Co source is "still hypothetical"; Bi-Au enrichment suggests remobilization of granite-related W-Bi-Te-Au veins. — [Rendiconti SGI paper](https://rendicontisocietageologicaitaliana.it/fulltext/4206/vein-intersection-zones-and-host-rock-composition-as-controlling-factors-in-polymetallic-ore-shoots-genesis-insights-from-the-southern-arburese-five-element-ni-co-as-bi-ag-vein-system-sw-sardinia)
- At Black Hawk (New Mexico), Mesoproterozoic granite and metamorphic hosts carry fissure veins with native Ag, Bi and As, and Co-Ni arsenides. — [NMGS abstract](https://nmgs.nmt.edu/meeting/abstracts/view.cfm?aid=2943)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rule:** cells with crystalline basement (or a granite contact) overlain by or adjacent to organic-rich shale or hydrocarbon-bearing sedimentary basins, during a later extensional or rifting episode, especially ≥20 Myr after an orogeny when basinal brines circulate into basement. Probability is boosted where the column contains older Ni-Co sources (mafic/ultramafic rocks) and evaporites (brine salinity). Veins are 0.1-1 km long and 0.1-2 m wide, in swarms in a 1-5 km district, at 0.2-2 km depth (inferred).
- **Block mapping:** **native silver** (TFC; the classic "silver sheet" ore, so this is the best TFC silver source besides epithermal/polymetallic veins), **bismuthinite** (TFC; native Bi has no separate block). Ni-Co arsenides: TFC **garnierite** is the only Ni ore, but garnierite is a lateritic Ni silicate. Using it here would be geologically wrong; flag it, and use it only as an optional gameplay compromise. U, Co and As have no mapping.

### Gaps
- Temperatures, salinities and typical tonnages for five-element veins were not obtained. Markl et al. (2016) full text was not accessible.
- The Cobalt-Gowganda (Ontario) setting, with veins associated with Nipissing diabase sills, was not researched. It may need a "diabase sill + Huronian sediments" rule variant.

## 14. Granite-related Sn-W veins, greisens, W veins and pegmatite-adjacent systems

### Takeaway
Sn and W deposits are tied to reduced (ilmenite-series), highly fractionated granites. These are commonly S-type two-mica leucogranites, but also I- or A-type. They are emplaced late- to post-tectonically in crust thickened by collision (Cornwall, Erzgebirge, SE Asia tin belt) or are subvolcanic (Bolivia). Ore concentrates in and above granite cupolas and ridges as greisens, sheeted veins and stockworks (cassiterite, wolframite, scheelite, with topaz, fluorite, tourmaline). Placers form downstream.

### Cited Findings
- Granite-related hydrothermal systems are the main source of Sn and W, with cassiterite, wolframite and scheelite as key ore minerals. Causative intrusions may be S-, I- or A-type, but are reduced (ilmenite series) and highly evolved (high SiO2, high Rb/Sr). Deposition happens by fluid mixing, boiling and water-rock interaction. — [Geoscience Australia overview](https://ecat.ga.gov.au/geonetwork/srv/api/records/20b34f0f-18c2-4980-9e25-9dd491761e7b)
- Mineralization (Europe, Malaysia, southern Africa) is closely associated in space and time with S-type ilmenite-series granitoids, related to late- to post-tectonic granites emplaced into substantially thickened crust. Ore sits near apical protrusions of the youngest, most differentiated granite. Styles include endogranitic veins, disseminations, pegmatites and pipes, and exogranitic stockworks, fissure veins and replacements. — [Rhodes University MSc review (1985)](https://researchrepository.ru.ac.za/handle/123456789/5245)
- In Hercynian (Variscan) S-type granites of Portugal, Sn rises with the degree of fractional crystallization. Greisenization raises SiO2, H2O, Sn, W, Nb, Ta, Rb, Zn and Pb. — [Neiva 2002](https://www.geologinenseura.fi/sites/geologinenseura.fi/files/nieva.pdf)
- **Sn veins (15b, Cornish type):** quartz-cassiterite ± wolframite with base-metal fringes near multiphase, specialized leucogranites, commonly with pelitic sediments. Paleozoic-Mesozoic most common. Foldbelts and accreted margins with late- to post-orogenic, partly anatectic granitoids. Ore lies in or above the apices of granitic cusps and ridges. Placer tin. Pathfinders Sn, As, W, B (+ F, Rb, Be, Nb, Cs, U). — [USGS model 15b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull89rn.htm)
- **Sn greisen (15c):** disseminated cassiterite and veinlet stockworks in greisenized specialized S-type leucogranite with topaz, fluorite, tourmaline and beryl, in mesozonal plutonic to deep volcanic environments. Foldbelts of thick sediments on cratons; granitoids postdate major folding. Zonal assemblages include cassiterite-molybdenite, wolframite-beryl-arsenopyrite-bismuthinite, and Cu-Pb-Zn sulfides. Greisen alteration is quartz-muscovite-topaz-fluorite ± tourmaline. Lodes are in or near cupolas and ridges. Examples: Altenberg, Cinovec (Erzgebirge), East Kemptville. — [USGS model 15c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull217y.htm); [Sn greisen GT deposit list](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull22pa.htm)
- **W veins (15a):** wolframite, molybdenite and minor base-metal sulfides in quartz veins in tensional fractures of epizonal granitic plutons and wallrocks. Settings are belts of plutons derived from remelting of continental crust, with country rocks at greenschist facies. Paleozoic to late Tertiary. Wolframite persists in soils and stream sediments. Examples: Pasto Bueno, Xihuashan, Chicote Grande. — [USGS model 15a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull8pv7.htm)
- Lehmann (2021, Lithos 402-403:105756) "Formation of tin ore deposits: A reassessment" exists, but its content was not retrieved. — [citation](https://journal.hep.com.cn/cg/EN/10.31035/cg2022066)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rule:** at continent-continent collision sites (and thick accretionary orogens), 10-40 Myr after peak crustal thickening (inferred from "late- to post-tectonic" and post-collisional anatexis; quantitative lag not sourced), spawn S-type leucogranite plutons where the column contains thick pelitic metasediments (the melting source). For each pluton, roll for fertility (weight by fractionation proxy: pluton age within the post-collisional window, high crust thickness). Fertile plutons get:
  - greisen bodies in the cupola top (0-300 m below the granite roof);
  - sheeted Sn-W veins in the roof zone (up to ~1-2 km above the roof);
  - Sn skarn where carbonate is present (Section 4);
  - downstream placers when eroded.

  Depth of the granite roof is 2-6 km (inferred mesozonal). Ore is exposed when E brings the roof into the window. Cluster plutons along the orogen in belts hundreds of km long (SE Asia tin belt analogue).
- **Block mapping:** **cassiterite** (TFC), **bismuthinite** (TFC; in greisen and W veins), **topaz** (TFC gem; greisen and topaz rhyolite), minor **native silver**/**sphalerite**/**tetrahedrite** in fringes. Wolframite and scheelite have no mapping. Fluorite, tourmaline and beryl have no mapping (beryl could optionally feed emerald only if Cr is present; normally not).
- **Cryolite note:** TFC has a cryolite block. The only major natural cryolite deposit (Ivigtut, Greenland) is in a highly evolved granite stock (pegmatite/greisen-like). Assign it to an ultra-rare "evolved A-type granite cupola" variant here or in the pegmatite researcher's rules. This is not sourced in this research.

### Gaps
- No secular distribution of Sn deposits was retrieved (Lehmann 2021 not read). Tin is often said to be enriched in Phanerozoic collisional belts, but this is not verified here.
- Pegmatite-hosted Sn-Ta-Li is out of scope (magmatic; other researcher).

## 15. Hydrothermal fluorite, barite and quartz veins; hydrothermal (vein-type) uranium (brief)

### Takeaway
Fluorite-barite(-quartz) veins form at ~120-180 °C (fluid inclusions) by mixing deep, highly saline (22-25 wt% NaCl eq.) basement brines with meteoric water. This happens commonly during post-orogenic continental extension (for example the Mesozoic opening of the Atlantic/Tethys in the Schwarzwald), in basement just below sedimentary cover. Fluorite also accompanies Climax-Mo and alkalic Au-Te systems, and fluorite plus U accompany volcanogenic U in rift calderas.

### Cited Findings
- Schwarzwald post-Variscan fluorite-barite-quartz veins precipitated through mixing of deep saline brine with meteoric low-salinity water, supported by calcite C-O isotope covariation. The brine was a basement-derived NaCl-CaCl2 brine. Fluid inclusion Th is 130-180 °C at 22-25 wt% NaCl eq.; quartz precipitated at 120-170 °C and 0.3-0.5 kbar. **Conflict:** sulfide-sulfate S-isotope temperatures give 300-350 °C. — (search-derived summaries of [Schwarzwald studies, U. Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/40569) and [geologyscience repository](https://repository.geologyscience.ru/handle/123456789/46602); secondary, unreconciled)
- Mesozoic F-Ba mineralization is linked to major extensional events during the gradual opening of the Atlantic and Tethys (Mitchell & Halliday 1976 first identified Mesozoic hydrothermal activity related to North Atlantic opening). — same secondary sources
- In the Harz Mountains, barite deposited by mixing of a metamorphic fluid with descending SO4-bearing fluids from residual evaporites of the Mesozoic cover. — [EJM Harz paper](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/ejm/detail/4/83920/Fluid_evolution_and_ore_deposition_in_the_Harz_Mountains_Germany) (abstract)
- Climax Mo systems are associated with Ag-base-metal veins and fluorspar deposits. — [USGS model 16](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull56zz.htm)
- **Volcanogenic U (25f):** uraninite, coffinite and brannerite in epithermal quartz-fluorite-sulfide veins and breccias in high-silica alkali rhyolite and potash trachyte. Continental rifts and associated calderas. Precambrian to Tertiary. Mo is deep, Hg shallow. Associated with fluorite deposits. Example: Marysvale (Utah). — [USGS model 25f](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2shh.htm)
- Five-element veins may contain U (Section 13).

### Inferences
- **Rule:** post-orogenic extension (≥30 Myr after a collision; rifting or basin formation over old basement) in cells with crystalline basement under a sedimentary cover with evaporites. Veins are 0.5-5 km long and 1-10 m wide, at 0.2-2 km below the basement-cover unconformity (inferred). Fluorite and barite have no TFC block. **Quartz → vanilla quartz ore** if the mod chooses to place nether-quartz-like ore in the overworld (TFC has no quartz ore block in the given list). Hydrothermal quartz veins are ubiquitous in nearly every type above (orogenic gold, epithermal, Sn-W), so vanilla quartz could be used as "vein gangue" filler.
- **Barite → TFC gypsum?** Not geologically equivalent (sulfates, but barite is BaSO4). Not recommended.
- **U:** no mapping.

### Gaps
- No primary source on global fluorite/barite vein abundance or secular distribution was read. MVT-related fluorite (Illinois-Kentucky) is out of scope (sedimentary-basin researcher).
- Vein-type U (Příbram, Jáchymov, Schwartzwalder) and unconformity U (Athabasca) were not researched. Unconformity U belongs with sedimentary-basin processes.

## 16. Colombian-type (black-shale, evaporite-brine) emerald and other hydrothermal gems (amethyst geodes, topaz, opal, pyrite)

### Takeaway
Colombian emeralds are unique non-igneous hydrothermal gems. Hot (~300 °C) evaporite-derived brines leached Be, Cr and V from Lower Cretaceous black shales and deposited emerald in carbonate-albite-pyrite veins and breccias. Thermochemical sulfate reduction was the key chemical step. Deposition happened at thrust and tear-fault zones during Andean tectonics, at ~65 Ma (eastern belt, extensional) and ~38-32 Ma (western belt, Muzo, compressional). Amethyst geodes in flood basalts (Paraná-Etendeka) formed at low temperature (~15-60 °C) from aquifer groundwater, not from the lava itself. Topaz comes from greisens and topaz rhyolites, opal from sinters and hot-spring water tables, and pyrite is ubiquitous.

### Cited Findings
- **Emerald model:** hydrothermal brines transported evaporitic sulfate to structurally favorable sites, where thermochemical reduction released Cr, V and Be from organic-rich shales. This enabled emerald formation without igneous activity. — [Nature 1994 (Muzo)](https://www.nature.com/articles/369552a0) (abstract). Brines formed at ~300 °C by dissolution of evaporites by hot basinal waters that leached Be, Cr and V from black shales. — (Giuliani et al., search summary; [IRD documentation](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/pleins_textes_6/b_fdi_35-36/42470.pdf), secondary). Fluid accumulation at fault tips in black shales led to overpressure, sudden decompression, and emerald veins (trapiche emerald work). — same secondary source. **Controversy:** P-T conditions from fluid inclusions are still debated, and Be extraction from shales is "not yet proved".
- **Ages and belts:** western belt (Muzo, Coscuez, La Pita, Peñas Blancas) at ~33 Ma in a compressive setting; eastern belt (Chivor, Gachalá, Macanal) at ~65 Ma in an extensional setting (Branquet et al. 1999). — [GIA 2017](https://www.gia.edu/articles/fall-2017-colombian-emerald-industry). Chivor-Macanal is 65 ± 3 Ma (K-Ar, Ar-Ar). Muzo-Coscuez is 38-32 Ma (Cheilletz et al. 1994). — [IRD dating paper](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/divers09-03/010022674.pdf) (secondary). Hosts are Lower Cretaceous (Valanginian-Hauterivian) black shales interbedded with dolomitic limestones; deposits are linked by tear faults and thrusts. — [IUGS Muzo geoheritage](https://iugs-geoheritage.org/geoheritage_sites/the-muzo-emerald-deposit/)
- **USGS emerald veins (31c):** emerald in plagioclase (albite-oligoclase)-dolomite veins in black shale. Cretaceous-Tertiary. Thick epicontinental anoxic marine shale; evaporites may have provided saline solutions. Major faults at intersections with minor cross faults. — [USGS model 31c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull51tf.htm)
- **Amethyst geodes (Paraná basalts):** two interpretations are hot residual lava fluids vs. groundwater from underlying aquifers (Gilg et al. 2003). Later work favors aquifer-driven (Guarani/Botucatu) low-temperature infill. Crystallization is ~50 °C (Juchem 1999), and 15-60 °C at Los Catalanes (Uruguay). — [Mineralium Deposita 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00126-024-01310-2); [Brazilian J. Geology](https://www.scielo.br/j/bjgeo/a/mMQ3WJHyNcyMDV44SMLPBqM/?lang=en); [Ametista do Sul fluid inclusion study](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00126-014-0522-7) (abstracts)
- **Topaz:** greisen alteration is quartz-muscovite-topaz-fluorite ± tourmaline. — [USGS 15c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull217y.htm). Topaz is an accessory in Climax-type and topaz-rhyolite-hosted Sn systems. — [USGS 16](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull56zz.htm); [USGS 25h](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull2nn2.htm)
- **Opal:** opal deposited at the paleo water table in hot-spring Hg systems. — [USGS 27a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4von.htm). Chalcedonic sinter and some opal veins occur in hot-spring Au-Ag. — [USGS 25a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0g4n.htm)

### Inferences
- **Emerald rule:** cells with Cretaceous-type (any age) black-shale plus evaporite basin fill, later inverted (thrusting) or rifted with fault-driven brine flow at ~250-350 °C. That means burial to ~6-10 km in the stratigraphic column, or a high-heat-flow event (inferred from 300 °C). Oxygenated-ocean evaporitic sulfate is needed (post-GOE; inferred). Veins in fault intersection zones, 10-100 m districts. Extremely rare: 1-2 provinces per planet. The normal emerald source is pegmatite/schist contacts (Be from granite pegmatite plus Cr from ultramafic rocks), which is out of scope here and should be covered by the pegmatite/metamorphic researcher.
- **Amethyst rule:** flood-basalt (LIP) cells overlying a regional sandstone aquifer. Geodes form in the upper vesicular parts of thick lava flows, any time after eruption while the aquifer is active. Place amethyst geode clusters in basalt within 0-300 m of the original flow tops.
- **Block mapping:** emerald → **TFC emerald** / vanilla emerald (vanilla's mountain-only emerald could be replaced by this rule plus the pegmatite rule). Amethyst → **TFC amethyst** / vanilla amethyst geode (vanilla's geode structure fits LIP basalts well). Topaz → **TFC topaz** in greisen. Opal → **TFC opal** in sinter and hot-spring Hg (precious opal from weathering in sedimentary basins is another researcher's topic). Pyrite → **TFC pyrite** in nearly all sulfide systems (VMS, porphyry halo, orogenic, Carlin). Ruby and sapphire are not hydrothermal (metamorphic/basalt-xenocryst); diamond is not hydrothermal.

### Gaps
- Precise Colombian emerald formation temperatures and depths are debated and not resolved here.
- Gilg et al. (2003) primary temperatures were not retrieved.

## 17. Native copper in flood basalts (Keweenaw / "basaltic Cu")

### Takeaway
Keweenaw-type native copper (with native silver) fills amygdules and flow-top breccias in thick subaerial rift basalts and interflow conglomerates. Production came ~58.5% from amygdaloidal flow tops, ~39.5% from conglomerates and ~2% from fissures. It formed ~1,060-1,047 Ma (±20 Myr), tens of Myr after the rift volcanism, after deposition of the overlying sediments. This is consistent with burial-metamorphic fluids expelled from the deep rift fill. Native copper rather than sulfide reflects very low sulfur in the basalt-hosted fluids (not verified in sources). The USGS model generalizes it to "basaltic Cu" in intracontinental and continental-margin rifts.

### Cited Findings
- **USGS basaltic Cu (23):** disseminated native copper and Cu sulfides in the upper parts of thick sequences of subaerial basalt. Cu sulfides (chalcocite, bornite, chalcopyrite) in overlying sedimentary beds. Host basalts are Cu-rich (100-200 ppm), interlayered with red clastics and overlain by shallow-marine/continental deposits formed near the paleo-equator. Settings are intracontinental and continental-margin rifts. "Regional low-grade metamorphism may mobilize copper in some districts." Ages: Proterozoic, Triassic-Jurassic, Tertiary. Calcite-zeolite + epidote + K-feldspar alteration; red hematite coloration. Ore controls are flow-top breccias, amygdules and fractures. Weathering leaves "widely dispersed copper nuggets in streams draining basalts". Signature Cu-Ag-Zn-Cd with very high Cu:Zn and anomalously low Au. Examples: Keweenaw, Kennecott (Alaska, Wrangellia Triassic), Boleo, Sustut. — [USGS model 23](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull1bcl.htm)
- **Keweenaw timing:** Rb-Sr dating of amygdule minerals gives mineralization ages of 1,060-1,047 Ma (±20 Myr), postdating deposition of most or all of the overlying Freda Sandstone. Variable initial Sr ratios suggest mixing of isotopically distinct fluid sources (Bornhorst et al. 1988, Econ. Geol. 83:619-625). — [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70206764)
- **Keweenaw hosts:** brecciated and amygdaloidal basalt flow tops (58.5% of production), interflow conglomerates (39.5%) and cross-vein fissures (2%). The Calumet & Hecla Conglomerate is the largest single native copper lode in the world. The district spans ~110 km of strike. — (search-derived from [MTU blog on Bornhorst 2017](https://blogs.mtu.edu/geo/2017/10/16/bornhorst-publishes-on-the-native-copper-district/) and [A.E. Seaman Museum publication](https://museum.mtu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-11/ASEMM_Web_Pub_2_Lake_Copper_2017.pdf); secondary, exact page not verified). Cu-isotope constraints on genesis are in Bornhorst & Mathur (2017, Minerals 7:185). — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-163X/7/10/185) (not readable: 403)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rule:** LIP or continental-rift basalt piles >~2-5 km thick (inferred; Keweenaw rift fill is very thick) with interbedded conglomerates and red beds. Mineralization 10-50 Myr after volcanism, when deep burial of the rift fill (≥5-10 km in the stratigraphic column, reaching zeolite-prehnite-pumpellyite to greenschist) drives fluids updip through permeable flow tops toward rift margins or inversion structures (inferred from the "down-dip/updip" hint and metamorphic mobilization in USGS 23). Ore is stratabound along specific flow tops and conglomerates, 1-30 km strike length per lode, within the top 0-2 km of the then-surface (inferred). Weathering yields copper nuggets in streams (placer-like native copper).
- **Block mapping:** **TFC native copper** (excellent fit; arguably TFC's best-justified real analogue) plus sparse **native silver** ("half-breeds"). Overlying redbed/shale Cu sulfides → **tetrahedrite**/**malachite** (sediment-hosted Cu is mainly another researcher's topic). Vanilla: copper ore. The same LIP cells also host amethyst/agate geodes (Section 16).

### Gaps
- Keweenaw host-lava age (~1.1 Ga), fluid temperature and the burial-metamorphic model were not directly confirmed in fetched text (MDPI and mindat pages returned 403).

## 18. Native sulfur and pyrite from hydrothermal systems (brief)

### Takeaway
Hydrothermal native sulfur forms at the acid, oxidized tops of volcanic-geothermal systems: steam-heated zones above the paleo water table, lithocaps, and late HS stages. Pyrite is the most widespread hydrothermal sulfide in essentially every type above. Large economic sulfur (salt-dome/evaporite biogenic) is a sedimentary topic.

### Cited Findings
- Native sulfur occurs with kaolinite, alunite and Fe oxides above the paleo water table in hot-spring Hg systems. — [USGS 27a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4von.htm)
- Late-stage native sulfur occurs in epithermal quartz-alunite Au deposits. — [USGS 25e](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0vxx.htm)
- At Sulphur Bank, native sulfur is generated by bacterial reduction of sulfate. — [Northern Miner](https://northernminer.com/news/americas-reclamation-of-california-mercury-deposits-a/1000137723) (secondary)
- Pyrite: massive pyrite dominates Cyprus and Kuroko VMS — [USGS 24a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull15r9.htm), [USGS 28a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull0bfp.htm). Pyrite halos surround porphyry deposits — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). Au-bearing arsenian pyrite is the Carlin ore mineral — [Muntean 2011](https://nbmg.unr.edu/staff/pdfs/Muntean_Carlin_Gold_Nature_Geoscience_w_SupInfo.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Rule:** every active or recently extinct (<~5 Myr; inferred) volcanic or geothermal cell (arc stratovolcanoes, rift volcanoes, hotspots) gets sulfur at 0-100 m in fumarole and solfatara patches, and in lithocap/steam-heated blankets of porphyry-epithermal systems. Erodes away quickly. Pyrite: add as a common gangue phase (5-20% of sulfide-system ore blocks) everywhere sulfides are placed.
- **Block mapping:** **TFC sulfur**; **TFC pyrite** (gem).

### Gaps
- Volcanic sulfur deposit (fumarolic, Andean volcano-hosted) tonnage and statistics were not researched.

## 19. Summary: simulation inputs and Minecraft/TFC block mapping by deposit type

### Takeaway
The deposit families fall into a small set of tectonic triggers: arc magmatism; post-collisional or post-subduction extension; collision and metamorphism; seafloor spreading and arc rifting; LIP/plume and continental rift; basinal brine flow. They are modulated by stratigraphic-column ingredients (carbonate, black shale, evaporites, pelites, basalt, BIF) and filtered by formation depth vs. exhumation. TFC's ore list covers Cu (native copper, malachite, tetrahedrite), Au, Ag, Sn, Bi, Zn, Fe, Hg (cinnabar → redstone), S and several gems well. Mo, W, Pb, Sb, U, Co, As, F and Ba have no blocks.

### Cited Findings
- Depth windows used in the table: porphyry systems span the upper ~4 km with HS disseminated Au in the top ~500 m and lithocaps >1 km thick — [Sillitoe 2010](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf). Porphyry Cu-Au emplacement 1-2 km — [USGS 20c](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull5m0l.htm). Skarns <4 km and Carlin <3 km — [Muntean 2011](https://nbmg.unr.edu/staff/pdfs/Muntean_Carlin_Gold_Nature_Geoscience_w_SupInfo.pdf). Epithermal from surface to ~1-2 km — [White & Hedenquist 1995](https://www.literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Epithermal%20gold%20deposits%20Styles%20characteristics%20and%20exploration.pdf). Hot-spring Hg <300 m and silica-carbonate Hg ≤1,000 m — [USGS/Smith et al. 2008](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/isotope-geochemistry-mercury-source-rocks-mineral-deposits-and-spring-deposits). Orogenic Au epizonal <6, mesozonal 6-12, hypozonal >12 km — [Groves et al. 1998](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70021148). Porphyry Sn 1-3 km — [USGS 20a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull9jce.htm). IOCG >10 km to the paleosurface — [Wikipedia IOCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide_copper_gold_ore_deposits). VMS at the seafloor — [USGS SIR 2010-5070-C](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105070C).
