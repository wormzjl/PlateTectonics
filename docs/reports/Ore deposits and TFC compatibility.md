# Simulate ore systems, then emit TerraFirmaCraft blocks

**Bottom line.** Every economic ore, gem, industrial-mineral, coal and petroleum deposit can come from **eight generator families** that run on events the PlateTectonics simulation already produces: subduction segments, collisions, rifts, plumes and LIPs, basin subsidence, uplift and erosion. Each candidate is then filtered by about a dozen **global time gates**, such as oxygenation, mantle temperature, land plants and plankton. Finally a **preservation test** compares the deposit's formation depth with the cumulative exhumation of its cell. The preservation test is not optional. About **62% of the 125,895 porphyry copper deposits formed in the Phanerozoic** have already been eroded, and only **574 of the 47,789 that remain** are exposed ([Wikipedia citing Kesler & Wilkinson 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_copper_deposit)). TerraFirmaCraft (TFC) **v4.2.12 for 1.21.1** can be supported by default without forking or mixing into it. The mod should provide its own `ChunkGenerator implements ChunkGeneratorExtension` plus a `ChunkDataGenerator` whose `generateRock` answers from the simulated stratigraphic column. TFC's climate model, seasons, crops, prospecting and ore blocks then work unchanged ([ChunkGeneratorExtension.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/ChunkGeneratorExtension.java); [KubeJS-TFC precedent](https://github.com/Notenoughmail/KubeJS-TFC/blob/1.21.1/src/main/java/io/github/notenoughmail/kubejstfc/worldgen/generator/WrappedChunkGenerator.java)). TFC's 33 ores cover iron, copper, tin, zinc, bismuth, nickel, gold and silver, plus coal, evaporites, sulfur, graphite and nine gems. They have **no block for lead, chalcopyrite, molybdenum, tungsten, chromium, PGE, uranium, manganese, aluminium, lithium, REE, phosphate, fluorite, barite or petroleum**, and simulated lithologies must collapse onto **21 rocks with no ultramafic rock** ([Ore.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/blocks/rock/Ore.java); [Rock.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/blocks/rock/Rock.java)). Four decisions matter most before implementation: the block scale and the separate render scale for orebodies; which planetary epoch t = 0 represents; whether TFC's own veins are switched off; and how redstone (cinnabar) and tin supply are guaranteed for TFC progression.

Scope and evidence. This report synthesizes eight research notes covering the deposit framework, magmatic, hydrothermal, sedimentary, weathering/metamorphic/gem, coal and petroleum deposits, plus TFC architecture and TFC ore content. TFC facts were read from branch `1.21.x` at commit `c3b4d953` (2026-10-14), which is identical to release v4.2.12 ([TFC releases](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/releases)). Simulation rules marked "design" are proposals derived from the cited science, not published algorithms. No published system generates ore deposits from a full synthetic plate history. The closest analogues are EarthByte's machine-learning studies on real reconstructions and Kesler & Wilkinson's diffusion model ([EarthByte](https://www.earthbyte.org/predicting-the-emplacement-of-cordilleran-porphyry-copper-systems-using-a-spatio-temporal-machine-learning-model/)).

## Fifteen tectonic settings host every major deposit class

The mineral-systems approach treats a deposit as the coincidence of a fertile source, a trigger, a fluid pathway with a trap, and later preservation. If any one is missing, nothing forms ([McCuaig et al. 2010](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/translating-the-mineral-systems-approach-into-an-effective-explor/); [GSWA mineral systems atlas](https://dmp.wa.gov.au/msa/mineral-systems-816.aspx)). Groves et al. reframe it as geodynamics, fertility, architecture and preservation. They argue that disparate deposit classes occupy equivalent tectonic niches ([Groves et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/4686208e77284388af35259ae9bd0fc7)). This maps directly onto a simulation. The plate model supplies geodynamics. The stratigraphic column supplies fertility and architecture (host rocks, seals, redox boundaries). The burial and exhumation history supplies preservation. Games such as TFC, Dwarf Fortress, Geolosys and Vintage Story use only host rock, depth window, shape template, rarity and a surface indicator, with no tectonic history or time ([TFC constants.py](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/resources/constants.py); [DF material tokens](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Inorganic_material_definition_token); [Geolosys](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/geolosys/files/2823995); [Vintage Story prospecting](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Prospecting_Pick)). Rock gating alone gives believable local results but not believable regional patterns, and regional patterns are the mod's advantage.

A few deposit types dominate each commodity, and size within a type is extremely skewed. These numbers are the validation targets. **Copper** comes about 60% from porphyries (about 65% of resources) and about 20% from sediment-hosted deposits ([USGS FS 2014-3004](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2014/3004/pdf/fs2014-3004.pdf); [USGS SIR 2010-5070-B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105070B)). Whole porphyry systems, including skarns and epithermal deposits, supply nearly three-quarters ([S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)). Hitzman gives 23% for sediment-hosted Cu ([Hitzman et al. 2005](https://eprints.utas.edu.au/705/)). **Gold** is about 30% orogenic, 30% Witwatersrand, 16% porphyry and 12% epithermal ([Frimmel abstract](https://information.americangeosciences.org/open-collections/igc/3075)). Witwatersrand's ~53,000 t is about one-third of all gold ever mined ([Frimmel 2019](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/The%20Witwatersrand%20Basin%20and%20Its%20Gold%20Deposits.pdf)); the ~40% figure in other reviews is looser ([Frimmel et al.](https://www.literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Controversies%20on%20the%20Origin%20of%20World-Class%20Gold%20Deposits%20Part%20II%20Witwatersrand%20Gold%20Deposits.pdf)). **Lead-zinc** is about half sediment-hosted, and nine of the ten largest deposits are SEDEX ([Leach et al. 2005](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70146125); [Mudd et al. 2017](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/geo_fac_articles/208)). **Molybdenum** sources conflict: BGS says porphyries supply 99% of Mo, Sillitoe says porphyry Cu systems supply half. The difference is probably whether Climax-type Mo porphyries are counted ([BGS07](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/8724/1/0719_Copper_profile.pdf)). Within a type, the **top 10% of deposits hold over 86% of Au, 84% of Cu, 71% of Zn and 73% of Pb** ([Singer 1995](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197194)). The median porphyry is 250 Mt at 0.39% Cu ([Hammarstrom 2017](https://www.segweb.org/SEG/Events/Conference-Archive/2017/Conference-Proceedings/files/topics/..%5Cpdf%5CPoster-Presentations%5CAbstracts%5CP089-Hammarstrom.pdf)). Porphyry density is 2–128 per 100,000 km² of permissive terrane, median 15 ([Singer 2005](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70197200)). Rarity follows the concentration factor over crustal abundance: about 4 for Al and Fe, 80 for Cu, 570 for Zn, and about 3,000 for Pb, Cr and Sn ([LibreTexts](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geology/Mineralogy_(Perkins_et_al.)/09%3A_Ore_Deposits_and_Economic_Minerals/9.01%3A_Mineral_Commodities/9.1.04%3A_Natural_Abundances_of_Elements)). That is why tin, which TFC needs for bronze, is the commodity most likely to be missing from a realistic world.

## About a dozen global switches gate deposits by planetary age

Deposit ages are episodic. Plate-margin deposits peak during supercontinent assembly at about 2.7–2.5, 1.9–1.8, 0.6–0.45 and 0.35–0.25 Ga. This is partly real and partly preservation bias, because collision encases deposits inside continents ([C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)). The supercontinent intervals used are Superia 2.8–2.6, Sclavia 2.55–2.40, Nuna 2.1–1.7, Rodinia 1.25–0.95, Gondwana 0.65–0.45 and Pangea 0.35–0.15 Ga, a ~500 Myr cycle. Ophiolites peak at about 800–750, 500–440, 160–150 and 100–90 Ma, all tied to ocean closures. The **1.7–0.8 Ga "Boring Billion"** generated almost 20% of preserved crust but less than 1% of orogenic gold production. It has no BIF and little VMS, but is rich in IOCG, CD Pb-Zn, anorthosite-Ti and anorogenic Sn granites. Archean deposits hold about 25% of gold resources from under 6% of crust ([C&H13](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/4507/1/SP393.1.full.pdf)). The simulation reproduces such patterns only if every deposit rule queries a shared global-state object instead of hard-coding Earth dates.

The sedimentary note proposes `WorldHistory.globalState(t)`. It returns atmosphere state, ocean redox state, a seawater-chemistry toggle, biological flags and climate belts, randomized per world but pinned to Earth by default. The mod's life/atmosphere timeline should own these fields, and every deposit generator should read them. The table gives the defaults and what each gate switches.

The gates must be keyed to simulated planetary state, not absolute Earth ages, because the player chooses elapsed time. A world run for 600 Myr from proto-terrain that starts in a modern-like state will never pass through PRE_GOE. It will then have no Witwatersrand, BIF or komatiite Ni. Unless the starting basement carries inherited ages, it will also have no craton old enough for diamonds. This is decision D2 below.

## Erosion removes 62% of porphyries, so preservation is a depth test

Preservation decides what the player sees. For each deposit, store formation depth `z_form` below the paleosurface and vertical extent `h`. Integrate the cell's net exhumation `E` after formation. The deposit is **buried** if E < z_form, **exposed** (minable in the window) if z_form ≤ E < z_form + h, and **destroyed** if E ≥ z_form + h. Destroyed deposits release their resistant minerals (gold, cassiterite, diamond, zircon, ilmenite) into the sediment-routing system as placer supply. A useful survival heuristic is t ≈ (d + h)/e. Exhumation rates of 0.03–1.4 km/Myr give porphyry lifetimes from about 3 Myr in wet, fast-uplift arcs to over 100 Myr in arid or stable settings ([Yanites & Kesler 2015](https://geomorphology.earth.indiana.edu/news-events/_news/2015-yanites-kesler.html)). The 2025 global landscape-evolution study puts porphyry origin at 2–5 km. It finds exhumation of 0.1–1.0 km/Myr in active belts, at most 0.25 km/Myr in hyper-arid settings such as the Central Andes, and over 10 km/Myr locally in Papua New Guinea ([Boggiani et al. 2025](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/13/683/2025/)). Climate therefore controls exposure: wet regions show young, sparse porphyries, dry regions older and more abundant ones ([Yanites & Kesler 2015](https://geomorphology.earth.indiana.edu/news-events/_news/2015-yanites-kesler.html)). Reconstruction choice alone changes predicted porphyry probability by up to 90% ([GSA 2025](https://gsameetings.secure-platform.com/connects25/solicitations/103002/sessiongallery/schedule/items/95303/application/5908)). That sensitivity argues for calibrating against statistics, not single deposits.

The scale arithmetic below is this report's own derivation from the mod's stated defaults, not a sourced result. It determines how much crust the player can see. The research notes assumed incompatible scales, from 1 m per block in the hydrothermal note to 1 km per block on 16 km cells in the coal note. With a vertical exaggeration of 30, metres per vertical block equal metres per horizontal block divided by 30. At 1 km per horizontal block, one vertical block is about 33 m. The vanilla range y −64..320 then spans about 12.8 km. A 4 km ocean floor sits at y ≈ −57, and an 8.8 km summit at y ≈ 327. The default exaggeration therefore fits Earth's relief almost exactly at about 1 km per block. Beneath a lowland surface at y ≈ 70, the player can dig through about 134 blocks, or about 4.5 km of crust. That window holds a whole porphyry ore shell, epithermal zones in the top ~30 blocks, and the top of the mesozonal orogenic-gold range only where exhumation brought it up. The table shows how the window shrinks with finer horizontal scales.

| 100 m/block | 3.3 m | 1.3 km | 0.45 km | Only if exhumed to within ~0.4 km |

The same arithmetic shows the opposite problem in plan view and for thin beds. At 1 km per block, a porphyry ore shell about 1 km across is a single block, and a Lac de Gras kimberlite pipe (50–150 m) is a tenth of a block ([Kjarsgaard 2007](https://www.stardiamondcorp.com/_resources/pdfs/Kjarsgaard-Kimberlite-Diamonds-Synthesis.pdf)). The Merensky Reef (30–90 cm) and most coal seams (0.3–3 m) are a hundredth of a vertical block ([Bushveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushveld_Igneous_Complex); [Pashin 2004](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2004/annual/abstracts/Pashin02.htm)). Only provinces fit naturally: Bushveld's 66,000 km² becomes about 257 × 257 blocks, Ghawar about 280 × 30 blocks ([GEO ExPro](https://geoexpro.com/the-king-of-giant-fields/)). The architecture must therefore use **two scales**. The simulation decides, at planetary scale, where a deposit is, what type and grade it has, and which depth slice is exposed. A separate render scale stamps the orebody into blocks at gameplay size, comparable to TFC's vein sizes of 8–60 blocks ([TFC vein JSON](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/tree/1.21.x/src/main/resources/data/tfc/worldgen/configured_feature/vein)), and with a minimum thickness for reefs and seams. Relative spacing should survive the scaling: clusters of 5–30 km inside belts hundreds of km long inside provinces ([S10](https://pyrite.test.utah.edu/fieldtrips/SEGFperu-2012/Readings/Sillitoe%202010.pdf)).

The shared machinery has seven stages. The first six run at world creation alongside the plate simulation. The seventh runs lazily at chunk generation.

```text
1. Event log. Per timestep and segment: subduction (rate, obliquity, slab age, overriding-plate stress),
   collision, rift, ridge, plume/LIP, basin subsidence, uplift, erosion. Already produced by the plate sim.
2. Per-cell column. Layers with lithology, age, thickness, Tmax, TTI, redox flags
   (hasRedBeds, hasEvaporite, hasBlackShale, hasCarbonatePlatform, unconformity), burial/exhumation path.
3. Scorer. For each family, prospectivity = fuzzy AND of source x trigger x pathway x trap,
   multiplied by WorldHistory.globalState(t) gates. Evaluate only on cells/steps where the family's
   trigger fired.
4. Instantiation. Convert prospectivity to a count with a density model (e.g. porphyry median 15 per
   100,000 km2 exposed, so formation rate ~2.6x higher because only 38% survive), then cluster
   hierarchically: province -> belt (100s of km) -> cluster (5-30 km) -> deposit. Draw size and grade
   from a log-normal per type (top 10% holds 70-86% of metal).
5. Depth-zoned template. Each deposit gets a vertical model (e.g. porphyry: sinter 0-0.1 km, lithocap
   0-1, phyllic 1-1.5, ore 1.5-4, pluton 5-15 km) anchored to the paleosurface at formation.
6. Preservation pass. Apply the buried/exposed/destroyed rule with cumulative exhumation; destroyed
   bodies feed placer supply; add an "assembly bonus" for deposits encased by later collision.
   Supergene, laterite and gossan overprints are applied here while the body is near the surface.
7. Lazy placement. Store survivors in a spatial index persisted with the world. At chunk generation,
   query deposits intersecting the chunk, resolve host rock from the column, map (mineral, rock, grade)
   to a block id through the palette, place blocks and TFC-style surface indicators.
```

## Deposit catalog: about 70 types with rules and TFC blocks

Each table lists setting and trigger, formation conditions, age window, size and geometry, and the TFC block mapping, with vanilla fallbacks in parentheses. Block IDs follow TFC conventions: graded ores are `tfc:ore/<poor|normal|rich>_<ore>/<rock>`, ungraded minerals and gems `tfc:ore/<ore>/<rock>`, placer gravels `tfc:deposit/<ore>/<rock>`, and coal and halite the simple blocks `tfc:lignite`, `tfc:bituminous_coal` and `tfc:halite` ([TFCBlocks.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/blocks/TFCBlocks.java)). "None" means no base-TFC block exists. "Stand-in" means a TFC mineral substitutes for a different real mineral, which is a lossy mapping.

### Surface, weathering and placer deposits (G7)

These deposits share one **weathering integrator**. For each exposed cell and step, add regolith `dR = k(MAT, MAP)·dt − erosion·dt`, with k = 4–40 m/Myr scaled up for hot, wet climates ([CR Geoscience](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/item/10.1016/j.crte.2003.10.008.pdf)). Track a maturity integral for time spent under humid tropical conditions at low slope. Glaciation strips the profile and burial freezes it. Placers share a **sediment-routing integrator**: heavy-mineral supply equals the sum of upstream erosion times source composition, decayed by exp(−d/L). Typical L is 3–8 km for tin, 10–50 km for gold and 500–2,000 km for diamond. Supply is boosted at slope breaks and deposited as a basal pay layer 0.3–2 m thick under barren gravel. Sea-level rise drowns the system (design after [USGS OF 91-306](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1991/0306/report.pdf) and [Otago](https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Gold-in-the-hills-patterns-of/9926515118201891)).

## Coal needs land plants, ever-wet climate and balanced subsidence

The oldest coals are Pragian–Emsian Devonian, and widespread coals start in the Carboniferous ([Bushnev 2024](https://pureportal.spbu.ru/files/116922033/Bushnev_et_al._2024.pdf)). After the Permian–Triassic extinction, coal is missing worldwide for about 10 Myr and returns around 243 Ma ([Retallack 1996](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/420910); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10158563)). Nelsen et al. reject the hypothesis that Carboniferous coal reflects a lag before fungi could decay lignin. That supports climate-plus-tectonics rules ([Nelsen 2016](https://repository.si.edu/items/52e46b6c-12ec-44d5-8ce6-160781d13956)). Peat needs ever-wet climate without a long dry season ([Cecil 1985](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70012717)). It needs low clastic input and accommodation roughly matching peat growth, which is 0.05–4.5 mm/yr ([Exeter](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/140884)). Pennsylvanian cyclothems last at most ~0.4 Myr, parts are 100 kyr cycles, and seams reach 3 m ([Pashin 2004](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2004/annual/abstracts/Pashin02.htm); [AAPG 2016](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2016/90258es/abstracts/9.20.html)). Intermontane Powder River seams exceed 10 m ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/depositional-models-two-tertiary-coal-bearing-sequences-powder-river-basin-wyoming-usa)). Compaction is 2–4:1 from peat to lignite and ~10–20:1 to bituminous coal ([PGI](https://geojournals.pgi.gov.pl/agp/article/view/24833); [Illinois](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/in-support-of-early-peat-to-coal-compaction-evidence-from-pennsyl/); [GSA 2001](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2001AM/webprogram/Paper26215.html)). A 1 Myr step cannot resolve individual seams. Each step should therefore emit a stochastic seam package: number of seams ≈ step length ÷ cycle length (0.1 or 0.4 Myr) × the fraction of cycles with peat, each 0.3–3 m thick, with rare 10–30 m seams in fast-subsiding intermontane or foreland basins (design).

Rank follows maximum burial temperature, which the per-cell thermal history already tracks. Time matters little, because vitrinite equilibrates at peak temperature ([Barker](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70011965)); kinetic models such as EASY%Ro add time ([Burnham](https://www.aapg.org/news-and-media/details/explorer/articleid/60439/modernizing-vitrinite-reflectance-models-for-paleothermal-history-calibration)).

## Petroleum needs a 60–150 °C kitchen, a seal and a trap that came first

A petroleum system needs, in order, an organic-rich source rock, burial into the oil window, expulsion and migration, a reservoir under a seal, and a trap that formed before the critical moment of migration ([AAPG Wiki](https://wiki.aapg.org/Petroleum_systems)). Source quality uses TOC categories ([IGI](https://igiltd.com/assets/42.-Green_Total-Organic-Carbon.pdf)) and hydrogen index. HI below 200 is gas-prone, 200–300 mixed and above 300 oil-prone ([GEUS](https://geusbulletin.org/index.php/geusb/article/download/4989/10659/31750)). The oil window is about 50–150 °C and the gas window 150–200 °C ([Kerogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerogen)). Ro schemes and Type III windows are documented by [Sodir](https://factpages.sodir.no/pbl/geochemical_pdfs/128_2.pdf), [BGS](https://earthwise.bgs.ac.uk/index.php/OR/14/055_Technical_information) and [JPG](https://jpg.co.uk/ab_oct_02_2.htm). The Lopatin time-temperature index doubles every 10 °C, with factor 1 at 100–110 °C. Its thresholds are 15 for oil onset, 75 for peak, 160 for the end of oil, about 1,500 for the wet-gas limit and above 65,000 for dry gas ([Nankai TTI table](https://geochemicaltransactions.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12932-014-0015-8/tables/3); [IPA](https://www.ipa.or.id/en/publications/a-hydrocarbon-generation-analysis-in-northwest-java-basin-using-lopatin-s-method)). Critics of multi-energy kinetics report that single-energy methods reproduce EASY%Ro results, which justifies the cheap method ([GEO ExPro](https://geoexpro.com/a-cool-vitrinite-reflectance-model/)). Sandstone porosity falls from 40% to about 28% by 1.5 km, and quartz cement takes over below about 2 km ([S&D 1991](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/1991/annual/abstracts/0678b.htm); [NTNU](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2440553?show=full)). About 95% of giant-field reserves sit in structure-related traps, three-quarters of them anticlines. On the Gulf of Mexico shelf, 93% of traps are structural and 14% are flank traps on salt or shale diapirs ([SEAPEX](https://archives.datapages.com/data/southeast-asia-petroleum-exploration-society/data/001/001001/44_seapex0010044.htm); [OGJ](https://ogj.com/general-interest/companies/article/17228764/trap-types-can-be-instructive-in-gulf-of-mexico-shelf-search)). Oil is biodegraded below about 80 °C ([Head 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v426y2003i6964d10.1038_nature02134.html)). Reservoirs once heated to 80–90 °C are paleopasteurized and resist later degradation ([Nature 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v411y2001i6841d10.1038_35082535.html)). Shallow biodegraded accumulations become oil sands ([Athabasca](https://archives.datapages.com/data/bulletns/2012/07jul/BLTN11064/BLTN11064.HTM)). Immature rich source rock yielding at least 4 gal/ton is oil shale ([USGS OF 87-628](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1987/0628/report.pdf)).

The algorithm runs on the existing per-cell burial histories. The single priority-flood per horizon is the same depression-filling algorithm the erosion code likely already uses, run on the inverted top-reservoir surface. Parameters marked † are unverified.

```text
per timestep dt (1 Myr), per cell, per organic layer:
  T = surfaceT + geotherm * burialDepth
  Tmax = max(Tmax, T)                                   # coal rank, Ro
  TTI += dt_Myr * 2^((T - 105) / 10)                    # Lopatin/Waples
  Ro  ~= 0.65 * (TTI / 15)^0.29                         # author's fit, valid TTI 3..500 †
  if source layer (TOC >= 1-2 %, HI by kerogen type):
     TR = sigmoid(TTI): ~0 at 15, ~0.5 at 75, ~1 at 160
     generated = TOC * HI * thickness * area * dTR
     expelled  = 0.5-0.8 * generated  -> charge in carrier bed †
     if TTI > 160: crack retained oil to gas
every 5-10 Myr and at the final state, per carrier/reservoir horizon:
  invert top-reservoir depth map; priority-flood -> closed traps, spill points, spill tree
  route charge updip; lose 1-10 % per cell traversed †; tune generation-accumulation efficiency to 1-20 % †
  fill trap to min(spill point, seal capacity); overflow to next trap (fill-spill)
  gas fills crest first and displaces oil out of the spill point (Gussow differential entrapment)
  seal capacity by column height: shale 50-300 m, evaporite 300-1,000 m †; excess leaks upward
  trap accepts only charge arriving after its formation step (fold, diapir, pinch-out age)
post-processing per accumulation:
  reservoir T < 80 C and never > 80-90 C -> biodegrade to heavy oil
  reservoir T > ~160 C -> crack to gas †
  breached by erosion or faulting -> tar seep, asphalt, oil sand at outcrop
  leak exponentially, time constant 100-300 Myr †  (reproduces "70 % since Coniacian")
field sizes: lognormal or fractal (dimension 1.6-1.8); 20-200 traps per productive basin †
```

The fill-spill logic follows invasion-percolation practice ([USPTO 10570706](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10570706); [Permedia/MPATH](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/pdf/2002/hedberg_australia/images/ndx_boettcher.pdf)). Field sizes are lognormal ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70012762)) or fractal ([AAPG 1991](https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/1991/annual/abstracts/0668.htm)). Calibrate globally against the following. Klemme & Ulmishek's six source intervals hold Silurian 9%, Upper Devonian–Tournaisian 8%, Pennsylvanian–Lower Permian 8%, Upper Jurassic 25%, mid-Cretaceous 29% and Oligocene–Miocene 12.5% of reserves ([USGS Bull. 1931](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1931/report.pdf); [Klemme](https://searchanddiscovery.com/documents/Animator/klemme2.htm)). About a quarter of sedimentary basins are productive ([JPG](https://archives.datapages.com/data/jpg/V003/N002/187_207.HTM)). Ghawar's 400 m oil column under anhydrite is the real upper case ([CSEG](https://csegrecorder.com/articles/view/what-you-know-about-the-ghawar-oil-field-saudi-arabia)). At 33 m per vertical block it would be 12 blocks thick.

Petroleum is the largest content gap. TFC has no oil, gas, tar or oil-shale block or fluid ([Ore.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/blocks/rock/Ore.java)). The geometry recipe is clear: flat gas-oil and oil-water contacts in world Y inside reservoir layers, tar at breached outcrops, and oil shale where rich source rock never passed TTI 15. Rendering it needs the mod's own blocks and fluid, which is decision D7.

## TFC 4.2.12 accepts any generator that implements two interfaces

TFC for Minecraft 1.21.1 is the v4 line. It has been stable since 4.1.0 (2026-04-04), and 4.2.0 (2026-06-21) "reworked large-scale terrain generation to mimic plate tectonics" ([v4.2.0](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/releases/tag/v4.2.0)). Version 4.2.8 added a `tfc:worldgen/rock_settings` datapack registry ([v4.2.8](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/releases/tag/v4.2.8)). The build targets NeoForge 21.1.234 and Java 21, requires Patchouli, and is EUPL-1.2 ([build.gradle.kts](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/build.gradle.kts); [LICENSE](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/LICENSE.txt)). TFC is not on a public Maven repository. Addons use CurseMaven or the GitHub release download URLs ([KubeJS-TFC build](https://github.com/Notenoughmail/KubeJS-TFC/blob/1.21.1/build.gradle); [TFC-Real-World build](https://github.com/yazloysasha/TFC-Real-World/blob/1.21.x/build.gradle.kts)). The repository's AGENTS.md promises backwards compatibility for methods "that could reasonably be considered API" ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/AGENTS.md)). Interfaces such as `ChunkGeneratorExtension`, `ChunkDataGenerator`, `ClimateModel` and `SelectClimateModelEvent` are therefore safer coupling points than region tasks or height fillers.

TFC's own `TFCChunkGenerator` (codec `tfc:overworld`, `SEA_LEVEL_Y = 63`) builds terrain in three steps ([TFCChunkGenerator.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/TFCChunkGenerator.java)). A stateless `RegionGenerator` runs 18 hard-coded tasks on a 128-block grid within 12,288-block Voronoi cells, covering continents, boundaries, hotspots, mountains, climate, rocks, rivers and biomes ([RegionGenerator.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/region/RegionGenerator.java); [Units.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/region/Units.java)). Biomes are zoomed to quart resolution. Height is then blended from per-biome noises. TFC's "plates" have no history: each cell's velocity is a hash, and boundary type comes from relative velocity. Rivers grow inward from the coast as fractal trees rather than from simulated runoff ([AddRiversAndLakes.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/region/AddRiversAndLakes.java)). TFC's generator therefore cannot be fed a simulated history without replacing most region tasks.

Four TFC subsystems matter for integration. **ChunkData** is a NeoForge attachment `tfc:chunk_data`. It holds four corner climate values per chunk (sea-level temperature, rainfall, rain variance, base groundwater), a `ForestType`, and surface and aquifer heights. It moves through PARTIAL and FULL status ([ChunkData.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/chunkdata/ChunkData.java); [TFCAttachments.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/TFCAttachments.java)). **Rock is never saved.** `RockData.getRock` calls `ChunkDataGenerator.generateRock(x, y, z, surfaceY, cache)` whenever asked, during generation and at runtime (loose-rock regrowth, debug overlay), and structure placement calls `createAndGenerate` for arbitrary chunks ([RockData.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/chunkdata/RockData.java); [ClimateStructurePlacement.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/structure/ClimateStructurePlacement.java)). **Climate** is chosen through `SelectClimateModelEvent`. TFC assigns `OverworldClimateModel` to any overworld whose generator implements `ChunkGeneratorExtension`. That model reads averages from ChunkData. It adds z-based seasons, a lapse rate of 0.16225 °C per block above y = 63 (capped at 17.822 °C, reached near y ≈ 173), and warming toward 15 °C at y = −64 ([ClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/ClimateModel.java); [OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java); [Helpers.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/Helpers.java)). Latitude is a static triangle wave on z, `triangle(−π/2, 0, 1/(4s), z − s/2)`. The equator sits at z = s/2 and a pole at z = −s/2, repeating every 2s, with s = `temperature_scale`, default 20,000 ([SolarCalculator.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/client/overworld/SolarCalculator.java)). **Biomes** pair each vanilla biome JSON with a `BiomeExtension` in registry `tfc:biome_extension`. There are 125, and the public `BiomeBuilder` allows new ones ([TFCBiomes.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/biome/TFCBiomes.java); [BiomeBuilder.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/biome/BiomeBuilder.java)). The public `SurfaceManager` chooses among 8 soils from ChunkData climate, but it expects TFC-shaped helper arrays ([SurfaceManager.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/surface/SurfaceManager.java)). Three features, `ErosionFeature`, `SpringFeature` and `CaveSpikesFeature`, hard-cast the generator to `ChunkGeneratorExtension` and crash under any other generator ([ErosionFeature.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/feature/ErosionFeature.java)). Ore veins only replace blocks and need nothing but TFC raw-rock blocks to be present ([VeinConfig.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/feature/vein/VeinConfig.java)).

Three integration routes have working precedents.

| Route | Mechanism | Precedent | Fit for PlateTectonics |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Own generator implementing TFC interfaces | `ChunkGenerator implements ChunkGeneratorExtension` + `ChunkDataGenerator`; no TFC mixins; TFC's `ChunkMapMixin` calls `initRandomState` for any implementer | KubeJS-TFC `WrappedChunkGenerator` and `KubeChunkDataGenerator` (MIT) ([source](https://github.com/Notenoughmail/KubeJS-TFC/blob/1.21.1/src/main/java/io/github/notenoughmail/kubejstfc/worldgen/generator/WrappedChunkGenerator.java); [ChunkMapMixin](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/mixin/ChunkMapMixin.java)) | **Recommended.** The simulation owns heights, rocks and climate |
| (b) Feed TFC's pipeline through mixins | ~25 mixins into region tasks, height filler, biome noise, chunk-data generator and `SolarCalculator` | TFC-Real-World (MIT) ([README](https://github.com/yazloysasha/TFC-Real-World/blob/1.21.x/README.md)) | Most "native TFC" look, but fragile and bound to per-biome height noise |
| (c) Redirect parts of `TFCChunkGenerator` | `@Redirect` on biome-layer creation, swapped surface manager | TerraFirmaGreg Core-Modern, 1.20.1, LGPL ([mixin](https://github.com/TerraFirmaGreg-Team/Core-Modern/blob/dev/src/main/java/su/terrafirmagreg/core/mixins/common/tfc/new_ow_wg/TFCChunkGeneratorMixin.java)) | Not suited: TFC terrain logic remains |

The route (a) contract is concrete, and each item below has a defined TFC counterpart.

| Component | TFC contract | PlateTectonics supplies |
|---|---|---|
| `PlanetChunkGenerator extends ChunkGenerator implements ChunkGeneratorExtension` | `settings()` with a valid `rock_layer_settings` (required codec field; every placed rock block must appear in `rocks`), `rockLayerSettings()`, `applySettings`, `chunkDataGenerator()`, `getOrCreateAquifer`, `findSpawnBiome`, `initRandomState(ChunkMap, ServerLevel)`; mirror TFC's copy-on-reinit fix | Codec with simulation settings plus a TFC `Settings`; sea level 63; `minecraft:overworld` noise settings at y −64..320 |
| `PlanetChunkDataGenerator implements ChunkDataGenerator` | `generate()` to PARTIAL with corner climate and ForestType; FULL with `surfaceHeight[256]` and aquifer heights; `generateRock(x,y,z,surfaceY,cache)` fast, thread-safe, deterministic forever | Present-day climate from the climate sim; ForestType from vegetation density; rock from the persisted stratigraphic column ([ChunkDataGenerator.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/chunkdata/ChunkDataGenerator.java)) |
| Biome source | Biomes need `BiomeExtension`s; `BiomeSourceExtension` only if TFC spawn search or river lookup is wanted | Classify simulated terrain into TFC biome IDs (e.g. `collisional_mountains`, rift valley, shield volcano, ice sheet), else override `findSpawnBiome` as KubeJS-TFC does |
| Fluids | `ChunkBaseBlockSource` picks `salt_water` vs `water` from biome `isSalty()` | Salt water in seas, fresh water in lakes and rivers ([ChunkBaseBlockSource.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/ChunkBaseBlockSource.java)) |
| Climate | `OverworldClimateModel` auto-assigned; rainfall [0, 500], variance [−1, 1], groundwater [0, 500] | Remap simulated climate into these ranges; align latitude with TFC's z convention or subclass the model and mixin `SolarCalculator` |
| Base stone | TFC raw-rock blocks | So veins, collapses, loose rocks and prospecting work |
| Persistence | Rock and deposits recomputed on demand after reload | Save simulation and deposit index in the world folder, or regenerate deterministically before any chunk loads |

Performance matters because TFC's own fill calls `generateRock` once per solid block, mitigated by `ChunkRockDataCache` ([ChunkBaseBlockSource.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/ChunkBaseBlockSource.java)). Route (a) can fill whole columns directly from the simulation, but TFC features still query point-wise. On licensing, EUPL-1.2 leaves the scope of "Derivative Work" to national copyright law ([LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/LICENSE.txt)). MIT-licensed addons that implement TFC interfaces or mix into TFC are common practice: KubeJS-TFC, TFC-Real-World, TFCGenViewer and tfc-atlas. Copying TFC source would carry EUPL obligations. This is not legal advice.

Beyond veins, TFC places placer deposits in 7 river and lake biomes. Shallow discs swap gravel and deep discs swap raw rock at y 40–63. Panning a deposit drops a 5.33% host-rock gem: granite→topaz, diorite→emerald, gabbro→diamond, limestone/marble→lapis, chert→ruby, chalk→sapphire, quartzite→opal, claystone/dolomite→amethyst, shale→borax, conglomerate→lignite, gneiss→gypsum, and pyrite for the rest ([constants.py DEPOSIT_RARES](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/resources/constants.py); [assets.py](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/resources/assets.py)). Other placements are a basalt/quartzite amethyst geode in 1 of 50 chunks, sapphire and emerald around hot springs, topaz and diamond in lava fissures, sulfur rivulets and crater-lake sulfur on stratovolcanoes, peat discs, and salt licks ([world_gen.py](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/resources/world_gen.py)). The prospector's pick scans a 25-block cube (radius 12) for `#tfc:prospectable` = `#c:ores`. Its false-negative chance is 0.3 − 0.06 × tier, and it reports size classes from TRACES (<10) to VERY_LARGE (≥80). It fires `ProspectedEvent` and accepts `registerRepresentative` for addon ores ([PropickItem.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/common/items/PropickItem.java); [prospectable.json](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.21.x/src/generated/resources/data/tfc/tags/block/prospectable.json)).

Several TFC placements contradict geology, and the simulation should correct them while keeping the block IDs. **Cinnabar** sits deep in metamorphic rock, but real cinnabar forms within a few hundred metres of the surface in hot springs and epithermal systems. **Garnierite** sits at y −80..0, but it forms in near-surface laterites over ultramafic rock. Lapis is placed in limestone instead of high-grade marble, diamond pipes in gabbro instead of kimberlite on old cratons, emerald pipes in any intrusive, and cryolite as a common deep-granite ore although it is unique on Earth ([USGS 27a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull4von.htm); [SIR 2010-5070-H](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5070/h/); [Ivigtut](https://tidsskrift.dk/meddrgroenland_geosci/article/view/140693)). Moving cinnabar to its true shallow, young setting creates the main gameplay risk. On a world whose arcs are old and eroded, shallow Hg deposits are gone. The redstone path therefore needs Almadén-type stratabound Hg in quartzite, which survives on old continents, plus accessory cinnabar in Carlin, epithermal and epizonal orogenic systems, plus one guaranteed cryolite deposit ([USGS 27b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull56g7.htm)).

The recommended ore path has two phases. In phase 1, PlateTectonics places only TFC raw rocks and lets TFC's default veins run. Biomes carry `#tfc:in_biome/veins`, and veins follow host rock automatically, so native copper already appears only in extrusive rocks and malachite only in carbonates. That gives TFC compatibility by default with no deposit code. In phase 2, the mod overrides the `in_biome/veins*.json` tags with `"replace": true`, keeping gravel and geode if desired, and adds one Java `DepositFeature`. That feature reads the persisted deposit index for the chunk, resolves host rock from the column, maps (mineral, rock, grade) through a palette, and places TFC-style indicators: surface nuggets within ~14 blocks when the body top is within 35 blocks of the surface, plus gossans and lithocaps as visual cues. Datapack JSON veins cannot express per-region geology, because configured features are static per datapack and vary only by biome tag. Contact-dependent deposits such as skarn, greisen and contact lapis are impossible with TFC's exact-block replacement.

## Conclusion

The geology supports a compact design. Eight generator families, one global-state object and one depth-versus-exhumation test explain where every deposit class forms and why most of them are absent at any given surface. The preservation test produces the young-porphyry and old-gold age patterns without further rules. The two most consequential findings are about scale and supply, not genesis. At the default 30× exaggeration, the 384-block window holds about 4.5 km of crust under lowlands, enough for whole porphyry systems. In plan view, however, nearly every individual orebody is smaller than a block, so a separate render scale is mandatory. The commodities TFC needs most for progression, tin and redstone, are also the most geologically concentrated and the first to be eroded away. A pure simulation will therefore produce worlds that cannot reach bronze or redstone unless a guarantee pass exists.

On the TFC side, the integration risk is lower than expected. Implementing `ChunkGeneratorExtension` and `ChunkDataGenerator` gives seasons, crops, soils, prospecting and ore blocks with no mixins. TFC's own veins even provide a usable first release because they key off host rock. The real costs are elsewhere: persisting the simulation so `generateRock` stays stable after reload; reimplementing river carving and volcanoes; collapsing lithologies onto 21 rocks; and deciding which missing commodities, petroleum above all, justify the mod's own blocks.
