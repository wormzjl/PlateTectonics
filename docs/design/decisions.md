| D-6 | **t = 0 is planet formation**, and every geologic gate keys off *simulated planetary state* (the per-step `GlobalState`), never absolute Earth dates | Matches the "proto-terrain, then elapsed time" flow | Report D2 |
| D-7 | **Latitude is linear in z, aligned to TFC:** north pole z = −s/2, equator z = s/2, south pole z = 3s/2; `temperature_scale` s = pole-to-equator distance (≈333,333 blocks at S = 30) | Avoids a client-side `SolarCalculator` mixin | Report D9 |

| D-11 | **Progression floor:** within a configurable radius of spawn, guarantee copper, tin, zinc + bismuth, gold + silver, nickel, iron, flux carbonate, coal and redstone, placed geologically where possible | Real densities are sparse at 30 m/block | Report D5 |

| D-21 | **Plates move over one world raster by whole-cell shifts** (translation only in v1); per-plate rasters and rotation are deferred | Exact copies, no resampling blur, simple and fast; maps show no need for rotation yet | [03](03-tectonics.md#as-built-phase-2-vs-planned) |
| D-22 | **Subduction polarity is remembered per plate pair; crust ≥ 15 km (arcs, oceanic plateaus) cannot subduct and is accreted** | Without both, ~95% of arc crust went back down flipping trenches and continents never grew | [03](03-tectonics.md#boundary-processes) |
| D-23 | **Plateau support model** (owner request: "why Tibet didn't sink"): buoyant crust + keel and eclogite roots + random delamination with rebound + underthrust split 35/55/10 + ceiling 5.3 km above sea level with lateral flow + collapse of hot crust when convergence stops. A plateau can stand 0.5–2 km low while a dense root is attached, but never sinks for good | Matches both halves of Tibet at ~5 km and the Andean "stuck low, then pop up" cases | [03](03-tectonics.md#plateaus-and-roots); [research notes](../research_notes/Tibetan%20plateau%20support/tibetan_plateau_support.md) |
| D-24 | **Arc output follows an Earth-like crustal growth curve** (setting; 0 = off) | Plate histories are chaotic; seeds otherwise differ several-fold in land area | [03](03-tectonics.md#crust-growth) |
| D-25 | **Every tectonic rule is stated in km and Myr, never in cells** (merge distance, shelf width, arc and underthrust bands, slopes) | Cell-count rules made 15.6 km runs merge plates 5× as often as 78 km runs | Phase 2 benchmark |
| D-26 | **Sea level is recomputed every step** from the water volume | The plateau ceiling and the elevation cap are measured from sea level | [03](03-tectonics.md#isostasy-and-elevation) |
| D-27 | **Oxygen is built with life** (owner request: "CO2 and O2 models need to be built with life"): a balance of organic-carbon burial (productivity that steps up with each innovation × phosphorus from continental weathering × burial efficiency from erosion, smoothed over 10 Myr) against reduced volcanic gases (fading as the mantle cools) and oxidative weathering. The GOE and NOE are outcomes, not scheduled milestones. Hysteresis: after the GOE only 60% of the reduced gases still consume O₂ (ozone, methane collapse); leaving `POST_NOE` needs < 0.15 PAL. Under a snowball oxygen and the phosphorus supply hold; a meltwater nutrient pulse follows the thaw. Milestones that need oxygen wait for it | Oxygen history then depends on each world's life, continents and erosion, and the Earth-like preset lands near Earth's GOE and NOE dates without forcing them | [07](07-life-atmosphere-sun.md#oxygen); Goldblatt et al. 2006 |
| D-28 | **Carbon thermostat with CO₂-dependent weathering**: solve for the temperature at which silicate weathering (land + sea floor) plus net organic burial removes the volcanic CO₂. Weathering ∝ pCO₂^0.3 on land and ^0.23 on the sea floor. Early-life methane adds up to +12 °C and collapses at the GOE faster than CO₂ responds, which can trigger a snowball. Life on land multiplies weathering (bare 0.25, mats 0.35, plants 0.8, forests 1.0); land weathering strength comes from the climate field each step | With the CO₂ term a faint young Sun leaves the planet cooler (it needs more CO₂, which weathers rock faster); the methane collapse gives a Huronian-style snowball at the GOE | [07](07-life-atmosphere-sun.md#carbon-thermostat); Krissansen-Totton et al. 2018 |
| D-29 | **Milestone waits are log-logistic** (σ = 0.6 in log space) around medians shorter than Earth's waits; the Earth-like preset uses Earth's own waits | With exponential waits and Earth as the median, under half of random worlds grew forests (coal) by 4.57 Gyr; now about three quarters do (test: 60–90 of 100 seeds). In-game vegetation is present anyway (A-13); the timeline only drives geology | [07](07-life-atmosphere-sun.md#life-milestones-stochastic) |
| D-30 | **Erosion scheme:** D8 Priority-Flood + FIFO routing with lakes and endorheic basins; implicit stream power (m = 0.5, n = 1) **plus a sub-grid relief term** k h² × wetness; the surface drops only by the isostatic share of the rock removed (15% crystalline, 27% sediment on land); transport-limited deposition in closed form, never above the lowest donor; lakes trap sediment; at sea: shelf to 120 m within 300 km, turbidite aprons to 900 km, delta top, then delta plain (mass conserved; refined by D-39 and D-40) | Implicit solvers are stable at 2–10 Myr steps. Coarse cells cannot resolve the steep slopes that do most mountain erosion, so the relief term stands in for them. Removed rock rebounds, so the surface drops less than the rock removed | [04](04-surface-processes.md#rivers-erosion-transport-and-deposition); Barnes et al. 2014; Braun & Willett 2013; Ahnert 1970; Montgomery & Brandon 2002 |
| D-31 | **Sediment deposited on ocean floor stays oceanic** (a passive-margin wedge on transitional crust) and is recycled at trenches: 50% is scraped into the overriding plate (up to sea level, D-38), the rest subducts | Converting thick piles to continental crust made unsinkable crust from sediment alone and inflated continental area to ~58% | [04](04-surface-processes.md#sea-floor) |
| D-32 | **Subduction erosion** removes 35 km³ of the overriding plate's crust per km of trench per Myr, at its trench cell; stated per km of trench so the volume does not depend on cell size | Earth: ~1.4 km³/yr over ~40,000 km of trench. With real, mass-conserving erosion the Phase 2 stand-in's hidden 25% loss is gone; this sink lets the arc feedback hold crust volume near the growth curve (D-24) | [03](03-tectonics.md#crust-growth); von Huene & Scholl 1991; Scholl & von Huene 2007 |
| D-33 | **Moisture is swept both ways along each row:** with the prevailing wind, and against it at 0.6 strength with a shorter decay length; each cell keeps the wetter value | With the prevailing wind alone, coasts at the downwind end of a continent get only rained-out air; on Earth, monsoons and cyclones keep many of them wet | [04](04-surface-processes.md#climate) |
| D-34 | **Phase 4 terrain stand-in:** block elevation = Catmull-Rom bicubic of the master cells, **clamped to the four surrounding cells**, plus fractal detail scaled by local relief (calmer near coasts). Climate is bilinear; temperature follows the detail with the lapse rate. The column is filled directly in `fillFromNoise` (no noise router); there are no caves until Phase 5 | Big shapes match the map preview exactly. Unclamped splines overshoot at steps: shelf breaks rose above the sea as chains of islands, and lone peaks sat in moats | [08](08-minecraft-integration.md#as-built-phase-4-first-slice) |
| D-35 | **TFC climate from the simulation:** ChunkData rainfall = annual precipitation × 0.2 (0–500); temperature reduced to sea level (TFC applies its own lapse rate); rain variance 0 (no seasonal rain in the model yet); groundwater = rainfall; forest type from the climate class; TFC biomes chosen by landform only | TFC's climate model reads only ChunkData. Its biomes describe landforms, so climate must not drive them twice | [08](08-minecraft-integration.md#as-built-phase-4-first-slice) |
| D-36 | **Basin closure:** ocean floor that one plate wraps round (touching its continent and no other plate) closes from its rim inward at 5 km/Myr; closed cells become continental crust as thick as their continental neighbours (at most 35 km) and keep their sediment. The closing front is fractional (a random draw per cell for the part-cell); the crust added is reported as its own budget line | Such slivers (rift and back-arc remnants of the plate engine) have no trench and lived forever: 3–8% of the surface, filled by sediment into land on oceanic crust. Rift oceans touch their own ridge, so they are never closed. Now 0–1.4% | [03](03-tectonics.md#boundary-processes) |
| D-37 | **Climate refinements:** temperature ∝ cos(latitude)^1.1 between −25 and 27 °C (Earth-like mean); plants recycle rain (forest 0.6, open 0.25) from the previous step's aridity; poleward monsoon (1.0) and storm-track (0.7) sweeps; ITCZ moisture lasts 7× longer near the equator; warm forests rain up to 1.4×; rain spread by a 250 km Gaussian; air dries 0.25 e-folds per km lifted above the highest ground it has crossed (forgotten over 1,000 km); the cold-air cap limits local rain only; land weathering normalized at 31.25 km (0.6), with the thermostat's life-on-land factor reduced where the climate field already holds the vegetation feedback. Constants in `ClimateConfig` (`--set`) | Biomes came out far from Earth's (deserts ~40%, few forests). Interiors dried with every climb over a bump, so finer cells dried them more; the crest rule makes mean land rain nearly cell-size independent (test). Across seeds: forest 36–44%, grassland 29–34%, desert 6–11% of land (Earth ~31, ~27, ~20) | [04](04-surface-processes.md#climate) |
| D-38 | **Sediment is part of the crust column and never exceeds it:** crustal flow moves sediment with the crust it moves (the giver thins as a whole column); the plateau ceiling and the fill of receiving cells allow for sediment being lighter; the accretionary wedge builds only up to sea level, the rest subducts; arc growth never thins crust that is above the cap | Flow moved crust but left the sediment, so columns had more sediment than crust; isostasy read that as negative crystalline crust and raised lone pillars to the 6.5 km cap, which then hid piles of up to 1,200 km of sediment from erosion. Tests now check the invariant | [03](03-tectonics.md#isostasy-and-elevation) |
| D-39 | **Sea = water bodies of at least 100,000 km²**, joined edge to edge; smaller hollows below sea level are lakes in the Priority-Flood (the largest body stays sea if none is that big) | One-cell "seas" inland swallowed whole rivers' loads: 56,000 mouth dumps per run (4.4 × 10⁹ km³) built pillars. As lakes they fill to their spill level and pass the rest on | [04](04-surface-processes.md#rivers-erosion-transport-and-deposition) |
| D-40 | **Marine spreading by straight-line distance:** sea cells are taken from the mouth in rings one cell wide (bucket queue); aprons take equal shares but never build above shelf depth; the delta top progrades past the search radius as far as it needs; a surplus spreads over the whole filled sea, never onto the mouth alone | Breadth-first order drew diamond fronts (45° coasts); unlimited apron shares and mouth dumps piled 20% of marine sediment into single cells. Cost: round fans search ~1.4× the cells of the old diamonds and each visit costs more (breadth-first: 1.35 s per 78 km world; rings: 3.7 s). The walk order depends only on the sea, so worker threads now walk it ahead and the spreading replays the walks in cell order, with byte-identical results on any thread count: 2.1 s per 78 km world, 46 s per 31.25 km world (it was 69 s) | [04](04-surface-processes.md#sea-floor) |
